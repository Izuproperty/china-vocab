STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Hitachi Raises Its Forecast for the Second Time as Energy Orders Jump 47% on HVDC and Data Centers

日立製作所 (Hitachi Seisakusho, Hitachi) raised its full-year forecast for the second time this year on October 28 after record results for the July-September quarter. Revenue rose 15% to ¥2.904 trillion and adjusted EBITA rose ¥51.8 billion to ¥376.1 billion, a 12.9% margin. Orders rose 32% to ¥3.79 trillion. The company now expects ¥12.0 trillion of revenue, ¥1.56 trillion of adjusted EBITA and ¥920 billion of net income, up ¥300 billion, ¥40 billion and ¥20 billion from its previous 業績予想 (gyōseki yosō, earnings forecast). It also raised the interim dividend to ¥32 from a planned ¥28.


The energy business carried the quarter. Its revenue rose 28% to ¥960.4 billion and its orders rose 47% to ¥1.566 trillion, driven by European 高圧直流送電 (kōatsu chokuryū sōden, HVDC transmission) awards and data center demand. Its margin widened 260 basis points to 15.2%, and the 受注残高 (juchū zandaka, order backlog) reached ¥10.5 trillion. Management said it now expects grid growth to last through 2034 instead of 2030, and that its data center projects show no delays or payment problems, with revenue from the new orders arriving next fiscal year. Digital Systems & Services grew revenue 10% to ¥814.1 billion, but its margin slipped 40 basis points to 15.5%.


Hitachi earns from AI through power equipment, grid links and software that runs industrial sites, and none of that depends on a single model lab raising money. Lumada, its data and AI business, made ¥2.428 trillion in the first half, up 31% and 44% of group revenue. The company raised its full-year forecast for HMAX, the AI suite it launched for factories, railways and data centers, to ¥510 billion, about 70% growth. Executive Hatakeyama told analysts most of that will be new business, answering the suspicion that HMAX relabels existing Lumada sales. The shares were little changed, because the margin gained only 10 basis points on 15% revenue growth.

Investor takeaway: Hitachi's AI exposure runs through transformers, HVDC links and data center operations, so its orders hold up when investors doubt how AI labs fund their compute. A flat share price after a second raise reflects margins, not demand. Watch whether the Digital Systems margin recovers in the second half and whether the data center orders turn into revenue in fiscal 2027 as management says.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Advantest Raises Its Profit Guide to ¥967 Billion on a 55.9% Quarterly Margin and Starts a ¥280 Billion Buyback Today

アドバンテスト (Adobantesuto, Advantest) reported July-September revenue of ¥477.7 billion on October 28, up 81.7% from a year earlier and 30% from the previous quarter. Operating profit rose 146% to ¥266.8 billion, for an 営業利益率 (eigyō riekiritsu, operating margin) of 55.9%, and gross margin reached 71.1%. Both figures beat the company's own forecasts of ¥434.5 billion and ¥224.5 billion. It raised its full-year guide to ¥1.863 trillion of revenue, ¥967 billion of operating profit and ¥741.5 billion of net income, from ¥1.714 trillion, ¥846 billion and ¥660 billion. It will buy back up to 11 million shares, about 1.5% of the total, for up to ¥280 billion through March 24, 2027, in a 自社株買い (jishakabu-gai, share buyback) that starts today, and raised the interim dividend to ¥49 from ¥29.


Yesterday's brief named memory test demand as the number most likely to disappoint, after Disco guided to an HBM pause. It barely matters to the total. Memory testers brought in ¥53.4 billion in the quarter and are forecast at ¥229 billion for the year, about 12% of sales. SoC testers for GPUs, custom ASICs and CPUs did the work: their quarterly sales rose to ¥353.3 billion from ¥259.6 billion, and the full-year SoC guide went up to ¥1.375 trillion from ¥1.2435 trillion. Sales to the Americas rose to ¥261.3 billion from ¥178.6 billion. Group CEO Douglas Lefever called the market a mega cycle, and the company said it expects 2027 to stay 供給制約 (kyōkyū seiyaku, supply-constrained). Annual SoC tester capacity has passed 5,000 systems and Advantest has moved up its target of 10,000.


The shares rose 3.2% on October 28 before the release, to about ¥34,900, after a 9% jump the day before. The company kept its calendar-2026 tester market estimate at a record $13.0-14.5 billion and flagged higher memory component prices as a margin risk. Tokyo Electron and Kioxia report on Friday, the same day the BOJ decides.

Investor takeaway: The raise answers the funding scare of mid-October with orders. At a 51.9% full-year margin and with a buyback absorbing shares from today, the stock has support on any AI pullback. The risks are the cost of doubling capacity and memory component prices. Friday's Tokyo Electron guidance will show whether front-end equipment demand matches what Advantest sees in test.

STORY 3 · MACRO
The Fed Holds With Two Votes for a Hike, the Dollar Rises to ¥158.3, and the BOJ Opens Its Meeting at 1.25%

The US Federal Reserve held its policy range at 3.75-4% on October 28 in Washington, early on October 29 in Tokyo, on a 10-2 vote. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack voted for a quarter-point hike. The statement upgraded growth to a strong pace, and Chair Kevin Warsh said inflation remains elevated. Futures priced about an 85% chance of a hike on December 9, and the US 10-year yield rose to 5.04%. The dollar climbed to about ¥158.3, near its 200-day moving average around ¥158.5.


The 日本銀行 (Nihon Ginkō, Bank of Japan) starts its two-day 金融政策決定会合 (kin'yū seisaku kettei kaigō, monetary policy meeting) today with the policy rate at 1.25%. Economists expect a hold on Friday, and Reuters reported that the quarterly 展望レポート (tenbō repōto, Outlook Report) is likely to say underlying inflation has reached 2%. Markets price about 80% for a BOJ hike in December. The trouble for the yen is arithmetic. The 日米金利差 (Nichibei kinrisa, US-Japan rate gap) is at least 2.5 percentage points, and if both central banks raise rates in December it stays there. The 10-year spread is wider still, with JGB yields near 2.8% last week. BOJ hikes alone cannot close a gap that the Fed keeps widening.


That leaves the Ministry of Finance as the yen's backstop. Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama said on October 27 that the government stands ready to take decisive action in currency markets, and MUFG analysts see ¥160 as the level where warnings would turn into 為替介入 (kawase kainyū, currency intervention). Governor Kazuo Ueda's press conference follows the decision on Friday afternoon.

Investor takeaway: A hold is priced, so Ueda's wording on December decides the yen's direction. An explicit 2% verdict with guidance toward a December hike would lift two-year JGB yields and cool the dollar's move above ¥158. A hedged message, with the Fed leaning toward its own hike, would push USD/JPY toward ¥160 and bring intervention into play. Exporters reporting this week benefit from the weak yen until that line is tested.
