STORY 1 · AI & TECH
GMO's Security Unit Finds a Chinese AI Hacking Agent on Every Server It Checked From Japan's Breach Wave

GMO Internet Group's security unit, GMOサイバーセキュリティ byイエラエ (GMO Saibā Sekyuriti bai Ierae, GMO Cybersecurity by Ierae), said in an October 19 report that attackers used ARTEX, an AI agent built in China for 侵入テスト (shinnyū tesuto, penetration testing), against Japanese companies. The firm examined six overseas servers used in attacks on Japanese organizations between September 18 and October 18 and found traces of ARTEX on all six, with no sign of any other AI agent. One 攻撃基盤 (kōgeki kiban, attack infrastructure) targeted about 20 Japanese companies, nine of them in the same industry, and eight of those targets have disclosed a 情報漏えい (jōhō rōei, data leak) in the past two months. The servers were pointed at e-commerce, finance, retail, logistics, media, education and marketing companies. Daiki Fukumori, a former Interpol investigator who runs the unit's cybercrime center, told Bloomberg the group appears to run separate campaigns by country and by industry. The firm has passed its findings to law enforcement and has not named an actor.


ARTEX was released as open source by a Chinese engineer as a tool for companies to test their own networks. It links several large language models, including Claude, GPT and DeepSeek, so they work as one agent that maps a route into a network and runs standard security tools along it. CrowdStrike tied the same tool to attacks on South Korean banks in late September and early October, where regulators say about 100,000 records leaked from 16 institutions including Shinhan, KB Kookmin, Hana and Busan Bank. After the Korean reports the developer made the GitHub repository private. Threatbook, the Chinese security company where he worked, has since announced a commercial version.


Japan's breach wave began before anyone linked it to AI. Daiwa Securities, East Japan Railway and SoftBank Corp. are among the companies hit since September, and on October 9 the Cabinet Secretariat told holders of large customer databases to assume they will be attacked. An agent that finds a way in and runs the exploit without a skilled operator cuts the labor in each intrusion, so one group can work through a whole industry's list of targets in weeks. Mid-sized Japanese companies that counted on being too small to attract a skilled attacker lose that protection. The report came out the same day the Nikkei disclosed Tokyo's plan for a security certificate for data centers and clouds, and it gives the Economic Security Act amendment due in January a documented case to point to.

Investor takeaway: Japanese companies with large customer databases face higher security budgets and breach-disclosure costs, and domestic security vendors such as GMO Internet Group (TSE: 9449) and Trend Micro (TSE: 4704) gain demand from them. The nine same-industry targets have not been named, and their identity will decide which listed sector carries the cost. Watch for attribution from the National Police Agency or the National Cybersecurity Office, and for further leak disclosures from the targeted industry.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
METI Asks for ¥400 Billion More of Rapidus Equity Over Three Years So Banks Will Lend It ¥2 Trillion

経済産業省 (Keizai Sangyō-shō, METI) told a subcommittee of the 財政制度等審議会 (Zaisei Seido-tō Shingikai, Fiscal System Council), the Finance Minister's advisory panel, on October 19 that it will request ¥400 billion of new government 出資 (shusshi, equity investment) in ラピダス (Rapidasu, Rapidus) over three fiscal years. The plan is ¥150 billion in fiscal 2027, already in METI's budget request, then ¥150 billion in fiscal 2028 and ¥100 billion in fiscal 2029. The government put ¥100 billion into Rapidus in fiscal 2025 and ¥150 billion in fiscal 2026, so cumulative state equity would reach ¥650 billion. The money would come through 産業投資 (sangyō tōshi, industrial investment), a part of the fiscal investment and loan program funded largely by dividends on shares the government holds. Each year's amount still needs budget approval.


The equity is there to secure bank loans. Rapidus plans to borrow ¥2 trillion from private lenders to expand its line in Chitose, Hokkaido, starting with about ¥400 billion in fiscal 2027, and part of that lending carries government guarantees. Lenders require a minimum 自己資本比率 (jiko shihon hiritsu, equity ratio) before they commit, so each tranche of state equity sets how much the banks will put in. METI's materials lay out the schedule: 2nm volume production in fiscal 2027, advanced packaging in fiscal 2028, positive operating cash flow in fiscal 2029, and positive free cash flow with a stock listing around fiscal 2031. Council members asked for a yearly check on whether the project still meets a policy need and can repay the state, and the council said profitability should be assessed on an ongoing basis.


Rapidus still has no named volume customer. It signed 17 design houses on October 5 to bring clients to the fab, and on October 16 METI cleared TSMC to make 3nm chips in Kumamoto from 2028, which gives Japanese chip designers a domestic leading-edge option from an established foundry. Because every tranche now goes through a yearly budget vote, a slip in the fiscal 2027 production start or a thin customer list would show up first in the size of the fiscal 2028 request.

Investor takeaway: The ¥2 trillion loan book falls mainly on Japan's large banks, and MUFG (TSE: 8306), a founding Rapidus shareholder, would hold exposure through both equity and credit. Equipment makers such as Tokyo Electron (TSE: 8035) and Screen Holdings (TSE: 7735) need that borrowing to happen before Rapidus can order tools for capacity beyond its pilot line. Watch the December fiscal 2027 budget for the ¥150 billion line, the size of the first bank syndicate, and any customer announcement before volume production starts.

STORY 3 · MACRO
The 20-Year JGB Auction Draws a Record 5.84 Times Cover, and the Yield Falls to 3.71%

The 財務省 (Zaimushō, Ministry of Finance) sold 20-year 国債 (kokusai, JGBs) on October 20 and drew bids of 5.84 times the amount on offer, the highest 応札倍率 (ōsatsu bairitsu, bid-to-cover ratio) on record, according to Bloomberg. The September sale drew 4.01 times, and the 12-month average is 3.73. The auction's average yield was about 3.72%, down from 3.86% in September. After the result the 20-year yield fell as much as 6 basis points to 3.71%, its lowest since September 9, and the 30-year yield fell about 5 basis points to near 3.97%. The 10-year yield rose 1.5 basis points to 2.92%.


Earlier in October the 20-year yield reached its highest since the mid-1990s and the 30-year hit a record. A global bond selloff tied to Middle East tension and oil drove part of the rise, and investors also pushed yields up over how Prime Minister 高市早苗 (Takaichi Sanae, Sanae Takaichi) will pay for her cut in the consumption tax on food. Those yields brought buyers back. More than 83% of the bids came from unidentified bidders, which Bloomberg read as large domestic investors such as the megabanks and possibly more foreign money. 富国生命 (Fukoku Seimei, Fukoku Mutual Life) said yields had risen more than it expected, leaving room to move money out of foreign-currency bonds and into yen debt. A strategist at Sumitomo Mitsui Trust Asset Management tied the demand to risk aversion after mass protests in France and the selloff in CoreWeave's stock and bonds.


超長期債 (chō-chōki sai, super-long bonds) have been the weakest part of the JGB market since life insurers slowed their buying, and the Finance Ministry planned to cut super-long issuance in fiscal 2026 to about ¥17 trillion, a 17-year low, according to a Reuters report last December. A record cover at 3.7% shows that the buyer base returns once the price is high enough. Lower long yields also reduce the state's funding bill, since the ministry's fiscal 2027 request already assumes a 3.8% interest rate and a record ¥36.6 trillion in debt service. The auction came nine days before the BOJ's October 29-30 meeting, where Governor Ueda has said the board will decide whether underlying inflation has reached 2%. Firm demand at the long end lowers the chance that a rate hike sets off a disorderly rise in super-long yields.

Investor takeaway: Fukoku's comment points to Japanese life insurers shifting money home from foreign bonds, which supports the yen at the margin and removes a source of demand for US Treasuries. Banks that hold large JGB portfolios, such as MUFG (TSE: 8306), see unrealized losses shrink when long yields fall. Watch September national CPI on Friday and the next 30-year auction for whether demand holds above 3.9%.
