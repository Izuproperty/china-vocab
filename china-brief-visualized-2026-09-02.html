STORY 1 · AI & TECH
$17.02, Up 30% Since April: ByteDance Repriced Doubao's Virtual Shares to Keep Its AI Team

ByteDance raised the internal valuation of its Doubao virtual-share incentive scheme, reserved for Seed Lab and core LLM teams, by nearly 15% to $17.02/share — a cumulative 30% rise over four months. Employees can now convert more cash salary into shares, up to 30% for senior researchers.


Doubao shares don't trade publicly, so the repricing is a valuation ByteDance sets for itself, not one the market has tested — useful as a signal of internal urgency in the AI talent war, less useful for judging what the business is worth.

Key signal: Compensation cost is migrating off the cash-comp line into a security nobody outside the company can independently price.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH
¥954M In, ¥2.1B Out: Zhipu's Revenue Quintuples, R&D Still Costs More Than It Earns

Zhipu AI's H1 2026 revenue rose roughly 400% y/y to ¥953.9M, with net loss narrowing to ¥2.0B. But R&D spending, up 36.6% to ¥2.1B, alone exceeds total revenue. Rival MiniMax grew revenue 283% with a wider adjusted loss.


Zhipu's market cap topped HK$1T in June, then shed roughly half its value. J.P. Morgan doesn't model an adjusted profit until 2028 — even a five-fold revenue jump leaves Zhipu a fraction of Anthropic's and OpenAI's run rates.

Key signal: Benchmark parity and open-weight adoption are not the same as monetization — the gap between them is the story, not the growth rate alone.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
51.5 vs. 49.8: China's Two PMI Gauges Are Telling Investors Different Stories

China's private RatingDog manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5 in August — a two-month high, nine straight months of expansion, the longest run in five years — driven by new export orders growing at their fastest pace in six months. The official NBS PMI, released a day earlier, read 49.8: a second straight month of contraction.


The gap traces to sample composition — NBS weights toward large, state-linked manufacturers; RatingDog tilts toward smaller, private, export-oriented firms. Both point to AI-hardware export demand as a common thread lifting the export-facing slice of the economy while domestic demand stays soft.

Key signal: Export-oriented tech/hardware supply-chain exposure and domestically-focused, state-linked exposure are not telling the same story right now — positioning should distinguish between them.
