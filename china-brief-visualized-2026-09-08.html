STORY 1 · AI GOVERNANCE
Beijing's Police Build Their Own Chatbot: A National "Anti-Fraud AI" App Goes Live

The Ministry of Public Security launched "national anti-fraud AI" on Sept 6, built by the Shanghai Public Security Bureau. It folds LLMs, multimodal models, and AI agents into one interface: describe a suspicious call or message, get a risk assessment, scam classification, and prevention guidance across text, audio, and video.


It succeeds the 2021 National Anti-Fraud Center app, one of the most-downloaded apps in the world — driven substantially by mandated installs, not organic demand. No model vendor was named, so there's no read on which Chinese lab benefits from the distribution.

Key signal: A data point on generative AI's absorption into state services at scale — not yet a testable claim about improved fraud detection.

STORY 2 · CAPITAL MARKETS & AI
Southbound Money, Northbound Doubts: Baidu Joins Stock Connect as Its AI Numbers Get Harder to Read

Baidu's dual-primary HKEX listing took effect Sept 1; its HK shares joined Shanghai-Hong Kong Stock Connect Sept 7, opening direct mainland access. Neither move raises capital. CFO Henry He says AI revenue now exceeds half of sales, with margins approaching search "in the near term" and GPU payback in 2-3 years.


Those claims arrive undisclosed at the segment level, next to a Q2 revenue miss (-4% y/y) and ERNIE losing ground to Qwen and Kimi. A Kunlunxin spinoff is in preparation.

Key signal: Stock Connect inclusion is plumbing, not a verdict on the AI thesis — watch Kunlunxin's eventual prospectus for the margin disclosure this announcement withheld.

STORY 3 · FISCAL POLICY
300 Billion Yuan, Three Banks, Five Insurers: Beijing's Latest Capital Top-Up for State Finance

China's Ministry of Finance will issue ¥300B ($54B) in special treasury bonds to recapitalize ICBC, Agricultural Bank of China, and Eximbank, plus five insurers including China Life and China Taiping. Banks take ~¥290B of the total; ABC and ICBC get the largest shares.


The stated driver is capital erosion from weak loan demand, thin margins, and informal equity-support expectations — not a new shock. It echoes the past two years' special-bond recaps, suggesting a recurring fiscal tool that keeps the burden off the PBOC.

Key signal: Watch dilution terms for ICBC/ABC minority shareholders — pricing on the Ministry's stakes wasn't disclosed alongside the announcement.
