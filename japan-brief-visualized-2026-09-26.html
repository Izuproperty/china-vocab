STORY ONE · AI & TECH

Woven City Turns One: An AI That Can Reason About What It Sees

Toyota's Woven City experimental town in Susono marked one year of live testing on September 25, with roughly 30 proof-of-concept trials running across 80 households and 160 resident "Weavers." Seven new corporate partners joined as "Inventors," bringing the total to 31, and Toyota confirmed an AI Vision Engine 2.0 update due by year-end that adds natural-language reasoning on top of a system already at world-leading levels on vision-language benchmarks.

The Toyota AI Robot Association has placed household robots in 11 residences and logged over 1,000 completed tasks — real usage data rather than a lab demo. Phase 2 construction began this month, targeting roughly 600 residents by November 2028, with public visitor reservations opening October 1.

Investor Takeaway

Woven City is where Toyota's embodied-AI bet gets tested at small scale before it reaches production lines. The Vision Engine's reasoning upgrade and the Robot Association's task count are the closest public read available on how fast that work is actually maturing — a second data point alongside Toyota's ¥1 trillion-a-year, 400,000-robot factory-automation estimate flagged two issues ago.

STORY TWO · SEMICONDUCTORS

Kioxia's CEO Tells His Own Sales Team to Stop Raising Prices

Kioxia CEO Hiroo Ota told his sales team this week to stop pushing further NAND price increases on data-center customers, telling Bloomberg that "cloud budgets are not infinite." NAND flash prices have already risen more than 100% in Q1 2026 and another 70% in Q2, and Kioxia's operating margin sits at 40.66% without any further increases.

The restraint arrives alongside two capital-markets moves: a 3-for-1 stock split, and a US listing Kioxia confirmed on September 15 it is preparing, without committing to timing or structure — Bloomberg has reported a target above $10 billion via American Depositary Shares as early as spring 2027.

Investor Takeaway

Ota is capping prices while Kioxia's stock trades up 475% year-to-date — a bet that a durable customer base matters more than squeezing the current cycle for every yen. SK Hynix and Micron are still pushing prices higher into the same shortage; Kioxia's restraint is a test of whether the NAND run holds up once one of its three major suppliers stops driving it.

STORY THREE · MACRO & FISCAL

Takaichi's Unfunded Tax Cut Heads Into an October 5 Diet Fight

PM Takaichi's cabinet-approved cut to the consumption tax on food, from 8% to 1%, still has no identified funding source six weeks after approval. It costs roughly ¥5 trillion ($32 billion) a year; only 3 of about 120 expiring special tax measures are set to be abolished, an FX-fund surplus is already earmarked for defense, and business lobbies oppose a corporate tax hike. Takaichi said September 23 she is eager to pass the bill; an extraordinary Diet session convenes October 5.

Bond markets are not waiting. The 10-year JGB yield has climbed from roughly 1.6% to near 3% since the cut was floated, and FY2027 interest payments are now projected at ¥16.59 trillion — up ¥3.5 trillion from FY2026.

Investor Takeaway

Takaichi has ruled out deficit-financing bonds, which leaves spending cuts or a new revenue source as the only paths that don't require reversing the tax cut. Yields near 3% already price in more borrowing than the government has admitted to; October 5 determines whether Takaichi proves the bond market wrong or confirms it.
