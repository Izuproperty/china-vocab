STORY 1 · AI & TECH · CHIPS / EXPORT CONTROLS
Reuters Finds More Than ¥23 Billion ($3.4 Billion) of Chinese Computing Projects Built Around Restricted Nvidia Chips, a Day After Jensen Huang Resigned

Reuters reviewed 150+ Chinese tender (招标) and investment documents for AI computing centres (智算中心) that specify Nvidia (英伟达) H100, H200 or H800 chips, worth more than ¥23 billion ($3.4 billion) combined. Buyers are local governments, a Sinomach (国机集团) unit, universities and regional centres.


The report followed Jensen Huang's (黄仁勋) resignation on November 12 over a relationship with a former executive tied to Megaspeed. Reuters found no link between the projects and Megaspeed, and no finding of export control (出口管制) violations by Huang has been made.

Key signal: Tighter Nvidia channel checks after the Huang scandal would squeeze grey-market supply to these local projects, raising demand for domestic chips and leaving H100-specified centres at risk of sitting idle.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · CHIPS / FOUNDRY
SMIC's Third-Quarter Profit More Than Doubles to $428 Million at 97.3% Utilisation, and Co-CEO Zhao Haijun Says AI Chip Capacity Stays Tight Through 2027

SMIC (中芯国际) posted record Q3 revenue of $3.19 billion (+33.9%) and profit of $428.4 million (+123%), with gross margin (毛利率) at 29.9% and capacity utilisation (产能利用率) at 97.3%. China made up 90.8% of revenue.


Co-CEO Zhao Haijun (赵海军) guided full-year revenue above $12 billion and said AI chip supply stays tight through 2027. Q4 margin guidance drops to 26–28% as depreciation rises, and capex (资本开支) fell to $1.09 billion with some tools taking 24+ months to arrive.

Key signal: SMIC is selling every wafer it can make, and equipment access now sets the pace of growth, which ties its outlook to US and Dutch tool-servicing policy.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · CREDIT / MONETARY
China's New Bank Loans Shrink by About ¥100 Billion ($15 Billion) in October as Households Repay ¥430 Billion ($64 Billion) and Loan Growth Hits a Record-Low 4.7%

PBOC (中国人民银行) data show new yuan loans (新增人民币贷款) of about −¥100 billion (−$15 billion) in October against a +¥20 billion ($3.0 billion) forecast, the third contraction this year. Households repaid ¥430 billion ($64 billion) net and companies ¥160 billion ($24 billion).


Medium- and long-term loans turned to net repayment, pointing to early repayment (提前还贷) and weak investment demand. M2 (广义货币) slowed to 7.1% while total social financing (社会融资规模) held at 7.2% on government bond issuance.

Key signal: Households and companies are paying down debt even at record-low rates, and with the PBOC focused on the yuan, government bonds are left to carry credit growth into year-end.
