STORY 1 · AI & TECH
–¥13.8 Billion: Tencent's AI Capex Push Turns Free Cash Flow Negative

Tencent's Q2 2026 revenue rose 11% y/y to ¥204.8B, beating estimates, but capex surged 176% y/y to ¥52.8B as the company builds out compute for its Hunyuan model family — pushing reported free cash flow to –¥13.8B, the first negative quarterly print in years.


Management says FCF would have been +¥37.6B excluding compute prepayments, and ex-AI operating profit still grew 19%. The market wasn't convinced either way: US shares fell ~4.3% on the print and are down ~26% YTD — a sign investors are discounting AI infrastructure spend rather than rewarding it until cash generation proves out.

Key signal: Revenue growth and AI ambition alone no longer move the stock — the market is now pricing the capex-to-cash-flow bridge.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH
Nine of Ten: Chinese Labs Now Lead the Global AI Video Leaderboard

On Artificial Analysis's blind-rated text-to-video leaderboard, nine of the top ten models are Chinese-developed — ByteDance's Seedance 2.0 (即梦) leads, with Alibaba's Wan, Kuaishou's Kling, MiniMax's H3 and Skywork's SkyReels also placing. Google's Gemini Omni Flash is the sole non-Chinese entry near the top.


Video generation has clearer near-term monetization than chat — advertising creative, short-form content, film/TV pre-production — and ByteDance/Kuaishou's owned distribution (Douyin, Kuaishou app) gives them a data and adoption advantage. The open question is whether blind Elo scores on short clips translate into the longer-form, higher-control output professional production actually needs.

Key signal: China's AI lead is most concrete in categories — like video — where owned consumer distribution doubles as a training-data and adoption engine.

STORY 3 · MACRO & FINANCE
A Five-Year Roadmap, No New Rate Move: PBOC Sets Out Its 2026–2030 Reform Plan

The PBOC published a 2026–2030 reform plan on August 10, prioritizing "prudent" financial opening-up: expanded RMB use in cross-border trade, deeper two-way market access, Shanghai/Hong Kong's roles as financial centers, CIPS build-out, and "steady development" of the digital yuan.


No new RRR or LPR move accompanied the plan, consistent with the PBOC's 2026 pattern of favoring structural and quantity tools over headline rate cuts. It's a direction-of-travel document, not a market-moving one — the near-term investor relevance is procedural: incremental easing of friction for foreign participation in onshore RMB markets, on a timeline the plan itself doesn't specify.

Key signal: The plan signals intent, not timing — "prudent" opening has historically moved slower than the framing suggests.
