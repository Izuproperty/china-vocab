STORY 1 · AI & TECH
The FSA Starts Vetting How Japanese Lenders Price the Risk in US AI Data Center Loans

The FSA is reviewing risk management on AI data center project finance at banks and life insurers, with most projects in the US. Officials question whether hyperscalers can earn adequate returns but say they do not want to choke off lending.


Nippon Life holds about ¥1 trillion of these loans and targets ¥2 trillion by 2035 at spreads above 2 percentage points. MUFG will manage ¥4.7 trillion of infrastructure funds with BlackRock.

Investor takeaway: Tighter capital treatment would compress the spread that made data center lending attractive, with life insurers most exposed.

The FSA frames this as vetting, not a brake. Lending continues, and domestic data center loans are still under consideration at Nippon Life.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
EdgeCortix Unveils a Physical-AI Chiplet Platform, and Kawasaki Heavy Signs On for Defense Systems

RAIDEN-X4 combines four dies for 3.36 PFLOPS FP4, 256 GB of memory and 1.54 TB/s die-to-die bandwidth, about 1.6x Jetson AGX Thor per MONOist. Kawasaki Heavy picked it for aerospace and defense products; Unigen is building servers.


Sampling starts early 2027 with volume in the second half. Foundry, node and pricing are undisclosed, and Nvidia's software ecosystem remains the barrier.

Investor takeaway: A domestic defense prime choosing a Japanese accelerator supports the sovereign-compute thesis, but revenue is a 2H 2027 story.
RAIDEN Roadmap
SIGNAL · DEFENSE DEMAND
防衛装備bōei sōbi · defense equipment
Kawasaki Heavy's selection gives EdgeCortix a domestic anchor customer in a budget line that is growing.
WATCH · MANUFACTURING
チップレットchippuretto · chiplet
No foundry or node disclosed. A Rapidus or TSMC Kumamoto link would change the sovereign-supply story.
RISK · ECOSYSTEM
フィジカルAIfijikaru ēai · physical AI
Robot makers build on Nvidia's Jetson and Isaac tools. A faster chip without that software base can struggle to win volume.
STORY 3 · MACRO & BOJ
JGB Yields Above 3% Have Started Pulling Money Home, but the Big Insurers Are Still Waiting

Investors bought a net ¥4.8 trillion of JGBs last month and Japanese banks have sold about $70 billion of foreign bonds this year. Life insurers, with ¥438.6 trillion in assets, are holding off until yields stabilize.


The yen trades near ¥159 despite last week's BOJ hike. Speculators hold the largest net yen long since July 2025 at $9.7 billion.

Investor takeaway: Repatriation flows need a visible BOJ terminal rate; the late-October meeting is the next catalyst for JGBs and the yen.
