STORY 1 · AI SECURITY
Six Names, One Advisory: Washington Accuses DeepSeek, Alibaba, Moonshot and Others of "Industrial-Scale" Model Distillation

NSA, CISA, and FBI jointly named DeepSeek, Alibaba, Moonshot AI, MiniMax, StepFun, and Z.AI on Sept 8 (advisory AA26-251A), alleging "aggressive, malicious, and targeted" distillation of Claude, GPT, Gemini, and Grok since late 2024 — billions of tokens across millions of exchanges. Specific claims: Moonshot's Kimi K3 drew heavily on Claude output; Kimi K2 used GPT-4o output; DeepSeek's R1 relied on organized distillation for synthetic training data.


Distillation itself is a legitimate technique — the allegation is one of scale and intent, "likely with Chinese government awareness." Agencies are reportedly urging providers to quietly throttle suspect accounts rather than announce blocks, limiting near-term visibility into any response.

Key signal: Doesn't change what these labs can ship today, but raises odds of tighter US API access controls and adds to the case for chip/cloud restrictions ahead of the Sept 24 Trump-Xi summit.

STORY 2 · CAPITAL MARKETS
After Unitree's 629% Debut, Beijing Quietly Raises the Bar for the Next Humanoid-Robot IPO

CSRC gave banks informal "window guidance" requiring humanoid-robot IPO applicants to show recurring revenue and a path to narrower losses, or genuine innovation, before approval. Follows Unitree's Aug 19 STAR Market debut: shares opened 629% above the ¥150.80 IPO price; H1 guidance implies revenue growth decelerating to 36-45% from 333% a year earlier, against a 200x+ trailing P/E at the peak.


Window guidance is unwritten — exact thresholds and consistency of enforcement remain untested until the next applicant. The signal is direct: regulators are trying to filter the next cohort before a repeat of a debut that priced in years of hoped-for growth against one quarter of decelerating actuals.

Key signal: Narrows near-term IPO optionality for humanoid-robot startups without proven recurring revenue — most of the sector outside Unitree, on current public disclosure.

STORY 3 · CURRENCY & TRADE
Strongest Since 2023: The Yuan's Rise Is Becoming a Policy Problem, Not Just a Trade Win

Offshore yuan traded near ¥6.70/USD this week, strongest since Jan 2023 (USD/CNY closed Sept 9 at 6.7054). Follows August trade data: exports +25.0% y/y to $401.44B (vs. +23.9% in July), imports +28.2% to $282.4B, surplus widening to $119.09B. CPI accelerated to 0.8% from a six-month low of 0.5%. Traders increasingly use the yuan as a carry-trade funding currency as the yen strengthens.


A stronger yuan aids capital-account stability and import costs but risks eroding the export competitiveness currently driving the growth headline — the tension the PBOC's daily reference rate is managing. Unlike the yen's BOJ-driven story, this is more a byproduct of trade-flow dollars than a deliberate rate move; PBOC hasn't signaled a hike.

Key signal: Watch whether the daily reference rate starts leaning against further appreciation — that would signal Beijing sees the current pace as a problem, not a win.
