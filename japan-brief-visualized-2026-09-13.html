STORY 1 · AI ORCHESTRATION
Sakana AI Drops Its Three Strongest Rivals From the Model Pool — and Claims to Still Beat Them

Sakana shipped Fugu Ultra v2 (Sept 11) plus a cheaper sibling, Fugu Max. Fugu is a multi-agent orchestrator delivered as one API: a trained coordinator routes pieces of a request across an underlying model pool and synthesizes results. The news is the pool change — Sakana excluded Claude Fable 5, Fable 5.1, and GPT-6 Astra, then still claims best-or-joint-best on 5 of 8 benchmarks, including a 21-point vendor-reported gap over Claude Opus 5 on visual reasoning.


Every number is vendor-reported; no evaluation harness, per-task breakdown, or routing disclosure exists. Sakana's own FAQ says which models answered a query "is not exposed by design."

Key signal: Excluding the three strongest available systems from the comparison pool tests whether coordination beats raw capability — a legitimate question, but not the head-to-head the scoreboard implies at a glance.

STORY 2 · FOUNDRY DEMAND
A Second Straight Record Month at TSMC Is the Demand Backdrop Behind JASM Kumamoto's Next Fab Decision

TSMC's August revenue hit a record NT$514.8B, +53.3% y/y and +10.1% over July's already-record figure — the second consecutive all-time high. Jan-Aug cumulative revenue is up 39.3% y/y; management guides Q3 growth of 46.8% and record FY capex of $60-64B. Orders tied to AI-infrastructure buildout are reportedly outpacing TSMC's own capacity construction.


TSMC itself isn't a Japan story, but Kumamoto subsidiary JASM is — and a parent running above its own capacity-build pace is the demand backdrop for JASM's Fab 2 (6nm, 2027 target) and any Fab 3 decision, reinforcing the case for the equipment cluster (Tokyo Electron, Sony, "Silicon Island Kyushu") built around it.

Key signal: A parent foundry under-capacity relative to its own order book is one that keeps regional fab investments on schedule rather than deferring them — a leading indicator for JASM's ramp, not a lagging one.

STORY 3 · CORPORATE FX RISK
One Yen, Fifty Billion Yen: The Currency Move Nobody's FY Forecast Priced In

Of 360 major Japanese companies disclosing FY2026 exchange-rate assumptions, 150 built plans on ¥155-159/dollar and 35 on ¥160-164 — the yen has since strengthened to ~¥153-154, below every band. Toyota estimates each ¥1 gain cuts annual operating profit ~¥50bn; Daiwa's Kenji Abe calculated a ¥1 gain cuts aggregate listed-company pretax profit ~0.3%. The yen has gained ~¥10 since the late-July US-Japan intervention.


This is the earnings-mechanics flip side of the jawboning/hike-timing story tracked elsewhere in this Brief — concrete, not speculative, and it compounds if the Sept 17-18 BOJ meeting delivers a larger-than-expected hike.

Key signal: The weak-yen tailwind that inflated overseas-subsidiary profits and export margins for two years is now running in reverse — a structural earnings risk distinct from the hike-timing debate.
