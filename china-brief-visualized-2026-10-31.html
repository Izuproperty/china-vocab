STORY 1 · AI & TECH · CHIPS
Iluvatar CoreX Runs Its Tiangai 300 Live and Claims 1.05 Times H200 Throughput, but Only One Result Is Third-Party and Its Method Is Unpublished

Iluvatar CoreX (9903.HK) ran the Tiangai 300 live at a Shanghai media day on October 28, and SCMP reported on October 30 that the company says it matches the H200. The 1.05 times throughput figure comes from a state-owned test lab, INESA, which did not publish its method. The other comparisons, up to 1.15 times on MoE inference, are the company's own.


The card has 144GB of memory, but Iluvatar did not say what type or at what bandwidth. Its IXL144 rack works out to about 70GB per GPU, and the 40% token-cost saving names no comparison system. The shares fell 8.8% on October 29.

Key signal: A credible H200-class inference part would relieve Chinese cloud buyers paying 20% to 50% more for domestic chips, but with HBM scarce and the memory spec missing, the claim needs a public benchmark and a named volume customer before it supports the valuation.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · FOUNDATION MODELS
01.AI Takes Several Hundred Million Yuan From Hangzhou State Funds and Moves Its Headquarters to Linping, Ahead of a Planned 2027 Hong Kong IPO

Kai-Fu Lee's 01.AI signed on October 29 for several hundred million yuan from Hangzhou municipal and Linping district state capital, with its headquarters and an industrial AI base moving to Linping. The money is part of a Pre-IPO round ahead of a Hong Kong listing targeted for 2027.


The company stopped training its own trillion-parameter model in 2025 and now customizes DeepSeek, Qwen and GLM for enterprises. Revenue went from just over ¥100 million ($15 million) in 2024 to ¥250 million ($38 million) in 2025, and the 2026 contract target is ¥1.5 billion ($226 million).

Key signal: Local state money now arrives bundled with compute subsidies and customers, which can fill a contract book and replace US-dollar fund capital, but project work that needs on-site engineers keeps margins thin, so the prospectus test is recognized revenue and gross margin.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · INSURERS
Ping An's Third-Quarter Profit Falls 93.7% to ¥4.06 Billion and CPIC's 68.4% as the Stock Slide Turns Equity Gains Into Fair-Value Losses

Ping An's Q3 net profit fell 93.7% to ¥4.06 billion ($611 million) and CPIC's fell 68.4% to ¥5.63 billion ($846 million), both reported October 28. Ping An's operating profit still rose 8.3% over nine months to ¥125.9 billion ($18.9 billion), while new business value fell 13.3%.


Equities held at fair value through profit drove the drop. Ping An's fair-value line swung from a ¥48.0 billion ($7.22 billion) gain to a ¥64.3 billion ($9.67 billion) loss, and its comprehensive yield fell to 2.3% from 5.4%. The CSI 300 entered a bear market on October 22.

Key signal: Insurers asked to buy stocks for market support now carry that volatility in earnings, which pushes new equity money toward high-dividend shares booked through OCI and away from growth and AI-hardware names; dividends tied to operating profit look safe.
