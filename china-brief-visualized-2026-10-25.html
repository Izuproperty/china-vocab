STORY 1 · AI & TECH · CHIPS
Cambricon Doubles Q3 Revenue to ¥3.53 Billion but Misses Forecasts, While Inventory Climbs to ¥11.66 Billion and Cash Falls to ¥555 Million

Cambricon's Q3 revenue rose 104.5% to ¥3.53B and net profit 147.7% to ¥1.40B, filed after the close on October 23. Bloomberg said sales missed projections because of production bottlenecks and tight HBM supply. The ¥13.5B full-year target needs about ¥3.97B in Q4.


Inventory rose ¥3.42B in the quarter to ¥11.66B, notes payable reached ¥4.97B from ¥351M at the start of the year, and supplier prepayments hit ¥3.04B. Cash fell to ¥555M and Q3 operating cash flow was negative ¥84M.

Key signal: Cambricon is locking up SMIC wafers and HBM ahead of demand, so the risk now sits in working capital: about eight quarters of cost of sales in stock, ¥555M of cash, and ¥4.97B of bills that banks need to keep rolling.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · ROBOTICS
Unitree's Wang Xingxing Pulls His Humanoid Timeline Forward to 2028, Two Months After Saying Mass Use Could Take a Decade

Wang Xingxing said on October 23 in Hangzhou that humanoids could complete 80% of voice-given tasks in factories and homes as soon as 2028, a day after telling CMG a breakthrough could come in 2027. In August he put mass use about ten years out.


He named Unitree Rehearsal, where AI agents read papers, write control code and test it in simulation before real trials, as the year's top project. Digitimes puts cumulative G1 output at 10,000 to 20,000 units; Bloomberg says the shares sit about 25% below their August peak.

Key signal: A listed founder cutting his sector's horizon from ten years to two supports volume-based valuations, but the claim has no task definition or outside benchmark, and it lands as CAICT warns of a bubble and regulators slow humanoid IPOs.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · FISCAL
Land-Sale Revenue Falls 27.9% in Nine Months as Tax Receipts Grow 6.1%, While Budget Spending Rises Only 1% and Local Bond Sales Reach 81% of Quota

MOF data for January to September show general budget revenue up 5.1% to ¥17.23T and tax up 6.1%, while spending rose 1% to ¥21.02T. Land-sale revenue fell 27.9% to ¥1.61T and fund-budget revenue 18.6%.


Local governments issued ¥3.56T of new special bonds, 81% of quota, and ¥1.90T of debt-swap bonds. ¥550B of unused quota is in use for Q4, and ¥300B of special bonds for bank recapitalization is planned this quarter.

Key signal: Taxes are growing six times faster than general spending, so the budget is tighter than the deficit suggests, and Q4 bond supply (remaining quota, ¥550B and ¥300B for banks) lands while the PBOC eases liquidity rather than cutting rates.
