STORY 1 · AI & TECH
SoftBank and Nokia Will Put AI-RAN on Live Traffic in Japan in 2027

ソフトバンク株式会社 (Sofutobanku Kabushiki-gaisha, SoftBank Corp.), the telecom arm of SoftBank Group, and Nokia agreed on October 20 to run an AI-RAN field trial on SoftBank's 商用網 (shōyōmō, commercial network) in Japan in 2027. Nokia's anyRAN cloud RAN software will run on AITRAS, SoftBank's own AI-RAN platform, with the AITRAS Orchestrator allocating server capacity between radio processing and AI jobs and Nokia's MantaRay network software linked into it. The hardware is Nvidia's Aerial RAN Computer, which pairs a Grace CPU with an RTX PRO 4500 GPU and ConnectX-8 networking. Neither company named trial sites or financial terms. SoftBank CTO 湧川隆次 (Wakikawa Ryūji, Ryuji Wakikawa) called the trial a milestone in moving AI-RAN from concept to execution.


AI-RAN puts GPU servers at the 基地局 (kichikyoku, base station) so the same machine handles the radio signal and sells spare compute for AI inference. The business case rests on that second job. A GPU server costs far more than the custom chips in a conventional base station, and it pays back only if AI workloads fill the hours when mobile traffic is light. SoftBank launched AITRAS in November 2024 and reported about 20% better uplink throughput in weak-coverage areas from outdoor tests. In August an Ericsson AI scheduler on its live 5G network raised 周波数利用効率 (shūhasū riyō kōritsu, spectral efficiency) by up to about 25% at the best sites. The Nokia trial is the first plan to run both jobs on one platform under real customer traffic while meeting carrier-grade reliability.


Nokia is the second large radio vendor on SoftBank's AI-RAN stack after Ericsson, which helps SoftBank sell AITRAS to other 通信事業者 (tsūshin jigyōsha, telecom carriers). For Nvidia the trial supplies a live commercial reference for its ARC hardware. The agreement came the same day SoftBank Group's five-year credit default swaps hit a four-year high on concern over AI funding and its CoreWeave exposure; the parent's shares fell 6% on October 20 and recovered 3.5% the next morning. AI-RAN runs on network assets SoftBank Corp. already owns, so its returns do not hinge on OpenAI's valuation.

Investor takeaway: SoftBank Corp. (TSE: 9434) gains if AITRAS becomes a product other carriers license, and Nokia's involvement makes that sale easier outside Japan. Watch the 2027 trial for disclosed GPU utilization and AI revenue per site, and for any commitment to GPU-based RAN from KDDI or NTT Docomo. If radio processing cannot share the GPU without hurting reliability, the plan narrows to a network-efficiency upgrade with no new revenue line.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Chip Exports Rise 49% in September While Japan's Export Growth Slows to 16.7%

The 財務省 (Zaimushō, Ministry of Finance) reported on October 21 that Japan's exports rose 16.7% from a year earlier in September to ¥10.97 trillion, the 13th straight monthly gain. Exports of 半導体等電子部品 (handōtai-tō denshi buhin, semiconductors and other electronic parts) rose 49.4% to ¥933.7 billion. 電気機器 (denki kiki, electrical machinery), the category that contains them, rose 30.3% to ¥2.15 trillion, and general machinery, which includes chipmaking equipment, rose 18.4% to ¥1.95 trillion. 輸送用機器 (yusōyō kiki, transport equipment), still the largest category, rose 10.8% to ¥2.28 trillion.


Total exports missed the 20.1% consensus and slowed from 19.3% in August. Chip-related goods supplied most of the growth that did arrive. Electrical machinery and general machinery together added about ¥800 billion from a year earlier, roughly half of the ¥1.57 trillion increase in total exports, and electronic parts alone added about ¥310 billion. Imports in the same semiconductor category rose only 5.3% to ¥605 billion, leaving Japan a surplus of about ¥330 billion in it. By destination, exports to the US rose 22.9% to ¥1.96 trillion, to Asia 17.5% to ¥6.07 trillion and to China 8.6%. Exports to the Middle East fell 12%. Companies passing higher costs into prices and a yen near ¥158 per dollar both lift the yen value of shipments, so part of the gain is price.


The electronic parts line includes NAND flash from キオクシア (Kiokushia, Kioxia) along with sensors and power chips, and its growth tracks AI server construction abroad. BOJ Deputy Governor Himino cited the same AI demand for capex and exports on the same day. Tokyo chip stocks rebounded on October 21 after the Nikkei closed at 64,075 on October 20, its lowest in over a month: Kioxia rose 5.1% and Advantest 4.8% in the morning session. Kioxia reports second-quarter results on October 30.

Investor takeaway: The data shows foreign demand for Japanese chips and chip tools growing about three times as fast as total exports, which supports second-quarter results at Kioxia (TSE: 285A) and Advantest (TSE: 6857). It also shows concentration risk: with transport equipment up 10.8% and Middle East shipments falling, Japan's export growth depends more each month on one capex cycle. Watch Kioxia's October 30 results for how much of the gain comes from NAND prices versus bit shipments, and October's first 20-day trade figures in mid-November.

STORY 3 · MACRO
BOJ Deputy Governor Himino Says Underlying Inflation Is Almost at 2% and That Waiting Has a Cost

氷見野良三 (Himino Ryōzō, Ryozo Himino), deputy governor of the 日本銀行 (Nippon Ginkō, Bank of Japan), said on October 21 that 基調的な物価上昇率 (kichōteki na bukka jōshōritsu, underlying inflation) is almost at the 2% target and likely to settle there. He said medium- and long-term inflation expectations keep rising, real interest rates are low, bank lending is growing fast and Japan still has 緩和的な金融環境 (kanwateki na kin'yū kankyō, accommodative financial conditions). He said strong AI demand is driving capital spending and exports and that upside risks to prices are elevated. He also warned that postponing policy adjustment could let inflation risks materialise and damage the economy.


The board meets October 29-30 and publishes a new outlook report. Governor Ueda said on October 16 that the meeting would decide whether underlying inflation has reached 2%, and his deputy has now given his own answer in public. The policy rate has been 1.25% since September 18. Markets still price almost no chance of an October move and about 74% for December, and the yen did not react: USD/JPY was at 158.35 after the remarks, up 0.13% on the day.


The trade data released the same morning shows more cost pressure ahead. September's deficit of ¥536.6 billion was about three times the ¥181 billion economists expected, and import growth slowed to 18.9% mainly because crude cargoes arrived late after tankers rerouted away from the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. The government expects October 原油調達 (gen'yu chōtatsu, crude procurement) to recover to about 100% of last year's level with Brent near $110 a barrel, which will push the energy import bill back up while the yen sits near ¥158. Himino's warning about delay, made while those import costs are rising, makes December the base case and leaves October more open than near-zero pricing implies.

Investor takeaway: A hike pulled forward from December would lift short-dated JGB yields and help bank earnings at MUFG (TSE: 8306) and SMFG (TSE: 8316), and it would trim the yen tailwind that inflated September's export values. Watch September national CPI on October 23, where forecasts put headline at 2.8-3.0%, then the outlook report's core CPI forecasts and any dissent on October 30.
