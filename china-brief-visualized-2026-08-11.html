STORY 1 · AI & TECH / ROBOTICS SHIPMENT DATA
8,400 Versus 5,900: AgiBot Overtakes Unitree in the First Independent Humanoid Shipment Count

Independent research firm Smart Analytics Global reported AgiBot shipped 8,400 humanoid robots in H1 2026 (44% global share), overtaking Unitree's 5,900 units (31%). Chinese makers held over 97% of the ~19,100 units shipped globally, more than triple H1 2025's 5,100.


The data lands the same day Unitree opened its STAR Market IPO subscription — a listing priced off a shipment-leadership claim this report now disputes, echoing an earlier Omdia count that also favored AgiBot.

Key signal: Unitree's IPO pricing is the first public valuation benchmark for the whole humanoid category — including AgiBot's own pending HK listing — built on shipment data that remains contested and self-reported.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH / CHIPS & EXPORT CONTROLS
The Legal Workaround: Washington Reviews How Chinese Labs Rent Their Way to Nvidia Compute

BIS is reviewing how Chinese AI firms access Nvidia chips by renting GPU capacity from data centers outside China — a legal channel distinct from direct chip exports. The review follows Kimi K3 and Qwen3.8-Max, which renewed questions about how much frontier compute Chinese labs can still reach.


No new rule has been announced. A closure of this pathway would raise the marginal cost of large training runs and add a further tailwind to Huawei Ascend, Cambricon and the broader domestic-chip substitution story.

Key signal: The compute-access map to watch has three lanes now, not two — direct exports, rationed H200 allocations, and offshore rental — and only the third remains largely unregulated.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO / BOND MARKETS
Historic Opportunity, Not Yet Sufficient: Goldman Sachs on China's Panda Bond Boom

Panda bond issuance reached ¥160B in H1 2026, up more than 60% y/y; dim sum bond issuance hit ¥358B, also up over 60%. International issuers accounted for roughly half of gross issuance in each market, with the mix shifting from offshore property developers toward utilities, consumer companies and sovereign borrowers.


Goldman's Chen Xinquan calls the boom a "historic opportunity" for the yuan, driven by lower funding costs rather than appreciation expectations — but cautions that cheap funding alone won't sustain global yuan use without broader market access.

Key signal: The bond boom is a real, structural data point for yuan internationalization — but Goldman's own note treats it as necessary, not sufficient, pending deeper cross-border market access.
