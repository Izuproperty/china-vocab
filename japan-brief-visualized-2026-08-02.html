STORY 1 · AI & TECH BigGo Finance / NVIDIA Newsroom
Japan's "sovereign" AI builders all lean on the same foreign stack: NVIDIA's open Nemotron

SB Intuitions (Sarashina, plus a new Large Telecom Model), NTT Data (tsuzumi 2), the Institute of Science Tokyo (Swallow), and GENIAC-backed startup Stockmark (JapanDocReader) all build their "domestic" Japanese-language models on top of NVIDIA's open Nemotron models, datasets, and training libraries — the same open-source stack Sakana AI folded into Fugu last month.

The dependency extends to infrastructure: on July 16, METI and NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang announced Japan would procure 27,500 next-generation Rubin GPUs — the largest disclosed national Rubin order anywhere — to power the ¥1 trillion Noetra physical-AI project.

Investor read-through: Japan's AI-sovereignty framing holds up as sovereignty over data and deployment surface, not over compute or open-source tooling — worth separating the two when pricing "de-risking from US tech" narratives around Noetra and its members.

STORY 2 · SEMI & INDUSTRIAL Company results / Investing.com
Kioxia swings from meltdown to record quarter, announces ¥800 billion buyback

Q1 FY2026 revenue reached ¥1,767.1bn (+76.2% QoQ, +415.5% YoY) with non-GAAP operating profit of ¥1,326.2bn at a 75% margin — exceeding Kioxia's entire FY2025 operating profit in a single quarter, driven by a ~70% rise in average NAND selling prices and AI-linked SSD demand.

Kioxia reached a net cash position and announced an ¥800bn share buyback, three days after an 18.3% single-day drop tied to China's CXMT listing and roughly two weeks removed from a ~60% drawdown from its 2026 peak. Shares jumped 17.72% on the print.

Investor read-through: A genuinely strong data point against the memory-oversupply thesis, but CXMT's ramp and Chinese domestic DUV progress remain structural risks one quarter doesn't retire — the Q2 guide is the next test.

STORY 3 · MACRO & TRADE Ministry of Finance / Customs
Japan's June trade balance swings to deficit as energy and chip imports outrun a weak-yen export boom

June's trade balance flipped to a ¥409.9bn deficit, reversing a ¥122.3bn surplus a year earlier. Exports rose 19.3% to a three-year high of ¥10.93tn — helped by the weak yen and AI-linked semiconductor demand (semiconductor exports +53.8%) — but imports rose faster, +25.4% to ¥11.34tn, led by mineral fuels (+41.9%) and semiconductor imports (+49.2%).

Japan is simultaneously a large net exporter and net importer of AI-linked chips, so the same demand cycle shows up on both sides of the ledger — while a weak yen inflates the nominal export line more than it improves the underlying trade position.

Investor read-through: Strengthens MOF's case for continued currency intervention and feeds the BOJ's inflation calculus ahead of any autumn hike — one month of data, so the July/August prints are the next checkpoint.
