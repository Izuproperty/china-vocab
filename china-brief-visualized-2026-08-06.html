STORY 1 · ROBOTICS / IPO
Book-Building Opens: Unitree Prices Its Humanoid Bet at a ¥42B Floor as Growth Cools

Institutional book-building for Unitree's STAR Market IPO opened August 5; retail subscription follows August 10. The offering targets up to ¥4.2B at a ¥42B valuation floor, the fastest STAR Market review on record (104 days, application to approval).


The prospectus is the more telling document: H1 2026 revenue growth guided to 35.6%–45.4% y/y, down from 332.64% in H1 2025; adjusted net profit down 6.4%–22.0%; humanoid ASP fell from ¥593K (2023) to ¥166K (2025), pulling humanoid gross margin from 87.67% to 63.18%.

Key signal: Pricing is being set against decelerating unit economics, not the headline shipment-share narrative — and Omdia disputes that narrative outright.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Hedging in Public: Samsung and SK Hynix Reportedly Test AMEC Tools They Both Deny Wanting

Reuters reported Samsung and SK Hynix have tested AMEC etching equipment for potential use at China fabs since roughly 2024, hedging against tighter US export controls after 2023 Validated End User status was revoked in 2025.


Both companies issued on-record denials. The gap between the reporting and the denials is itself a data point about how foreign chipmakers in China are managing disclosure risk around US policy exposure.

Key signal: Confirmed AMEC orders — not testing reports — would be the real inflection point for domestic equipment-maker credibility with non-Chinese customers.

STORY 3 · TRADE POLICY
Case-by-Case From Now On: MOFCOM Answers US Drone Curbs With Export Controls and a Six-Entity List

MOFCOM announced same-day countermeasures Wednesday: drone exports to the US now face case-by-case strict review rather than automatic licensing, and six US entities were added to China's countermeasure list.


The response mirrors the licensing-friction playbook MOFCOM has used on rare earths since 2023, extended to the drone sector in direct response to the FCC's Chinese-drone import ban and DHS's UFLPA entity-list expansion.

Key signal: The mechanism — discretionary review, not a ban — preserves Beijing's ability to tighten or loosen without a new rule, a durable feature of the broader tit-for-tat pattern.
