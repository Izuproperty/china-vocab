STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Tokio Marine Says Cyber Claims Tripled in October and Sompo Reports 2.5 Times Its Average as AI Speeds Up Attacks

東京海上ホールディングス (Tōkyō Kaijō Hōrudingusu, Tokio Marine Holdings) received three times its usual number of サイバー保険 (saibā hoken, cyber insurance) claim notifications in October, and SOMPOホールディングス (Sonpo Hōrudingusu, Sompo Holdings) said its October 保険金請求 (hokenkin seikyū, insurance claims) ran 2.5 times the monthly average for the year to September 30, Bloomberg reported on November 20. Both insurers gave the figures at earnings briefings on November 19 and linked the rise to attackers using AI to find and exploit weak points faster. Sompo executive vice president Junsuke Koike said customer inquiries about raising coverage amounts or adding coverage items have also tripled.


Tokio Marine managing executive officer Yoshinari Endo said the company expects some impact on full-year results, but losses should stay inside the assumptions in its initial plan, and the cyber line should remain profitable in the year to March 2027. He said Tokio Marine may cap payouts or tighten terms depending on each customer's risk profile. Standard 支払限度額 (shiharai gendogaku, payout limits) run from about ¥100 million to several hundred million yen per contract. Yasuko Fukuda, senior executive officer at MS&AD Insurance Group Holdings, said the market has significant room to expand. A 2025 General Insurance Association of Japan survey found that only 8.8% of 中小企業 (chūshō kigyō, small and medium-sized enterprises) owners carried cyber cover. Sompo will launch a service with Trend Micro in December that monitors customers' internet-facing vulnerabilities.


The October spike also showed up outside Japan. Broker Marsh said on November 17 that its clients reported 105 cyber incidents in the last two weeks of October, against 48 in the last two weeks of May, and its North American cyber practice leader attributed a significant number to AI tools. In Japan the claims follow the Takaichi government's November 7 emergency cyber package, adopted after NTT Docomo said up to 300,000 customer records were stolen (Issue #161). Demand and losses are rising at the same time. If the insurers raise limits only for customers who pass vulnerability checks, premium growth can outrun claims. If AI-assisted attackers keep hitting many policyholders through the same software flaw, a single campaign can produce correlated losses that per-contract pricing does not cover.

Investor takeaway: Tokio Marine says October's cyber losses stay within its plan, so the near-term earnings hit is small. The larger exposure is correlation, because one AI-assisted exploit of widely used software can trigger claims across many SME policyholders at once. Watch February's third-quarter results for cyber loss figures and any tightening of policy terms.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Kioxia Falls 11.7% and Advantest 10.3% as the Nikkei Drops 5.1% to a Seven-Month Low Before a Three-Day Weekend

The 日経平均株価 (Nikkei Heikin Kabuka, Nikkei Stock Average) fell 3,007.56 points, or 5.06%, to 56,436.65 on November 20, its lowest close since April 13 and the fifth-largest one-day point drop on record, Jiji Press reported. The Topix fell 3.88% to 3,674.22, and 214 of the 225 Nikkei stocks closed lower. キオクシア (Kiokushia, Kioxia) fell 11.66%, アドバンテスト (Adobantesuto, Advantest) 10.32% and 東京エレクトロン (Tōkyō Erekutoron, Tokyo Electron) 7.06%, while ソフトバンクグループ (Sofutobanku Gurūpu, SoftBank Group) lost 14.48%. The Nikkei ended the week down 7.7%.


Wall Street set up the drop. On Thursday the Nasdaq fell 2.25% and the S&P 500 fell 2.02% for a sixth straight decline, and Nvidia lost 3.3%. Anthropic opened its first trading day at $111.50 against its $83 IPO price but closed at $97.17, and a Tokyo asset-management strategist said hopes that the listing would revive AI stocks faded as it gave up gains. Seoul fell with Tokyo. The Kospi dropped 5.21%, with Samsung Electronics down 7.01% and SK Hynix down 5.92%. Jiji also cited 持ち高調整 (mochidaka chōsei, position adjustment) before the long weekend, because Tokyo is closed on Monday for 勤労感謝の日 (Kinrō Kansha no Hi, Labor Thanksgiving Day).


Kioxia fell hardest among the chip names because its profits depend on NAND contract prices, and Nvidia's strong volume guidance does nothing to support those. The stock had already dropped 6.9% to ¥8,467 on November 16, about 77% below its split-adjusted June peak, and its October-December guidance came in below consensus. Advantest's tester orders track GPU unit volumes, which Nvidia guided about 12% higher for its next quarter (Issue #173). Tokyo Electron still has a ¥150 billion buyback running to March 31, 2027. None of the three changed guidance this week, so Friday's prices reflect US sentiment and pre-holiday selling. Tokyo reopens on Tuesday and will price two US sessions at once.

Investor takeaway: More than 90% of Prime stocks fell, so the selling came from US AI sentiment and pre-holiday de-risking, and no Japanese chipmaker cut guidance. Kioxia carries the most downside because its guide depends on NAND prices that Nvidia's volume outlook does not lift. Tuesday's open will price two US sessions; watch whether Advantest and Tokyo Electron hold above their November 16 closes.

STORY 3 · MACRO
October CPI Rises to 2.3%, but Core-Core Inflation Slows to 2.2% as Utility Subsidies Lapse and Fresh Food Jumps

Japan's 消費者物価指数 (shōhisha bukka shisū, consumer price index) rose 2.3% from a year earlier in October, up from 2.1% in September and the fastest pace since November 2025, according to data released on November 20 by the statistics bureau of 総務省 (Sōmushō, Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications). Core CPI, which excludes fresh food and is officially called 生鮮食品を除く総合 (seisen shokuhin o nozoku sōgō), rose 2.2%, up from 2.1% but below the 2.3% consensus. The measure that also strips out energy, known in markets as core-core, slowed to 2.2% from 2.4%.


Two items lifted the headline. Fresh food prices rose sharply, and energy costs rose because the government briefly scaled back subsidies on 電気・ガス料金 (denki gasu ryōkin, electricity and gas bills). Tokyo plans to restore those subsidies for the winter months, which will lower energy inflation in the winter readings. Neither item reflects domestic demand. The core-core slowdown means prices outside food and energy rose more slowly in October than in September.


The 日本銀行 (Nihon Ginkō, Bank of Japan) meets on December 17-18 with its policy rate at 1.25%. A Reuters poll published on November 18 found 86% of economists expect a hike to 1.50%, and Governor Ueda told parliament this week that the larger risk to inflation is now an overshoot of 2% (Issue #174). Core-core at 2.2% is still above the target, and a yen near 160 keeps raising import costs: producer prices rose 7.1% in October with yen-based import prices up 24.8% (Issue #167). Capital Economics expects the BOJ to lift rates to 2.5% by the middle of next year. A board member who wants to wait now has one soft reading to cite, and Tokyo's November CPI on November 27 is the last major price release before the meeting.

Investor takeaway: Fresh food and a temporary lapse in utility subsidies drove the headline rise, while core-core slowed to 2.2%. That leaves the December hike case resting on the weak yen and import costs, since domestic demand is not adding to inflation. A second soft core-core print in Tokyo's November CPI on November 27 would raise the odds that the BOJ waits until January.
