STORY 1 · AI & TECH
XPeng Names Its Robotaxi YOYO and Opens Invite-Only Rides After 2,200 Employee Trips, With Break-Even Pencilled In for Late 2027

XPeng named its robotaxi business 小鹏悠游 (XPENG YOYO) on October 8 and launched a booking mini-program that admits outside riders by invitation code. The GX-based car runs VLA 2.0 on four Turing chips (3,000 TOPS) without lidar or HD maps, after more than 2,200 employee rides since July.


XPeng targets rides without safety drivers in 2027 and vehicle-level break-even in Guangzhou in the second half of 2027. That break-even counts each car's fares against its running costs only, so it says nothing yet about when the unit as a whole earns money. Partners will run fleets, and none has been named.

Key signal: XPeng has opened its robotaxi to outside riders, but until it discloses fleet size and fare data its 2027 driverless and break-even targets cannot be measured against Baidu Apollo Go and Pony.ai.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH
StepFun Sets an October 13 Launch for STEPX Neo, Betting a Model Lab Can Sell Its Own Agent Phone

StepFun said on October 8 that STEPX Neo, first shown at WAIC in July, launches commercially on October 13. Step AOS splits app functions into services its Amoo agent can call, and the phone's on-device generative AI service cleared regulatory filing on September 23.


Owning the OS lets StepFun's agent act across apps without needing Android or a handset maker's permission. Its L3 rating matches devices from Huawei, Xiaomi, OPPO and vivo, so the rating does not set it apart. Hardware adds inventory and thin margins to a model company preparing a Hong Kong IPO.

Key signal: Owning the operating system lets StepFun's agent operate apps without a platform owner's permission, and the October 13 price and day-one app list will show whether that advantage covers the inventory and margin burden hardware adds before a Hong Kong IPO.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
A-Shares Reopen With the STAR 50 Down 4.82% and ChiNext Down 3.15% as Chip and Optical Stocks Hit Limit-Down on ¥1.68 Trillion Turnover

Mainland stocks fell in the first session after the holiday. The STAR 50 dropped 4.82% and ChiNext 3.15%, while the Shanghai Composite lost 0.79% to 3,811.9. Turnover rose ¥244.1 billion to about ¥1.68 trillion, and decliners beat gainers 3,742 to 1,696.


Chip and co-packaged optics stocks led the fall after a rumor of 1.6T module price cuts that companies denied and a Morgan Stanley note on US FCC policy. Oil majors and banks rose. The STAR 50's roughly 14% gain this year left it most exposed to profit-taking. The PBOC added ¥120 billion through a three-month outright reverse repo.

Key signal: The first session after the holiday hit the year's best performers hardest, with chip and optical names limit-down while banks and oil majors rose, which leaves AI hardware positioning exposed to any confirmation of new US restrictions.
