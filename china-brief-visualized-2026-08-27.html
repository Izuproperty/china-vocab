STORY 1 · AI & TECH / FOUNDATION MODELS
6 of 125 Billion: Alibaba's Newest Qwen Runs Lean and Says It Beats Opus 4.6 Anyway

Alibaba released Qwen3.8-Flash-Next open-weight on Aug 26: a 125B-parameter MoE model activating only 6B parameters per token, plus a 51B-parameter N-gram embedding layer that runs on system RAM instead of GPU memory. Self-published benchmarks claim wins over DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Claude Opus 4.6 on a majority of tasks, at roughly 1/9th the training cost of Qwen3.7-Plus.


The pitch is cost-per-token, not raw capability — a 6B-active model is far cheaper to serve at scale than Alibaba's own 2.4T-parameter Qwen3.8-Max. But the comparisons are self-published and unverified, following the same pattern as the Qwen3.8-Max claims three weeks earlier.

Key signal: Alibaba is now shipping architecture previews ahead of full releases — a cadence play to stay in the open-weight conversation against DeepSeek and Moonshot, independent of whether any single benchmark claim holds up.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH / AUTONOMOUS DRIVING REGULATION
Not the Robot — The Manufacturer: China's Draft Traffic Law Decides Who Pays When Autopilot Is at Fault

A first reading of a Road Traffic Safety Law amendment, opened Aug 25 at the NPC Standing Committee's 24th session, adds an autonomous-vehicle chapter assigning traffic-violation liability to the manufacturer or importer whenever the autonomous-driving function is engaged — not the occupant.


Over 70% of 2026 passenger-vehicle sales already carry L2 systems and Urban NOA penetration has passed 20%, so the framework touches a large share of the domestic fleet already on the road, not a future hypothetical segment.

Key signal: Manufacturer-liability principles reshape how OEMs price risk into feature labeling and give insurers a clear underwriting counterparty — but this is a first reading with no effective date, the earliest stage of a multi-reading process.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO / TRADE POLICY
7.5 Points, One Month Out: Washington Tests the Tariff Ceiling It Promised Beijing

Bloomberg reported Aug 24 that Washington is preparing a ~7.5% Section 301 tariff on Chinese goods tied to an excess-manufacturing-capacity probe, timed for release before the Sept. 24 Xi-Trump summit. That would push the cumulative second-term China tariff rate to roughly 20% — the exact ceiling MOFCOM said on July 27 that Washington had committed to.


One option under discussion: publish a higher headline rate but suspend part of it to land at an effective 7.5%, letting Washington claim a bigger nominal number while landing where Beijing was told to expect.

Key signal: Overcapacity tariffs historically concentrate in solar, steel, EVs, and battery materials rather than landing broadly — sector-specific exposure matters more here than the headline rate.
