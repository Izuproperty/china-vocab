STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Sony Raises Its Profit Guide to ¥1.87 Trillion on PlayStation and Spider-Man, While Image Sensor Profit Rises 12% in a Phone Market Down 20%

ソニーグループ (Sonī Gurūpu, Sony Group) reported on November 5 that July-September sales rose 10% to ¥3.429 trillion, operating profit rose 45% to ¥622.3 billion and net profit rose 44% to ¥449.6 billion. All three are records for a second quarter, and first-half operating profit passed ¥1 trillion, CFO 陶琳 (Tō Rin, Lin Tao) said. Sony raised its full-year forecast for operating profit by ¥150 billion to ¥1.87 trillion and for net profit by ¥120 billion to ¥1.33 trillion, 29% above last year and a record. Sales guidance rose ¥500 billion to ¥13 trillion. Entertainment drove the increase. Game and network services profit rose 83% to ¥220.1 billion as PlayStation monthly active users reached a September record of 127 million, and Pictures profit rose to ¥92.6 billion from ¥13.9 billion on the release of Spider-Man: Brand New Day.


Sony's AI exposure sits in its イメージセンサー (imēji sensā, image sensor) business, which supplies the cameras that feed vision AI in phones, cars and robots. Segment sales fell 6% to ¥577.6 billion because the smartphone market shrank about 20% from a year earlier. Tao blamed memory prices, which raised the cost of midrange and cheaper phones and cut their volumes the most. Operating profit still rose 12% to a second-quarter record of ¥154.6 billion, helped by a richer product mix and a ¥25.9 billion currency gain. Sony raised the segment's full-year profit guide by ¥30 billion to ¥450 billion, a figure that already absorbs about ¥16 billion of losses from the July 28 Kumamoto earthquake.


Executive Naoya Horii said some customers did not launch new phones in September as usual, which moves sensor shipments into the second half without changing the annual total. EE Times Japan read the remark as a reference to Apple. Horii also said the largest phone brands are gaining share, which favors Sony because its high-end sensors go into their flagship models. Wafer input averaged 147,000 a month in the quarter against capacity of 160,000. The longer bet is the ¥747 billion Kumamoto joint venture with TSMC signed in August, with mass production planned for 2029. Tao said it will make Sony's sensors cheaper and better "for the physical AI era." Sony also booked about ¥30 billion of further losses to wind down Sony Honda Mobility and said those charges are now largely complete.

Investor takeaway: Films and games carry Sony's raised guide, and the sensor unit grew profit on lower sales because flagship phones held up while cheaper ones fell. Memory costs now set the sensor volume outlook, because they push up the bill of materials for the midrange Android phones that buy mid-size sensors. Content and services make up about 70% of sales and profit, so the group can absorb a weak phone year. The guide implies second-half operating profit up about 14% on flat sales, and the delayed iPhone shipments have to land in that period for it to hold.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Rohm Raises Its Operating Profit Guide 67% to ¥50 Billion on Data Centers and Factory Orders, and Says Power-Chip Merger Talks Are Running Late

ローム (Rōmu, Rohm), the Kyoto maker of power and analog chips, raised its forecast for the year to March 2027 with its first-half results. It now expects sales of ¥550 billion, up from ¥510 billion, and operating profit of ¥50 billion, up from ¥30 billion. Ordinary profit rises 47% to ¥53 billion and net profit to ¥45 billion from ¥29 billion. Rohm said spending on data centers is growing and demand from factory automation (FA) is recovering. It also credited 価格適正化 (kakaku tekiseika, price correction), its term for raising prices to cover costs. Investing.com reported that second-quarter operating profit of ¥7.9 billion missed analyst forecasts, so the new guide depends on a stronger second half.


The upgrade follows a ¥158.4 billion net loss in the year to March 2026, when Rohm booked a ¥193.6 billion 減損 (genson, impairment) on its power semiconductor assets after silicon carbide (SiC) capacity ran ahead of electric vehicle demand. That write-down lowered the depreciation charged against this year's sales, which helps the margin recovery. At the results briefing on November 5, President 東克己 (Azuma Katsumi, Katsumi Azuma) said the 事業統合 (jigyō tōgō, business integration) of power chips with Toshiba and Mitsubishi Electric is taking longer than first planned. He said the talks "have not stalled and are moving forward," NHK reported, but gave no new date.


The three companies signed a basic agreement on March 27. Jiji reported in late September that they still disagreed on equity ratios, which businesses to carve out and who would lead, and that the target had slipped to the November interim results with March 2027 as a fallback. Azuma's comments confirm that the November target has passed. A combined business would rank second in power semiconductors behind Infineon, and METI has said it supports a domestic consolidation. The results landed in a weak market: the Nikkei 225 fell 1.79% to 61,077.90 on November 5, a fourth straight loss, as AI hardware stocks kept falling and Ibiden hit its daily limit down.

Investor takeaway: Rohm's profit recovery comes from price increases, a cyclical rebound in factory orders and a lower depreciation base after last year's write-down. The merger has more bearing on the stock than the guide, because consolidation is the route to cutting duplicate SiC capacity across three companies that each overbuilt for EVs. March 2027 is now the realistic deadline for a structure. Until then, Rohm's second half has to deliver most of the ¥50 billion in operating profit, and the Q2 shortfall leaves little room for a slower FA recovery.

STORY 3 · MACRO
BOJ Minutes Show a Board Member Wanted to Debate Larger Rate Hikes When the Bank Raised to 1.25% in September

The 日本銀行 (Nippon Ginkō, Bank of Japan) released the 議事要旨 (giji yōshi, meeting minutes) of its September meeting on November 5. At that meeting the board raised the policy rate by 0.25 point to 1.25% on a 7-2 vote, its second hike in three months, to curb price rises caused by high oil prices and a weak yen. One member said policy had entered "a new phase in which the size of rate hikes needs to be discussed." Kyodo read this as a reference to a 0.5-point move, twice the BOJ's usual step. Markets had already begun to price faster or larger hikes before the minutes came out.


Most members agreed that prices risk rising above the 2% target and confirmed that the Bank will keep raising rates. Another member said the September move brought the rate closer to the 中立金利 (chūritsu kinri, neutral rate), which makes 機動的 (kidōteki, nimble) decisions possible. The September hike had already cut the interval between moves from about six months to three. On October 30 the board held at 1.25% on an 8-1 vote. Hajime Takata proposed 1.5%, and the Outlook Report said underlying inflation is generally consistent with 2%.


The 利上げ幅 (riage haba, size of a rate hike) question changes how investors should read the December meeting. Most economists polled in late October expected a 0.25-point hike in December, which would take the rate to 1.5%. A member arguing for half-point steps in September means the board has discussed a faster route to neutral, and a 0.5-point hike would move the rate to 1.75% in one step. The yen gives the board a reason to consider it: the dollar traded near ¥158 last week, and Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama has repeated warnings about one-sided moves.

Investor takeaway: The minutes show that a half-point hike is part of the board's discussion, which raises the risk of a hawkish surprise in December for short-dated JGBs and supports the yen. The 0.25-point base case still holds, because only one member raised the idea and the October vote was 8-1 for a hold. The Summary of Opinions from the October meeting, expected next week, will show whether the size argument gained supporters after the Outlook Report declared inflation at target.
