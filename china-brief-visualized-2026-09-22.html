SPECIAL EDITION · US-CHINA
Before the Handshake: Washington and Beijing Set Up an AI "Incident Line" Ahead of the Trump-Xi Summit

Bessent and Vice Premier He Lifeng closed weekend talks in New York agreeing to a standing USA-China AI Dialogue — a notification channel for AI incidents that reach national-security concern, with a first working session slated for Shenzhen in about two months. The arrangement goes to Trump and Xi at their September 23–25 Washington summit, Xi's first visit to the US capital in over a decade.


The mechanism commits both sides to talk when something alarming happens. It does not touch model development, compute buildout, or the chip export and outbound-investment controls that remain unresolved on separate tracks — and Bessent framed primary policing responsibility as resting with companies, not governments.

Key signal: A notification channel is a lower bar than "AI safety talks" implied — it's communication infrastructure, not a governance regime, and leaves the harder export/investment constraints untouched.

STORY 1 · AI & TECH
$400 Million, No Model Shipped Yet: Tencent Backs a Stealth Tsinghua Startup at $1.42B

Tsinghua professor Dai Jifeng's seven-month-old Naive AI closed a third round, taking cumulative funding to $400M and valuation to $1.42B, led by Tencent. No product has shipped; a first open-weight model is due by month-end. The bet: modifying an existing open-weight Chinese model via post-training and RL, rather than pretraining from scratch, is materially cheaper — and investors are pricing that methodology before seeing a single benchmark score.


Risk that's specific to this model: depending on someone else's base model creates a licensing dependency that from-scratch labs like DeepSeek or Alibaba's Qwen team don't carry.

Key signal: The September model release is the first real test — it needs to show post-training competes with frontier open-weight releases, not just that it's cheaper to build.
PRETRAINING VS. POST-TRAINING: COST AND RISK TRADEOFF
SIGNAL · METHODOLOGY BET
后训练hòu xùnliàn · post-training
Cheaper than pretraining from scratch. Tencent's participation buys a second architecture bet without funding a full training run.
WATCH · SEPTEMBER RELEASE
开源权重kāiyuán quánzhòng · open weights
First model due by month-end. Needs to compete with Qwen/GLM on benchmarks, not just cost, to validate the valuation.
RISK · DEPENDENCY
授权风险shòuquán fēngxiǎn · licensing risk
Modifying another lab's open-weight model means Naive AI needs that base model to stay available and licensable — a risk from-scratch labs don't carry.
STORY 2 · AI & TECH
"Let Computing Power Reach the Physical World": Hygon Moves Its Server-Chip Line Into Robotics

Hygon, the STAR-listed chipmaker built on licensed AMD Zen architecture, launched a new CPU1000-series chip today in Shenzhen aimed at robotics, machine vision, and intelligent manufacturing — its first move beyond data-center CPUs since founding in 2014. The pivot follows D-Robotics, Cambricon and others extending from data-center silicon into embedded physical-AI compute, and addresses a structural problem: Hygon's 2016-licensed AMD baseline ages every year without a renewal or independent architecture.


No specs, pricing, or design partners disclosed — there's no way yet to benchmark this against D-Robotics' Sunrise line or Cambricon's embedded parts.

Key signal: Translating server-CPU design experience into competitive low-power edge silicon is a different engineering discipline — Hygon has no public track record here yet.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
$32.3 Billion and Rising: China's Stock Stamp Duty Take Jumps 82% as AI Trading Drives Turnover

China collected ¥216B ($32.3B) in stock stamp duty Jan-Aug 2026, up 82% YoY, as average daily trading value rose 72%. The chip- and AI-heavy STAR Market 50 Index is up 23% YTD while the broader CSI 300 has traded flat — a gap between flat headline returns and sharply higher turnover consistent with rotation into and out of AI-linked names rather than broad-based gains.


Beijing halved the stamp duty in 2023 specifically to stimulate trading; a sustained multiple of that year's volume now raises the opposite question about whether current activity is healthy liquidity or the kind of speculative concentration that has preceded intervention before. Nothing in the release signals a policy response.

Key signal: Stamp duty is a turnover tax, not a price indicator — the 82% jump says trading volume is up sharply, not that returns are.
