STORY 1 · AI & TECH
SoftBank Group Loses About ¥768 Billion in the Quarter on a US Data Center Writedown, and Raises a Record $56.5 Billion

ソフトバンクグループ (Sofutobanku Gurūpu, SoftBank Group) reported on November 10 a net loss of about ¥768 billion for July-September, according to its investor presentation, which took the April-September result to a loss of about ¥0.8 trillion against a ¥2.9 trillion profit a year earlier. Investment gains for the half fell by ¥2.2 trillion to ¥1.7 trillion. Two charges drove the quarter. The company booked a $3.7 billion 減損 (genson, impairment) on power-generation equipment bought for US AI data centers, which it tied to a review of how it procures power, and a ¥321.9 billion loss on prepaid forward contracts secured on Intel shares. The Vision Fund segment made a small gain of $534 million.


The financing figures are larger than the loss. The group raised a record $56.5 billion in the quarter: $39.9 billion from foreign and domestic bonds, senior term loans and loans secured on OpenAI shares, and $16.6 billion from margin loans on Arm shares and the Intel forwards. It repaid a $40 billion ブリッジローン (burijji rōn, bridge loan) six months before its March 2027 maturity, then completed $30 billion of follow-on investment in OpenAI in October, bringing its total to $64.6 billion for about 13% of the company. Loan-to-value stood at 15.9% at end-September against a 25% ceiling, and cash was ¥5.0 trillion, more than twice the bonds due in the next two years.


The writedown lands on the US part of the build-out, which the midterm results have made harder to permit. A day earlier, the CEO of SoftBank Corp said the SB Neo neocloud may start outside the US, and the group's slides list a 5-gigawatt plan in France and a $1.85 billion initial investment in Malaysia with Grab and PETROS alongside the 8-gigawatt Ohio campus leased to OpenAI for 20 years. 純資産価値 (jun shisan kachi, net asset value) was ¥59.8 trillion at September 30 but ¥44.7 trillion at November 9 prices, so the October-November tech selloff removed about ¥15 trillion of the asset base behind the 15.9% ratio. The company described its stance as balancing "gas and brake". Five-year default swaps were at 466 basis points before the results.

Investor takeaway: SoftBank funded the quarter by borrowing against its OpenAI, Arm and Intel stakes, and the 15.9% loan-to-value was measured when those stakes were worth more. At November 9 prices net asset value is ¥15 trillion lower, which raises the ratio on the same debt. Further falls in OpenAI or Arm valuations would push it toward the 25% ceiling and limit new borrowing for data centers.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Kokusai Electric Books a Record ¥167 Billion of Orders, Keeps Its Guide, and Jumps 14%

国際電気 (Kokusai Denki, Kokusai Electric), the Tokyo maker of batch 成膜装置 (seimaku sōchi, deposition equipment), reported on November 9 that July-September revenue rose 35.5% to a record ¥88.6 billion, about 6% above the ¥83.7 billion consensus. Adjusted operating profit rose 55.6% to ¥22.5 billion, a 25.3% margin, and adjusted net income rose 57.6% to ¥15.4 billion. 受注高 (juchū-daka, order intake) reached about ¥167 billion, a quarterly record and more than ¥20 billion above the forecast the company gave with first-quarter results. The CFO said he expects orders to stay at that level through the second half and beyond. The guide for the year to March 2027 stayed at ¥340 billion of revenue and ¥86 billion of adjusted operating profit, which already reaches the ¥330 billion medium-term revenue target two years early.


DRAM supplied 37% of quarterly revenue, logic and foundry 26%, service 20% and NAND 13%. China took about 35% of sales on the call's figures, and Chinese NAND equipment sales fell about 69% from a year earlier while DRAM and logic purchases there rose. Kokusai's furnaces and 原子層堆積 (genshisō taiseki, atomic layer deposition) tools coat many wafers at once, and the company holds about 49% of batch deposition and more than 80% of batch ALD. Its sales rise with layer counts in DRAM, 3D NAND and gate-all-around logic. Management expects more than ¥20 billion of 2nm gate-all-around tool sales this year, and forecasts wafer fab equipment spending to rise more than 25% to about $140 billion in 2026 and more than 30% to about $190 billion in 2027.


The stock rose 14.4% on November 10, when the Nikkei gained 1,684 points to retake 60,000, after falling about 28% the week before. The order figure drove the move, since adjusted EPS of ¥61.44 missed the ¥67.86 consensus. Keeping the annual guide with a record order book shifts the revenue into the year to March 2028, where management targets about 40% growth and an operating margin above 30%. The ¥20 billion demo center it is building in Oregon puts its tools next to US memory and logic customers as Chinese NAND orders fall.

Investor takeaway: A ¥167 billion order quarter against ¥88.6 billion of sales gives a book-to-bill near 1.9 and fills much of next year's revenue. Management kept this year's guide, so any upgrade would come in the year to March 2028. The main risk is deeper cuts by Chinese customers, who still take about a third of sales.

STORY 3 · MACRO
Japan's September Current Account Surplus Falls 46% to ¥2.37 Trillion as Overseas Dividends Drop and Oil Pushes Goods Into Deficit

The 財務省 (Zaimushō, Ministry of Finance) released balance of payments data on November 10 showing a September 経常収支 (keijō shūshi, current account) surplus of ¥2.372 trillion, down from ¥4.376 trillion a year earlier and below the ¥3.354 trillion consensus. It was the third straight monthly surplus and followed ¥4.06 trillion in August. For April-September the surplus was ¥17.15 trillion, 0.4% below the record ¥17.22 trillion of a year earlier.


The 第一次所得収支 (dai-ichiji shotoku shūshi, primary income balance), which records dividends and interest earned abroad, fell 27.1% to ¥3.55 trillion because overseas subsidiaries paid less in dividends to their Japanese parents. Goods trade swung to a ¥187.5 billion deficit from a ¥247 billion surplus. Exports rose 12.8% to ¥10.73 trillion on AI-related electronics, but imports rose 17.8% to ¥10.92 trillion. Crude import prices in the half averaged ¥108,622 a kiloliter, up 60.3%, or about $108 a barrel, as Middle East tensions lifted oil. The サービス収支 (sābisu shūshi, services balance) deficit widened to ¥457 billion from ¥254 billion in September, even though inbound tourism produced a record ¥3.25 trillion travel surplus for the half.


Japan's surplus comes almost entirely from investment income, and companies keep much of that income abroad. BOJ Governor Kazuo Ueda made that point to the IMF before this release, and September shows how fast the surplus shrinks when subsidiaries send less home. Lower dividend repatriation and a goods deficit both cut the foreign currency that Japanese companies convert into yen. That comes after the Finance Ministry spent ¥27.13 trillion this year defending the currency, with the dollar still near ¥158. With crude averaging about $108, the goods balance is likely to stay weak, so the yen depends more on whether the BOJ raises rates in December.

Investor takeaway: Primary income fell by ¥1.3 trillion in one month, three times the ¥435 billion swing in goods, so lower corporate dividends caused most of the drop. If repatriation stays low into year-end, the yen loses a source of buying just as reserves sit $230 billion below February's level.
