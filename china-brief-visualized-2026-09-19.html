STORY 1 · AI & TECH
89% Cheaper, No Open Weights: Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash Doubles Down on Agentic Video

Alibaba's Qwen team released Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash on Sept 18 — its first model built around agentic multimodal workflows, combining native audio-video understanding, reasoning and tool use with a 1M-token context window. Unlike much of Alibaba's 2026 lineup, it carries no open weights and ships API-only.


Alibaba's own numbers claim a 26%+ average benchmark gain over Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus and an ~89% cut to video-input cost, with audiovisual performance said to approach Gemini 3.8 Flash — self-published figures that, per Alibaba's own recent release pattern, have not yet been independently verified.

Key signal: The API-only, agentic-first packaging — after a run of open-weight releases — signals Alibaba is testing whether hosted agentic access monetizes better than the open downloads that built Qwen's adoption lead.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH / GOVERNANCE
Five Days Before Trump-Xi: Beijing Calls Anthropic's Amodei "Fearmongering" Over Chip Controls

Amodei's Sept 12 essay called for a coordinated AI slowdown, continued chip export restrictions on China, and a crackdown on distillation — and drew fast public alignment from OpenAI, xAI and Google DeepMind. Two days later, MOFA spokesperson Guo Jiakun rebuked the essay directly at a Beijing briefing.


What's new isn't the substance — both sides have held these positions for months — it's the level: China's foreign ministry, not a company or state-media commentary, is now the one answering a US lab CEO, five days ahead of the Trump-Xi meeting.

Key signal: Watch whether the US-lab consensus behind Amodei's essay shows up in a harder US negotiating line at the summit, or stays rhetoric — that's the read-through for chip-adjacent names.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
One Qiushi Essay: PBOC's Pan Gongsheng Declares China's Credit-Growth Era Over

In a Sept 16 Qiushi article, Governor Pan Gongsheng framed China's slowing credit growth as structural — shrinking property and LGFV demand outpacing what new industries can absorb — rather than a problem requiring stimulus, days after August loan data badly missed forecasts.


The article previews a shift toward price-based regulation over loan-volume targets, and a crackdown on "involution-style" competition among lenders — consistent with bond/equity financing now exceeding bank loans as a share of total social financing for the first time this cycle.

Key signal: The framing pre-commits the PBOC away from a stimulus-driven credit rebound into year-end — a data point against expectations of an imminent RRR cut resolving the August lending miss.
