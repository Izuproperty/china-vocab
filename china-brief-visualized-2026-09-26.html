STORY 01 · AI & TECH
$1 Billion Run Rate, Built on a Price Hike: DeepSeek's Numbers Land Days Into a CAC Probe

DeepSeek's annualized revenue run rate doubled to $1 billion, per CEO Liang Wenfeng, helped by an API price restructuring that raised effective prices 2.3–4.5x without shrinking the customer base, he says. The disclosure lands as the company finalizes a ~$7.4B round and preps a Shanghai STAR Market IPO targeting a $68.8B valuation.


Liang separately called Huawei Ascend training chips "one of the company's biggest bets," with Q4 2026 deliveries expected — a supply dependency that matters more once DeepSeek answers to public shareholders. The revenue story also lands the same week China's Cyberspace Administration opened a data-security probe into the company.

Key signal: Revenue growth driven substantially by pricing rather than disclosed usage growth, with an unresolved chip-supply dependency and an active regulatory probe both landing in the same week as the IPO prep.

STORY 02 · AI & TECH
7,000 Environments, One Training Run: Xiaomi Open-Sources Its Bet on Reinforcement Learning

Xiaomi open-sourced MiMo-V2.6-Pro-RL (1.02T total / 42B active params) and Flash-RL (309B / 15B), plus 7,000+ RL environments and the training pipeline behind them, trained in a single mixed run across coding, agentic, vision, and cybersecurity tasks rather than domain-by-domain.


Xiaomi disclosed training costs directly — $2.62M for Pro, $850K for Flash, under six days. Its own benchmark puts Pro at 46 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index versus Kimi K3's 44 and GLM-5.3's 45, though closed frontier models still score around 53.

Key signal: The disclosed sub-$3M training cost for a trillion-parameter-class model says more about falling domestic RL infrastructure costs than the self-reported benchmark ranking does.

STORY 03 · MACRO
¥229.4 Billion in a Week: Local Bond Issuance Outpaces China's Own Spending

Local government special bond issuance hit RMB 229.4 billion for the week of September 21–27, up RMB 126.1 billion week-on-week; September's RMB 438.1 billion total runs at 104% of plan, with 2026 cumulative issuance progress at 76.5%. Construction-earmarked proceeds rose to 47.9% of the total.


The pickup follows an NDRC pledge to accelerate policy-based financing tools, but it sits next to an earlier data point showing August fiscal spending fell faster than July's. Issuance is accelerating while disbursement appears to be slowing — a gap that could resolve either as normal lag or as a project-pipeline shortfall.

Key signal: October spending data measured against September's issuance pace will show whether this is a timing lag or a genuine execution problem for Q4 infrastructure support.
