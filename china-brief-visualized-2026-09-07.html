STORY 1 · AI & TECH · CHIPS
874% Revenue Growth, Two Generations Behind: CXMT Closes In on Micron's Margins

CXMT's H1 2026 revenue rose 874% YoY to ¥150.3B, with net margin recovering to 51.6% from negative a year prior. The report came a month after an IPO that briefly valued the company near $487B; S&P now estimates market cap at $582.5B, putting CXMT above Tencent by some measures.


The growth is concentrated in lower-end DRAM — WSJ reports CXMT trails the DRAM leaders by two to three technology generations, so its entry into the higher-margin AI-driven HBM market (targeted for end-2026) is a catalyst, not yet a fact.

Key signal: Watch for CXMT's first HBM yield data before crediting the stock's valuation with an AI-memory growth story that hasn't shipped yet.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · ROBOTICS
1,300x Earnings, No ChatGPT Moment Yet: What Unitree's IPO Swings Say About China's Robot Bet

Unitree's IPO-day P/E hit ~1,300x against a ~124x STAR Market average before the stock gave back roughly $30B in value, stabilizing ~45% below peak by Sept 1. Its own prospectus shows only ~9% of humanoid revenue comes from industrial customers — the rest from subsidized research buyers.


The industry's own "ChatGPT moment" benchmark — 80% task completion in unfamiliar settings from a voice command — is estimated by Unitree's own CEO at 2 to 10 years out, a range wide enough to span most institutional holding periods.

Key signal: Production share (>90% global) is not the same evidence as commercial demand (~9% of Unitree's own revenue) — track which one the market is actually pricing.

STORY 3 · MACRO · TRADE
Sanctioned for Compliance: Chinese Rare Earth Suppliers Quietly Halt US Shipments Ahead of Xi's Washington Trip

Since early August, a handful of Chinese rare earth suppliers have stopped shipping to the US — not under a new export-control order, but out of fear that complying with the RBA's due-diligence standard (which Beijing sanctioned) would draw punishment. Yttrium exports to the US remain at roughly half 2024 levels.


The standoff is now on the US agenda ahead of Xi's Sept 24 Washington visit, tied to prior Busan-agreement commitments — making the summit outcome a more direct catalyst than the underlying export data itself.

Key signal: This is a compliance-risk story, not a tariff story — Chinese suppliers are now pricing regulatory risk into customer selection, independent of official export-control lists.
