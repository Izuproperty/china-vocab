STORY 1 · SEMICONDUCTORS
11.95 Nanometers, No EUV: CXMT Starts Mass-Producing Its Most Advanced Memory Chip

CXMT announced Sept 20 that its G5 DRAM platform has entered mass production, using self-aligned quadruple patterning to reach an 11.95nm half-pitch without EUV tools. Two 24Gb LPDDR5X parts are already qualified in mainstream Chinese smartphones — 50% denser than CXMT's existing 16Gb line, with over 50% more dies per wafer than G4.


The gain lands in mobile memory, not the HBM segment that determines AI-accelerator competitiveness; CXMT's own roadmap still puts HBM production at end-2026. A July round of independent testing on the prior generation found voltage-scaling and batch-consistency issues that haven't been retested against G5.

Key signal: G5 extends CXMT's commodity-DRAM profitability run into mobile parts — the HBM production timeline, not this announcement, is what would extend that into AI-linked memory.

STORY 2 · ROBOTICS CHIPS & TRADE POLICY
$400 Million for the Robot Chips the FCC Just Blacklisted

D-Robotics, the robotics-chip spinout of Horizon Robotics, closed a $400M Series C led by Mirae Asset, with Meituan and Hefei/Shenzhen state-backed funds participating — bringing total funding to ~$770M. Its Sunrise chips have shipped 8M+ units; the S600 landed 20+ customers within six months.


The round closed weeks after the FCC placed Sunrise-powered humanoid and service robots on its Covered List, restricting US market access. Investors backing the round anyway suggests the growth case rests on domestic and non-US-aligned demand.

Key signal: A Tier 2 robotics-chip supplier raised its largest round yet specifically after losing US market access — a bet on China's own robotics buildout, not exports.

STORY 3 · FISCAL POLICY
Spending Fell Faster in August Than July: China's Fiscal Pullback Deepens

MOF data released Sept 18 showed broad budget spending fell 6.7% y/y in August, steeper than July's 4.4% drop, even as revenue rose 2.9%. The 8-month deficit narrowed to ¥5.5T ($821B). Bond sales added ~¥1T to August financing, but governments have used only 68% of the annual quota through August, vs. 76% a year earlier.


Officials are speeding deployment of existing budget allocations — an ¥800B infrastructure program started disbursing in August — rather than announcing new stimulus, even as spending itself keeps contracting.

Key signal: Bond issuance is expected to accelerate into year-end, but actual spending contracted further in August — which of the two signals holds is the thing to watch into Q4.
