STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Japan's Digital Agency Locks In AI Governance Rules as 180,000 Bureaucrats Get GENAI Access

Version 2.0 of the Digital Agency's generative-AI procurement guidelines entered into force Sept 1, six weeks after council approval. The update mandates a Chief AI Officer at every ministry and a structured high-risk review process reported to the Advanced AI Utilization Advisory Board — governance infrastructure layered onto a FY2026 pilot extending the Gennai platform to roughly 180,000 employees, with full rollout targeted FY2027.

The scale makes this one of the largest sovereign generative-AI deployments anywhere, and the audit-trail/explainability requirements echo the FSA's parallel financial-sector AI mandate — a sign Tokyo is converging on one governance template across sectors rather than writing bespoke rules per regulator.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Tokyo Signals AI and Chips Will Anchor the Next Slice of Its $550 Billion US Investment Pact

Trade Minister Akazawa told reporters in Washington Sept 4 that AI and semiconductors will carry "very significant weight" in the pact's next funding round. Only $109bn of the $550bn total has been committed after more than a year — $36bn to US oil/gas/mineral projects, $73bn to a nuclear-and-gas buildout — with zero so far directed to AI or chips.

Equity covers just 1–2% of committed capital; the rest runs through loans and guarantees via Japan's state-backed lenders. An eventual AI/chip tranche would likely finance US-based capacity rather than hand Japanese firms direct stakes — a different exposure than METI's domestic Rapidus/Kioxia subsidy programs.

STORY 3 · MACRO & FINANCE
Berkshire's Greg Abel Says Its Japan Trading House Bet Is a "Many Decades" Position

On his first Tokyo trip as CEO, Greg Abel told CNBC Berkshire's stakes in Mitsubishi, Mitsui, Itochu, Sumitomo, and Marubeni are a position it intends to hold "for many decades." All five stakes now exceed 10%, with Sumitomo and Marubeni crossing that line as recently as May. The combined position's value has risen from $35.4bn at end-2025 to roughly $42bn by early September.

Abel flagged expectations for continued dividend growth and buybacks, and said current JGB-yield pressure isn't a near-term constraint on the trading houses — a framing worth stress-testing given Berkshire's own incentive to talk its book heading into a September BOJ decision that's rattling other rate-sensitive Nikkei names.
