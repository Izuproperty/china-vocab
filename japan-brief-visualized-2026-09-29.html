STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Fujitsu Packages Cyber Defense for Infrastructure Operators as Japan's Neutralization Powers Start Thursday

Fujitsu launched a service on September 25 that combines dark-web monitoring using KELA intelligence, continuous mapping of internet-facing systems, and threat hunting by its red-team consultants. It will feed into Fujitsu's AI-based Cyber Security Nerve Center, which opens in October.


On October 1 the active cyber defense law lets police and the SDF access and disable attack servers. Reporting and information-sharing duties for core infrastructure operators phase in through November 2027, and those operators need outside help to detect compromises quickly.

Investor takeaway: Regulation is turning security monitoring into a recurring compliance purchase for infrastructure operators; Fujitsu's late-October results will show whether security orders are big enough to break out.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Sakai Chemical's Capacitor Powder Is Running Short as AI Servers Soak Up MLCCs

Bloomberg reported on September 27 that Sakai Chemical's capacity for barium titanate, the dielectric powder in multilayer ceramic capacitors, is filling quickly on AI data center demand. Its hydrothermal process makes the fine particles needed for thin, high-capacitance layers.


AI servers use far more MLCCs per rack than conventional servers. Peer Nippon Chemical Industrial plans a 1.5x capacity increase for fiscal 2027; Sakai has not announced one yet.

Investor takeaway: Tight powder supply gives Sakai room to raise prices or lock in contracts with capacitor makers; a capacity or pricing announcement is what would move segment earnings.

STORY 3 · MACRO & FISCAL
A Takaichi Adviser Puts a 10-to-15-Year Clock on a 3.5% Defense Budget

Takuji Aida, a member of Takaichi's growth panel, said on September 28 that lifting defense spending toward 3.5% of GDP would be a 10-to-15-year goal and that dual-use programs in the ¥370 trillion investment plan could count toward it. He expects the next BOJ hike in January.


At nominal GDP near ¥630 trillion, the gap from 2% to 3.5% is roughly ¥9 to 10 trillion a year at the end state, more than the whole ¥8.9 trillion FY2027 request. Phased over 10 to 15 years, each year adds ¥0.6 to 1 trillion.

Investor takeaway: A long timeline shrinks the annual step, but JGB supply depends on whether the year-end security documents fund it with taxes or bonds.
