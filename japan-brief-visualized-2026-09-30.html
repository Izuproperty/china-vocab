STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Hitachi Signs Up Agile Robots to Build the Brain for Its Physical AI Business

Hitachi and Munich-based Agile Robots signed a basic agreement on September 29 to co-develop AI models that let industrial robots handle complex tasks on their own. Hitachi supplies a model that learns skilled workers' tacit knowledge and an in-house edge AI chip for vision and touch data. Agile Robots supplies AgileCore, its arms, mobile robots and Agile ONE humanoid, and a model trained across 20,000+ installations.


The output will be sold globally inside HMAX Industry. Hitachi gets general-purpose robot hardware without building it, and its revenue comes from the software and maintenance contracts attached to each deployment.

Investor takeaway: Licensing a proven robot stack speeds Hitachi's physical AI launch and ties HMAX Industry growth to recurring software and service contracts; late-October results are the first place order figures could appear.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
SoftBank's SAIMEMORY Brings In Shinko Electric to Package Its HBM Challenger

SAIMEMORY signed a basic agreement on September 28 with Shinko Electric Industries to develop packaging and manufacturing processes for Z-Angle Memory. ZAM stands DRAM dies on edge side by side, while HBM stacks them flat and hits heat limits at about 20 layers.


Shinko, taken private in 2025 by a JIC-led consortium, makes flip-chip substrates for high-end processors. Its packaging work decides whether upright dies with wireless links can be built in volume. Prototypes are due in fiscal 2027 and practical use in fiscal 2029.

Investor takeaway: Shinko covers ZAM's hardest manufacturing step, but a fiscal 2029 target keeps this a long-dated option for SoftBank and Japan's memory ambitions; the fiscal 2027 prototype is the first checkable milestone.

STORY 3 · MACRO & BONDS
Japan's 40-Year Bond Sale Draws Its Strongest Bids Since 2020 at a 4.23% Yield

The MoF's September 29 sale of 40-year JGBs drew a bid-to-cover of 3.1, against 2.82 in July and a 2.67 average over 12 months. The yield settled near 4.23%, and JGB futures rose after the result.


Life insurers can match long liabilities above 4%, so they bought despite a global bond selloff, a BOJ that hiked to 1.25% on September 18, talk of another hike in October, and an unfunded tax cut going to the Diet on October 5.

Investor takeaway: Real-money buyers are returning to the super-long end above 4%, which steadies the part of the curve that led September's selloff; today's 2-year auction and Thursday's Tankan will show how hard hike bets push the front end.
