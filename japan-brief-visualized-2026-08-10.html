STORY 1 · AI & TECH ITmedia AI+ / Yahoo News Japan
Japan's justice ministry rules AI voice cloning can infringe existing "publicity rights" — no new law needed

The Ministry of Justice's August 7 report — the output of a five-session expert study group running since April — holds that publicity rights (パブリシティー権) and personality-rights protections already extend to voice, even without a dedicated statute. Posting an unauthorized AI-cloned "cover" of a singer's or voice actor's voice for profit can now be argued as a rights violation.

Ordinary human impressions and mimicry remain exempt. The guidance follows a string of voice-actor complaints since 2023, including voice actor Kenjiro Tsuda's November 2025 lawsuit over an unauthorized AI-cloned video of his voice.

Investor read-through: Compliance cost, not existential risk — expect voice-synthesis vendors and UGC platforms operating in Japan to tighten consent/takedown workflows as this feeds into the AI Promotion Act's evolving liability architecture.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTOR BigGo Finance / Nikkei
SUMCO swings to an operating loss on record wafer demand — a preview of Japan's AI-capex margin math

SUMCO's April–June results captured a paradox: 300mm wafer shipments for AI-linked logic and memory hit record levels, revenue rose 4.7% to ¥215.0bn, yet the company posted an operating loss of ¥6.4bn versus a ¥7.5bn profit a year earlier. Depreciation from new-plant ramp-up rose ~¥11.6bn YoY to ¥64.4bn, outrunning near-full utilization.

President Jiro Tatsuta said "5–10% price hikes won't come close to covering costs," declined to open long-term supply agreement renewals, and confirmed the Yoshino-Kari greenfield plant remains shelved indefinitely.

Investor read-through: Strong AI demand isn't automatically translating into margin at the materials layer — echoes Fanuc's muted guidance raise despite a 37% order surge. Pricing power, not volume, is the variable to watch.

STORY 3 · MACRO & RATES Bank of Japan / Bloomberg
BOJ's July summary shows a board leaning toward a September or October hike — but still divided on timing

The Summary of Opinions from the July 30–31 meeting, published August 10, shows the Board held at 1.00% by an 8–1 vote (Takata dissenting for 1.25%). Several members cited real rates near 25-year lows as room to hike further; others wanted more data. One opinion explicitly favored timely hikes to avoid rapid tightening later.

This lands alongside Ueda's "as soon as September" framing, the Aug 3 Outlook Report's upside inflation risk language, and a sixth straight month of real wage gains reported Aug 4–5.

Investor read-through: Whether the BOJ hikes again is broadly priced — the live question is September vs. October. July CPI (Aug 21) and August CPI (Sep 18) are the hard checkpoints before the Sep 18–19 meeting.
