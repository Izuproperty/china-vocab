STORY 1 · ROBOTICS / CAPITAL MARKETS
8,289 Times Oversubscribed: Unitree's IPO Prices Robots at 219x Earnings

Final allocation results for Unitree's STAR Market listing show the retail tranche oversubscribed ~8,289x, with a final winning rate of 0.0181%. Priced Aug 6 at ¥150.80/share (~¥61B / $9.0B valuation), the deal implies a trailing P/E near 219x — 5-6x the sector average. Trading is expected to open the week of Aug 17-21.


Unitree's own prospectus discloses current humanoids can't yet reliably perform complex, unstructured tasks; H1 2026 revenue growth decelerated from 300%+ to roughly 35-45% y/y, with adjusted profit down double digits. The valuation prices in years of flawless execution on an unproven roadmap.

Key signal: Retail demand is a sentiment gauge, not a commercial-reality check — watch the stock once the lock-up period ends and scarcity pricing fades.

STORY 2 · AI / OPEN-SOURCE MODELS
20 US Firms, One Letter: Meta and Nvidia Answer China's Open-Weight Lead

Meta released Muse Glimmer (weights for Muse Spark 1.2 pending) and Nvidia followed with Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, bundled into a 20+ company US letter opposing "premature restrictions" on open-weight AI — a direct response to Chinese labs' dominance of the category.


Nine of the top 10 text-to-video systems on Artificial Analysis' leaderboard are Chinese. Hugging Face's CEO says China could lead open-weight AI, or the frontier outright, by year-end. OpenAI and Anthropic — both closed-model revenue models — did not sign the letter.

Key signal: The coalition's composition (chipmakers, clouds, infra) suggests this is an ecosystem play as much as a capability race — watch adoption, not just release cadence.

STORY 3 · MACRO / ENERGY
1.02 Trillion Kilowatt-Hours: China's July Power Use Sets a Global First

NEA data show July electricity consumption of 1.02 trillion kWh, +8.6% y/y — the first time any country's monthly usage has topped 1 trillion kWh. Secondary industry (manufacturing) rose 4.7% y/y to 593.6B kWh; primary industry (agriculture) rose 20.2% y/y off a small base.


State media cites heatwaves, resilient industrial output, and rising compute/EV-charging load. The heatwave component is not durable; the compute and EV-charging components are structural and worth tracking as they compress toward autumn.

Key signal: A coincident activity indicator that's harder to manage than GDP — but decompose the July spike before extrapolating it forward.
