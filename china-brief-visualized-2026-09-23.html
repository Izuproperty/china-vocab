SPECIAL EDITION · US-CHINA SUMMIT
First White House Visit in Eleven Years: Xi Arrives as Rare Earths Become Beijing's Strongest Card

Xi Jinping landed in Washington September 23 for a three-day state visit ahead of Thursday's summit with Trump — his first trip to the US capital in eleven years. The agenda spans AI, tariffs, critical minerals, Iran, and Taiwan. The November 10 tariff-truce deadline remains unresolved: USTR Greer said Washington could support a three-to-six-month extension but "we aren't going to have the extension today," citing insufficient rare-earth export flows from China as the sticking point.


China controls the large majority of global rare-earth processing capacity, and Washington has so far avoided steps that risk prompting Beijing to tighten the flow further. Separately, both sides have floated reciprocal tariff cuts on roughly $30B of trade under the existing "Board of Trade" framework.

Key signal: A truce extension alone, without a rare-earth or tariff resolution, pushes the deadline into a new window rather than removing the underlying uncertainty.

STORY 1 · AI & TECH
500,000-Chip Clusters, Three Times the Speed: Alibaba Unveils Its Most Powerful AI Chip and a 10-Trillion-Parameter Qwen Plan

At its Apsara Conference, Alibaba's T-Head unit unveiled the Zhenwu V900 accelerator — 3x the performance of predecessor Zhenwu M890, deployable in clusters up to 500,000 processors, mass production targeted Q1 2027. Alongside it, Alibaba said Qwen4 is already training, with Qwen4.5/Qwen5 planned at 5-10 trillion parameters versus Qwen3.8-Max's current 2.4 trillion.


Both claims are self-reported. V900 specs beyond the performance multiple — power draw, process node, yield — are undisclosed, and no Qwen benchmark this year has had independent verification.

Key signal: The announcement establishes intent, not delivery — Q1 2027 production and whether a 10T-parameter model is trainable on domestic compute alone are both still open questions.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH
6.6% in a Day: Tencent's New Image Model Claims Parity With ByteDance's Seedream

Tencent's Hunyuan lab released Hy-Image-3.5-Preview the same day Alibaba opened Apsara — 4K generation, multi-turn editing, and internal-test claims of matching ByteDance's Seedream 5.0 Pro while edging out Google's Nano Banana Pro and Alibaba's Qwen-Image-3.0 Pro. Shares rose as much as 7.8% intraday, closing +6.6% at HK$458.4.


The comparisons are self-administered — no independent benchmark confirms parity with Seedream rather than a narrower approach to it, and Tencent hasn't disclosed its evaluation methodology beyond "internal designer testing."

Key signal: A single-day mid-single-digit move on a preview release, with no GA or third-party benchmark, says more about how the market prices Chinese AI announcements than about the model's real standing.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
629% Up, Then 55% Down: Beijing Quietly Slows the Humanoid-Robot IPO Pipeline

Chinese regulators are using informal "window guidance" to hold back new humanoid-robotics listings, scrutinizing whether valuations and revenue reflect genuine commercial demand versus state- and university-linked purchases. The trigger: Unitree Robotics' own STAR Market debut, which spiked 629% intraday, closed +460% day one, and has since fallen ~55% from that peak.


The caution looks sector-specific — mainland IPO issuance overall is up 59% YoY to $148.9B in 2026 — rather than a broad capital-markets pullback.

Key signal: Whether informal guidance becomes published listing standards, versus a pause that lifts once markets absorb the volatility, isn't confirmed yet — that's the thing to watch.
