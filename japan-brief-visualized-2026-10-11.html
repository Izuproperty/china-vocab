STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Tokyo Tells Companies to Assume They Will Be Hacked After 20 Million Customer Records Leak in Two Weeks

国家サイバー統括室 (Kokka Saibā Tōkatsushitsu, National Cybersecurity Office), part of the Cabinet Secretariat, issued a notice on October 9 asking companies that hold large volumes of personal data to harden their systems and to work on the premise that they "could be attacked at any time." The internal affairs ministry, METI, the Information-technology Promotion Agency and 金融庁 (Kin'yūchō, FSA) issued their own alerts the same day. The trigger is a run of 不正アクセス (fusei akusesu, unauthorized access) cases since late September. 物語コーポレーション (Monogatari Kōporēshon, Monogatari Corp.), which runs the Yakiniku King restaurant chain, said on October 5 that about 10.79 million app member records had leaked. ブックオフ (Bukkuofu, BOOKOFF Group) disclosed up to 6.43 million on October 9, and car-sharing operator Times Mobility reported up to about 6.6 million at the start of the month. Daiwa Securities said about 110,000 customer records were exposed through an inquiry server run by a contractor.


The notice is specific. It tells firms to patch internet-facing systems and content management software promptly, retire unsupported products, require 多要素認証 (tayōso ninshō, multi-factor authentication), encrypt stored data, stop keeping passwords in plain text, check whether cloud storage is publicly exposed, and write breach-reporting duties into contracts with every 委託先 (itakusaki, contractor). It also calls security a management issue that boards must fund. Responders have traced several intrusions to a SQL injection flaw in Metabase, an open-source analytics tool, and others to API keys pulled out of public smartphone apps. A joint document from outside security researchers found no log evidence that attackers used AI. It added that the scale of the probing makes automated or agent-driven scanning hard to rule out: one observer logged 326 IP addresses making more than 1,200 requests in about 40 minutes. The government notice lists AI misuse among the reasons attacks are getting harder to stop.

Investor takeaway: Japan's response so far is guidance, so the near-term effect is unplanned security spending at retailers, brokers and platforms with large app memberships. More disclosures are likely as firms audit their systems, which favors security vendors and integrators over consumer names that hold member data. Watch whether the PPC or FSA convert the requests into inspection items.

STORY 2 · SEMI & INDUSTRIAL
Machine Tool Orders Hit a Record ¥223 Billion in September as AI Hardware Makers Order Ahead

日本工作機械工業会 (Nihon Kōsaku Kikai Kōgyōkai, Japan Machine Tool Builders' Association, JMTBA) said on October 9 that 工作機械受注 (kōsaku kikai juchū, machine tool orders) rose 60.4% from a year earlier to ¥223.2 billion in September, the highest for any single month. Orders rose 12.8% from August and have now grown year on year for 15 straight months. 外需 (gaiju, foreign demand) rose 66.8% to ¥159.3 billion, also a record, after August's ¥146.4 billion had broken the June high. 内需 (naiju, domestic demand) rose 46.5% to ¥63.9 billion and jumped 24.1% on the month. Orders for January through September total ¥1.67 trillion, up 43.3%.


Machine tools cut and shape metal parts for other factories, so orders show where manufacturers plan to add capacity six to twelve months ahead. The association credits AI and robotics. AI server racks need machined cold plates, enclosures and connectors, chip equipment makers need precision frames and vacuum parts, and data center power gear needs large machined housings. In August, North American orders rose 71.8% from a year earlier, the fastest of the three regions, and domestic orders from electrical and precision machinery makers rose 18.4% on the month while automotive orders fell 4.9%. Nikkei reported this month that the industry's 受注残 (juchūzan, order backlog) exceeds ¥1 trillion and that 芝浦機械 (Shibaura Kikai, Shibaura Machine) plans to raise output by half to work through it.

Investor takeaway: A record month and a backlog above ¥1 trillion give machine tool makers revenue visibility into 2027, so results now depend on how fast they can ship. The weak yen inflates the foreign figures. A pullback in AI capex would show up first in North American orders, so watch that line in the October data due in mid-November.

STORY 3 · MACRO
Week Ahead: Producer Prices, a Household Inflation Survey and Koeda in Kumamoto Before the BOJ's October Meeting

Tokyo markets are closed on Monday for スポーツの日 (Supōtsu no Hi, Sports Day), so the week's data comes in three days. On Tuesday at 8:50 JST, 日本銀行 (Nihon Ginkō, Bank of Japan) publishes the September 企業物価指数 (kigyō bukka shisū, Corporate Goods Price Index). The August index rose 7.6% from a year earlier, its third straight month above 7%, as Middle East supply disruption lifted petroleum and coal products 14.7% and nonferrous metals 43.3%. The 輸入物価 (yunyū bukka, import price) index rose 24.8% in yen terms and 16.7% in contract currencies, so roughly a third of imported inflation came from the weak yen. Economists expect September at 7.6%. At 13:30 the same day the BOJ releases its quarterly Opinion Survey on the General Public's Views and Behavior, which shows how much inflation households expect.


Wednesday brings a 5-year JGB auction; the last one cleared at 2.239%. On Thursday at 8:50 the Cabinet Office releases August 機械受注 (kikai juchū, core machinery orders), where economists expect a 2.5% monthly rebound after July's 3.7% drop. That series tracks domestic capex plans and can be checked against the record machine tool orders above. At 10:30 the same morning, board member 小枝淳子 (Koeda Junko) speaks to local leaders in 熊本 (Kumamoto), where TSMC's JASM runs its first fab. It is the only board speech on the BOJ's published schedule this week. Governor Ueda told the National Securities Convention on October 6 that the bank will keep raising rates, but he did little to challenge market pricing of roughly a one-in-four chance of a move from 1.25% at the October 29-30 meeting.

Investor takeaway: Tuesday's producer prices and household survey are the week's main inputs for an October 30 decision that markets still price as unlikely. Producer inflation above 7% and rising household expectations would lift hike odds and support the yen. Koeda's Kumamoto speech is the only scheduled board guidance this week.
