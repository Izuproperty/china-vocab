STORY 1 · AI & TECH
DeepSeek Ships V4.1-Flash-1017 Under the MIT Licence, Claims 77.0 on DeepSWE at About 20 Cents a Task, and Opens Its Own Search API

DeepSeek moved its app and API to V4.1-Flash-1017 on October 17 and posted MIT-licensed weights. Price and architecture are unchanged: 552B backbone, 8B/16B active, 1M context.


It claims 77.0 on its own DeepSWE benchmark at about $0.20 a task, against 74.0 for Claude Opus 5, while still trailing Opus on Terminal-Bench and HLE. A paid Search and Fetch API starts billing on November 1.

Key signal: DeepSeek is competing on cost per finished agent task and adding paid search, but its benchmark lead is self-reported and a ranking-based curb on open weights would hit exactly this kind of release.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · ROBOTICS
Ex-Huawei Driving Scientist's Gewu Tongzhi Raises Nearly ¥100 Million From Hangzhou State Funds to Give Factory Robots a Sense of Touch

Gewu Tongzhi, founded this year by former Huawei ADS scientist Cai Jia, raised nearly ¥100M from Hangzhou Capital, Hangzhou Talent Group, a provincial embodied-AI fund, Peak and others.


Its model infers contact and force from video, calibrated on real tactile data, to give robots "hand feel" for fabric, optical modules and auto parts. First proof-of-concept delivery is due by end-2026.

Key signal: Local state funds keep seeding new embodied-AI teams outside the top 20 despite bubble warnings, and Gewu's video-to-touch approach would cut manipulation data costs if it works, but it has no benchmark, valuation or delivered project yet.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & POLICY · WEEK AHEAD
Week Ahead: The Financial Street Forum Opens Today With Wu Qing and Pan Gongsheng, and Regulators Promise a Policy Batch Days After Visa's Clearing Approval

The Financial Street Forum runs October 19–22. Wu Qing keynotes today, and regulators said a batch of reform policies would come during the forum, without naming any.


Equities fell hard in the week to October 16 while ETFs took ¥126.9B of inflows. Investors want stabilization signals from Wu, and market-access watchers want Connect and licensing steps after Visa's October 12 approval.

Key signal: Wu Qing's keynote sets the tone for equities after a week of state-backed ETF buying, while any new licences or Connect measures in the policy batch matter most for financial services firms building China business.
