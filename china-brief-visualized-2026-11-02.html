STORY 1 · AI & TECH · CODING AGENTS
DeepSeek Open-Sources a "Super-agent" Prototype That Watches GitHub and Feishu Chats and Starts Fixing Bugs Without Being Asked

DeepSeek's Harness team posted the design on October 31 and put the code on GitHub under MIT (about 3,300 stars by Sunday). A Router watches GitHub, Feishu chats and email and dispatches parallel Workers that reproduce bugs and try fixes, with a web panel for items that need a person.


DeepSeek argues one engineer can follow only three to five sessions, so the bottleneck is human attention. It calls the code a research prototype, not a product, and published no benchmarks or cost data. The release lands as its pre-IPO round reportedly reaches ¥105.5 billion ($15.9 billion).

Key signal: An always-on agent burns far more tokens than chat, which feeds DeepSeek's API revenue, but a tool that reads team chats runs into the open CAC data probe and the US federal ban, so expect adoption mainly inside China and watch for a priced Harness tier.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · EMBODIED AI
Wujie Dynamics Signs a ¥1 Billion Robot Pact With JD.com, Starting With Two Unstaffed Beijing Coffee Counters

Wujie Dynamics and JD.com signed an agreement on October 31 targeting ¥1 billion ($150 million) of robot business. K15 wheeled, two-armed robots already run two 7Fresh Coffee stores in Beijing without staff, with more than 1,000 stores planned.


The K15 has a 1,200 TOPS domestic compute platform and fits 800mm spaces. Founder Zhang Yufeng came from Horizon Robotics. Wujie raised more than $200 million in an angel round in June and claims nearly $100 million of orders.

Key signal: A coffee counter is a narrow, repeatable task with measurable throughput, a realistic first market for embodied AI, but the ¥1 billion ($150 million) is a target without unit prices or a timetable, and the rollout stands at 2 of 1,000+ stores.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · MARKET SUPPORT
Regulators Call In Banks and Brokers Today to Widen Stock-Support Tools After the STAR 50 Fell 24% in October

The PBOC and CSRC meet brokers and fund companies on November 2, and the PBOC, NFRA and CSRC meet banks on the buyback relending facility. Cailian reported, unconfirmed, that a big state bank may give China Reform Holdings and China Chengtong up to ¥100 billion ($15.0 billion) each in buyback loans.


The STAR 50 fell 24.13% in October and ChiNext 23.02%; Reuters put the loss at about ¥10 trillion ($1.50 trillion). The existing ¥800 billion ($120 billion) tool pool is only about 29% used for buybacks after two years.

Key signal: Weak take-up shows companies are reluctant to borrow for buybacks while prices fall, so changes to rates, eligibility or collateral would matter more than a bigger quota; a readout without terms would amount to verbal support.
A Month of Losses, a Tool Pool Mostly Unused
