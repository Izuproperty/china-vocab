STORY 1 · AI & TECH
AI-Found PHP Flaws Knock SoftBank's Quake Alerts Offline, and METI Orders an AI Red Team for November

PHP's developers released fixes on October 21 for two critical 脆弱性 (zeijakusei, vulnerabilities) that let an attacker run code on a web server without logging in. Wiz Research found the bugs with its own Atlas tool and Google Gemini and OpenAI GPT models, reported them to PHP on August 24, and says the faulty code has sat in the language for more than a decade, back to PHP 5.4. PHP runs about 70% of the world's websites. Cloudflare chief executive Matthew Prince said exploit attempts reached the millions, and Amazon logged an attempt on its decoy servers on October 17, four days before the patch was public.


Japanese operators responded by switching services off. ソフトバンク (Sofutobanku, SoftBank Corp., the mobile carrier) suspended 緊急速報メール (kinkyū sokuhō mēru, emergency alert texts) to SoftBank, Y!mobile and LINEMO phones at 06:47 JST on Friday and expects to restore them by October 26, so its users will not get earthquake, tsunami or evacuation warnings by text until then. MUFG, Nomura, ANA and JR East suspended some online services, and the Ministry of Finance, the National Tax Agency and the Japan Meteorological Agency took systems offline. Nippon Paint patched and brought its site back. Officials have not confirmed any breach tied to the flaws.


The same day, industry minister Ryosei Akazawa said the 情報処理推進機構 (Jōhō Shori Suishin Kikō, Information-technology Promotion Agency, IPA), which reports to METI, will set up a team in November that uses AI to run 模擬攻撃 (mogi kōgeki, simulated attacks) on Japanese systems so their owners can fix weak points first. He said defenders have to think like attackers, and he cited this month's run of breaches at Japanese companies rather than PHP. Trend Micro chief executive Eva Chen told Reuters that AI translation has removed the language barrier that used to make Japanese corporate records harder to steal and use. Japan now faces AI on both sides: models found a decade-old flaw in weeks, and attackers began exploiting it within days of disclosure.

Investor takeaway: Japanese banks, carriers and ministries chose to go offline because they could not patch thousands of PHP servers in two days, and that gap between disclosure and exploitation will keep shrinking as AI tools spread. Demand for managed patching and vulnerability scanning should rise for Trend Micro (TSE: 4704) and for the system integrators that run legacy client sites, such as NTT Data and Fujitsu. Watch whether SoftBank restores alerts by October 26, whether any Japanese breach is traced to the flaws, and how much money IPA's team gets in the next extra budget.

STORY 2 · ROBOTICS
Fanuc Lifts Its Profit Guide 10% to a Record and Adds a ¥50 Billion Buyback as CNC Orders More Than Double

ファナック (Fanakku, Fanuc) reported after the close on October 23 that July-September 受注 (juchū, orders) reached ¥306.0 billion, 8.5% more than the ¥281.9 billion it booked in April-June. Orders in its FA division, which sells 数値制御 (sūchi seigyo, numerical control, CNC) systems and servo motors to machine tool makers, were 2.3 times the level of a year earlier. Quarterly operating profit of ¥63.2 billion came in 24% above the level its own first-half guide implied, and the operating margin rose to 26.0% from 20.6%. 経常利益 (keijō rieki, ordinary profit) rose 43.5% to ¥80.8 billion for the quarter and 38.1% to ¥149.0 billion for the half.


Fanuc raised its full-year operating profit forecast for the year to March 2027 to ¥240.7 billion from ¥218 billion and its ordinary profit forecast to ¥308.8 billion from ¥271.2 billion. Nikkei reported that this puts net profit up 37% at a record, the first in 12 years. The company also set an interim dividend of ¥72.54 and a 自社株買い (jisha kabu gai, share buyback) of up to 10 million shares, about 1.08% of the total, or ¥50 billion, running from October 26 to April 30. The shares closed at ¥5,946 on October 19, a third below their May high, so the buyback lands on a stock that has already de-rated.


The CNC surge follows Japan's machine tool industry. Orders for Japanese machine tools hit a record ¥223 billion in September as makers of AI servers, power equipment and chip tools ordered ahead, and nearly every one of those machines needs a controller and servos, which Fanuc dominates. The second half looks weaker on paper: the new guide adds only ¥10.4 billion of operating profit on ¥57.9 billion of extra sales, which implies a lower margin than the first half. In July Fanuc wrote that some customers had ordered in advance and that it could not count on that pace continuing, and the stock fell 14% the next day.

Investor takeaway: Fanuc (TSE: 6954) is earning record profits on CNC demand from AI-related capex, and its new guide of ¥240.7 billion still sits well below the roughly ¥270 billion analysts had pencilled in, with a second-half margin plan that leaves room to beat if the yen stays near ¥158. The risk is that pulled-forward orders unwind: if FA orders fall back in October-December, the 2.3x print was timing. Watch JMTBA's October machine tool orders in mid-November and Yaskawa's servo commentary for confirmation.

STORY 3 · MACRO · BOJ
September Core CPI Rises to 2.1% and the Measure Without Energy Jumps to 2.4%, a Week Before the BOJ Meets

Japan's nationwide 消費者物価指数 (shōhisha bukka shisū, consumer price index) excluding fresh food rose 2.1% in September from a year earlier, up from 1.7% in August and in line with forecasts. It is the first reading above the Bank of Japan's 2% target in nine months. Headline inflation also came in at 2.1%, from 1.9%. The 生鮮食品及びエネルギーを除く総合 (seisen shokuhin oyobi enerugī o nozoku sōgō, CPI excluding fresh food and energy) rose to 2.4% from 1.9%, above the 2.3% consensus. Goods prices rose 2.8% and services 1.5%, up from 2.6% and 1.2%.


Fuel costs rose because the Middle East conflict keeps oil above $110 a barrel, the yen near ¥158 makes imports dearer, and AI-related demand is lifting prices for some goods. Those forces outweighed the government's fuel subsidies. Services matter most to the BOJ because companies are passing higher wages through to customers, and 価格転嫁 (kakaku tenka, price pass-through) in services is what the bank needs to see before it calls inflation self-sustaining. The jump in the ex-energy measure means the rise cannot be put down to oil alone.


The BOJ meets on October 29-30 with its policy rate at 1.25%. Reuters sources expect no change, a higher inflation forecast in the outlook report, and possibly a statement that underlying inflation has probably reached 2%, which the bank has never said since adopting the target. Governor Ueda said last week that the bank will not accept an overshoot. The government is moving more slowly: economy minister Minoru Kiuchi said the conditions for declaring デフレ脱却 (defure dakkyaku, an exit from deflation) have not yet been met. Masato Koike of Sompo Institute Plus expects a December hike and argues prices are not accelerating fast enough to justify October. The September print also weakens the case of the two dissenters, Toichiro Asada and Ayano Sato, who argued last month that inflation was still below target.

Investor takeaway: A 2.4% ex-energy reading gives the BOJ the data to say underlying inflation has reached 2% on October 30, which would make a December hike the base case and support two-year JGB yields and the yen near ¥158. Kiuchi's comment shows the Takaichi government is not ready to say deflation is over, and that gap is the political risk to a December move. Watch the outlook report's fiscal 2027 core forecast, Tokyo's October CPI on the morning of October 30, and whether Asada and Sato dissent again.
