STORY 1 · AI & TECH
ElevenLabs Makes Its First Acquisition, Buying Kotoba, Maker of the Koto Japanese Voice Model, for About $100 Million

US voice AI developer イレブンラボ (Irebun Rabo, ElevenLabs) agreed to buy Kotoba Technologies, whose Koto model handles Japanese 音声合成 (onsei gōsei, speech synthesis), 音声認識 (onsei ninshiki, speech recognition) and 同時通訳 (dōji tsūyaku, simultaneous interpretation). ElevenLabs announced the deal on November 16, and chief executive Mati Staniszewski told a Tokyo press conference on November 17 that the price was about $100 million, which Business Insider Japan converted to about ¥15.9 billion. It is ElevenLabs' first acquisition. The company expects to close late in the fourth quarter, subject to regulatory approval, and all 15 Kotoba employees will join.


Kotoba was founded in July 2023 by 小島熙之 (Kojima Noriyuki), a former Hugging Face researcher who helped build Fugaku-LLM on RIKEN's supercomputer, and 笠井淳吾 (Kasai Jungo), a former assistant professor at Toyota Technological Institute at Chicago. It is headquartered in San Francisco and does its research in Tokyo. Koto runs the Japanese side of Salesforce's Agentforce Voice, generally available since August, and supplies translation for SoftBank's だれでもAI service. Asked whether the SoftBank arrangement survives the deal, Kojima said he could not comment on individual contracts and that the aim is to move customers over without disruption. He said the consumer interpretation app will continue and that Kotoba will now hire the sales and support staff it never had.


ElevenLabs paid about $6.7 million per employee for a model that had already passed enterprise procurement at Salesforce. Against the $22 billion valuation set in its September employee tender, the price is under 0.5% of the company, so buying beat the cost and time of training a Japanese model to the same standard. The deal also moves one of the few Tokyo-built frontier speech models under a US owner. That matters to Japanese buyers that want domestic suppliers for call recordings and customer voice data, because the alternative they had picked is now part of a foreign platform. ElevenLabs already works with NTT Docomo Business on contact centers, and the company said it will hire local sales leadership and invest in Japanese infrastructure. ElevenLabs' announcement did not commit to keeping Koto as a separately sold product or to honoring Kotoba's existing API terms.

Investor takeaway: Watch the close and whether Koto stays on sale under its own name; ElevenLabs only committed to folding Kotoba's research into its models, which puts the SoftBank translation contract and Kotoba's API customers at renewal risk. Japanese contact-center outsourcers face cheaper native-quality voice agents from a vendor with Salesforce and Docomo Business already as channels. Domestic AI developers that sell on data residency, such as NTT and Fujitsu, lose a local competitor and gain a sales argument.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Preferred Networks Picks SEMIFIVE and Samsung Foundry to Build Its MN-Core L2000 Inference Chip, Stacking DRAM Directly on the Logic Die

プリファードネットワークス (Purifādo Nettowākusu, Preferred Networks, PFN) chose South Korea's SEMIFIVE as ターンキー (tānkī, turnkey) vendor for the MN-Core L2000, its next 推論 (suiron, inference) chip, with Samsung Foundry supplying manufacturing technology and 3D-integration infrastructure. The announcement came on November 16 in the US and November 17 in Asia, as SEMIFIVE unveiled its 3D-IC platform at the SC26 supercomputing conference in Chicago. The L2000 積層 (sekisō, stacks) DRAM directly on the logic die and connects the two through hundreds of thousands of vertical channels, with no silicon interposer. PFN says this will give the chip more than twice the メモリ帯域幅 (memori taiikihaba, memory bandwidth) of the L1000, which ships in 2027. Tape-out is set for the first quarter of 2027, and PFN's roadmap lists the L2000 for 2028.


Chief executive 岡野原大輔 (Okanohara Daisuke) said bandwidth is the main bottleneck in generative AI inference, and that stacking makes heat, power delivery and yield harder to manage. He said SEMIFIVE's turnkey record would let PFN solve those problems. SEMIFIVE will run the supply chain across DRAM suppliers, the foundry, bonding, packaging and test. Chinese trade press puts the L1000 at about 200 TFLOPS of FP8 compute with 32GB of stacked memory and more than 10TB/s per chip, and PFN's own site lists 128GB and over 40TB/s for a four-chip workstation. None of the reports gave a process node or a customer. PFN is targeting an IPO between 2028 and 2030 to fund MN-Core mass production, and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries invested ¥10 billion in September.


Stacking DRAM on logic lets PFN avoid HBM, which SK Hynix, Samsung and Micron allocate to Nvidia and the hyperscalers first. An inference chip that does not wait for HBM allocation can be priced and shipped on PFN's schedule, which is the core of its pitch to Japanese data centers. The choice of Samsung also means Japan's best-funded private AI chip designer will manufacture in Korea rather than at ラピダス (Rapidasu, Rapidus) or TSMC's Kumamoto fab, a week after reports that Rapidus has not yet made a working 2nm SRAM cell. Samsung runs a METI-funded advanced packaging lab in Yokohama, but none of the announcements said where the L2000 will be bonded or tested.

Investor takeaway: The Q1 2027 tape-out is the next checkpoint, along with any disclosure of the node and of where back-end work is done. A working stacked-DRAM part would cut PFN's exposure to HBM shortages and support its IPO case; trouble with heat or yield would push the 2028 product and the listing. Japanese equipment and materials suppliers gain only if bonding and test land in Japan, so Samsung's Yokohama lab is the link to watch.

STORY 3 · MACRO · BOJ
BOJ Board Member Takata Says Rates Should Reach a 'Good' Level Early, on the Day GDP Shrank and With the Yen Near 159

日本銀行 (Nihon Ginkō, Bank of Japan) 審議委員 (shingi iin, policy board member) 高田創 (Takata Hajime) said in a speech on November 16 that the Bank must move its policy rate "at an early stage" to what he called a "good" position, near the middle of estimates of the 中立金利 (chūritsu kinri, neutral rate). He said the Bank should raise rates nimbly after gauging how loose domestic financial conditions are, that hikes need not follow fixed intervals, and that the neutral rate may sit above market estimates. He cited the weak yen and import costs as adding to inflation. Takata proposed a rise to 1.5% at the October meeting and lost 8-1. The policy rate is 1.25%, and BOJ staff put the nominal neutral rate between 1.1% and 2.5%, a range with a midpoint of 1.8%.


He spoke on the same day the Cabinet Office reported that real GDP shrank 0.1% annualized in July-September, with private consumption down 0.3%. Markets fell further on November 17. The Nikkei closed down 1.34% at 58,860.43, a two-day loss of 3.73%, the dollar traded near ¥159.1 after briefly passing ¥159.60, and the 10-year JGB yield stood near 2.88%, up from about 2.67% on November 9. Finance Minister 片山さつき (Katayama Satsuki) warned against one-sided moves after the yen briefly crossed 160 per dollar on November 12. Governor Ueda speaks on November 30, and the board meets on December 17-18.


Takata's case ties the rate to the currency. A weak yen raised yen-based import prices 24.8% in October, those costs cut household spending, and that drop in spending caused the GDP contraction. A hike that supports the yen works on the source of the consumption fall, so the hawks read the weak GDP print as a reason to move in December. The two members appointed under Prime Minister Takaichi dissented on the September hike and will read the same data as a reason to wait, which makes a split vote likely. If the board follows Takata to the 1.8% midpoint, that takes two more quarter-point moves, December's and one more by spring.

Investor takeaway: Ueda's November 30 speech settles December: if he repeats Takata's language on hikes without fixed intervals, the curve should price a path toward 1.75% by spring, which some forecasters already expect by about March. A December hike with the dollar near ¥160 lowers the chance the Finance Ministry has to sell dollars again. Megabanks gain margin from each move, while exporters lose part of the yen tailwind behind this autumn's raised profit guidance.
