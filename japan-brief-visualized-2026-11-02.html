STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Tokyo Asks Anthropic and OpenAI for Wider Access to Bug-Hunting AI Models, With an Emergency Cyber Package Due This Week

The Japanese government has asked アンソロピック (Ansoropikku, Anthropic) and OpenAI for more access to their most advanced models that can find software 脆弱性 (zeijakusei, vulnerabilities), Nikkei reported on October 30. Officials want to use the models to check government systems, and they will encourage 重要インフラ (jūyō infura, critical infrastructure) operators such as power and telecom companies to adopt them. The request is part of a cybersecurity package the government plans to release as early as this week. Neither company had commented publicly in the reports available by Sunday.


Prime Minister Takaichi ordered the package at an emergency cabinet meeting on October 26, after dozens of companies reported 不正アクセス (fusei akusesu, unauthorized access) to their systems and possible leaks of client data in recent weeks. "By next week, the government will compile an emergency package of measures," she said. The most visible victim is GMO Internet Group, where a hacker demanded $50 million by October 21. GMO's security unit found that a Chinese AI security-testing tool called Artex was used in several of the attacks. Attackers are using AI to find holes faster, so Tokyo wants defenders to run the same class of model over their own code first.


The request builds on earlier contact. PANews reported that a Japanese cybersecurity official met Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark on October 13, and that the two sides confirmed Anthropic will work with a government-affiliated AI safety institute in Japan to study how AI can be used in attacks. US developers release their strongest cyber-capable models only to a limited group of vetted organizations. Japan is asking to put more of its ministries and infrastructure operators on that list, and to get them on it faster.

Investor takeaway: The package will show whether Tokyo pays for access and deployment or only issues guidance. If power and telecom operators are told to adopt US vulnerability-scanning models, their security budgets rise, and the integrators that install and run these tools for Japanese clients, such as NEC, NTT Data and Fujitsu, gain service work. Watch for a named list of models and users, a budget line in the supplementary budget, and any statement from Anthropic or OpenAI on what they will provide.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Lasertec's Quarterly Revenue Falls 26% and Misses Forecasts, but It Raises Its Order Guide to as Much as ¥500 Billion on Repeat EUV Inspection Orders

レーザーテック (Rēzātekku, Lasertec) reported July-September 売上高 (uriagedaka, revenue) of ¥40.4 billion on October 30, down 26% from a year earlier, and operating profit of ¥15.8 billion, down 41%. Both came in below market consensus. Management said the quarter matched its own plan: tools shipped in the first half of 2026 have taken longer than expected to clear customer acceptance, so revenue recognition started later than it had assumed.


Orders moved the other way. Lasertec raised its full-year 受注見通し (juchū mitōshi, order outlook) to ¥400-500 billion from ¥300-400 billion. The increase comes from repeat orders for mass-production use of the ACTIS A200HiT, its actinic EUV マスク検査装置 (masuku kensa sōchi, mask inspection system), which checks photomasks with the same 13.5nm light the lithography scanner uses. Customers are ordering before they finish their own evaluations of the tool. In the year to June 2026, orders rose 126% to ¥237.5 billion while revenue fell 8.3% to ¥230.5 billion, and the order backlog stood at about ¥323 billion.


The gap between orders and revenue comes from timing, because Lasertec books revenue when customers accept the tool. An order year of ¥400-500 billion against about ¥230 billion of annual revenue would push the backlog well above a year of sales. Concentration is the main risk. Actinic patterned-mask inspection is bought by a handful of leading-edge logic makers and mask shops, so a capex pause at one of them would cut orders quickly.

Investor takeaway: The release gives investors weak reported earnings and a much larger order book at the same time. Slow acceptance makes second-half revenue harder to hit, even as the raised order range lifts the revenue base for fiscal 2028. Watch October-December order intake against the new range, any change to the full-year revenue forecast, and what TSMC, Samsung and Intel say about EUV mask capacity in their next capex updates.

STORY 3 · MACRO
The Food Tax Cut Bill Heads for a Lower House Vote This Week, but the Upper House Panel It Must Clear Is Split 20-20

The LDP wants committee and plenary votes in the 衆議院 (Shūgiin, House of Representatives) on Friday on the bill that would cut the 消費税 (shōhizei, consumption tax) on food from 8% to 1% for two years from April 2027, Jiji reported on October 31. The bill also pays low- and middle-income workers a benefit equal to one percentage point of consumption tax. Opposition parties want about 30 hours of debate in the 財務金融委員会 (Zaimu Kin'yū Iinkai, Financial Affairs Committee), and the total reaches 28 hours after Wednesday's session. They also want Prime Minister Takaichi to attend. Yuichi Goto, who handles Diet affairs for the centrist group formed by the Democratic Reform Party and Komeito, proposed that she sit for two hours. The two sides will discuss her attendance on Wednesday, the day after the 文化の日 (Bunka no Hi, Culture Day) holiday.


The harder test comes in the 参議院 (Sangiin, House of Councillors). On October 30 the LDP and the CDP agreed to set up a 40-member 特別委員会 (tokubetsu iinkai, special committee) on the tax bills, expected to form on Wednesday. The ruling and opposition camps hold 20 seats each, and the LDP chair normally does not vote, so the committee has no clear majority. The session ends on December 12. Komeito's Yasuhiro Nakagawa said a bill that creates a new system without specific figures is unprecedented and that his party cannot vote until the details are clear. He also called a temporary cut "very problematic" because farmers, restaurants and households take a second hit when the rate goes back to 8%.


The cut feeds directly into monetary policy. The BOJ's October 30 Outlook Report left the measure out of its forecasts and estimated that it would lower fiscal 2027 core CPI by about 1.3 percentage points. The BOJ forecasts fiscal 2027 core CPI at 2.6%, so a passed bill would mechanically pull measured core inflation down to about 1.3% in the first year of the cut, with no change in underlying price pressure. The Jiji reports give no annual revenue cost for the cut and no figures for the benefit program.

Investor takeaway: This week's lower house vote sets Japan's fiscal path for 2027. If the bill passes, the BOJ will be raising rates while headline CPI falls by more than a point, which makes its communication harder and puts more weight on its core-core and services measures. JGB investors will focus on funding, since none of the reports names a revenue source. Watch Friday's plenary vote, the upper house committee's first session, and whether Komeito's demand for figures forces the government to publish a cost estimate.
