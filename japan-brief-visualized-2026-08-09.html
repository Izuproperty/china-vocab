STORY 1 · AI & TECH TechTimes / Robotics 24/7
ZEALS' Omakase Robotics launches Japan's first hospital-certified humanoid — on a Chinese chassis

Omakase Robotics, ZEALS' robotics division, began commercial sales of the D1 humanoid on August 5 at roughly ¥5 million ($32,000) per unit, certified through a proof-of-concept at University of Tsukuba Hospital and pitched at hospitals, care facilities, hotels, and logistics sites for reception, wayfinding, and item handoff.

The D1 runs on a Unitree G1 chassis — the same Chinese-made hardware the US FCC added to its national-security Covered List roughly eight days before the launch, citing confirmed backdoors. Omakase's own software layer, Omakase OS, is the "quasi-domestic" (準国産) piece; the hardware supply chain still runs through China.

Investor read-through: Complicates the "sovereign AI, sovereign hardware" pitch underpinning Noetra and Japan's 10-million-robot strategy — watch for hospital/METI procurement rules as the next checkpoint.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTOR Aju Press / Japan Times
TSMC's Kumamoto fab returns to full capacity a week after Kyushu's biggest quake since 2016

TSMC confirmed August 4 that JASM Fab 1 is back to full-capacity operation after the M7.1 earthquake that struck Kyushu on July 28 — closing out the outlier status flagged in prior coverage, when Fab 1 lagged Tokyo Electron, Sony Semiconductor, and Renesas' restarts between July 29 and August 4.

The roughly one-week recovery compares favorably with the multi-month disruption after 2016's Kumamoto quakes, attributed to stronger seismic design and post-2016 business-continuity planning across the cluster.

Investor read-through: Direct P&L impact was always modest (Kumamoto is under 3% of TSMC's global capacity) — the open question is whether JASM-2's aftershock-related construction pause adds any slippage to the 2028 3nm target.

STORY 3 · MACRO & TRADE Nikkei Asia / KED Global
South Korea and Taiwan overtake Japan in exports for the first time — and Tokyo cuts its own growth forecast

South Korea ($496.3bn) and Taiwan ($416.6bn) each surpassed Japan ($384.4bn) in H1 2026 exports for the first time, as South Korean semiconductor exports surged 162.6% YoY to $192.4bn on AI-accelerator and HBM demand captured by Samsung and SK Hynix. Japan's chip exposure sits in materials and equipment, not memory/logic volume.

The Cabinet Office cut its FY2026 real GDP growth forecast to 0.9% from 1.3%, even as South Korea raised its own 2026 forecast from 2.0% to 3.0% over the same window.

Investor read-through: Sharpens rather than changes the Rapidus/Noetra thesis — but gives Takaichi's already sub-60%-approval cabinet an awkward domestic narrative on the exact technology its industrial policy has bet on.
