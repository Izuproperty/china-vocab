STORY 1 · AI & TECH · CHIPS / CAPITAL MARKETS
Alibaba Files to Spin Off T-Head in Hong Kong, With Analysts Valuing the Zhenwu Chip Designer Anywhere From $25 Billion to $86 Billion

Alibaba (阿里巴巴) filed with HKEX (港交所) on November 10 to spin off T-Head (平头哥) as a separately listed subsidiary through an H-share offer. The filing set no size, valuation or date; SCMP reports a $2–3 billion raise and Caixin cites a $25–86 billion valuation range.


T-Head designs the Zhenwu (真武) AI accelerators used inside Alibaba Cloud and has reported annualised revenue above ¥10 billion ($1.5 billion). Its dependence on the parent for sales will decide where in that range the deal prices.

Key signal: A Hong Kong listing gives offshore investors a direct way to own a Chinese AI chip designer, but the price depends on how much revenue the prospectus shows coming from outside Alibaba.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · CHIPS / SUPPLY CHAIN
Nexperia's China Unit Says Its 12-Inch Wafer Platform Now Covers Every Product Category and Lead Times Have Fallen to Four to Six Weeks, Deepening the Split From Its Dutch Parent

At the China International Semiconductor Expo in Beijing, Nexperia China (安世半导体) said its domestic 12-inch wafer (十二英寸晶圆) platform now covers every product category and some lead times (交货周期) have fallen to four to six weeks.


No wafer supplier was named and product lines are still migrating. The claim also feeds the Dutch parent's $2 billion damages case against Wingtech (闻泰科技), which accuses the China side of building a rival chain.

Key signal: Nexperia China is turning its forced break from the Dutch parent into a sales pitch on speed and price, which lowers auto-chip supply risk inside China but strengthens the legal case against Wingtech in Amsterdam.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · FISCAL / TAX POLICY
China Collected More Than ¥120 Billion ($18 Billion) of Offshore-Trust Tax in October as Declarations Pass ¥160 Billion ($24 Billion) and Personal Income Tax Jumps Nearly 90%

The State Taxation Administration (国家税务总局) said personal income tax (个人所得税) declared on offshore trusts (离岸信托) passed ¥160 billion ($24 billion), with more than ¥120 billion ($18 billion) paid in October after the Oct 22 deadline.


October PIT receipts rose nearly 90% and the ten-month total already tops all of 2025. Most of the money is back tax under a three-year lookback, so it gives the budget a one-off lift in a weak land-sale year.

Key signal: Beijing has turned offshore trusts into a visible, taxed structure, which raises one-off revenue now and reduces the tax reason for wealthy families to keep assets offshore.
