STORY 1 · AI & TECH / CHIPS & EXPORT CONTROLS
10,000 of 75,000: The First H200s Actually Reach China

ByteDance and Tencent each took delivery of ~10,000 Nvidia H200 accelerators — the first physical shipments to mainland China since Washington's December 2025 case-by-case approval. Each of ~10 cleared Chinese buyers may import up to 75,000 units under the January 2026 framework; NDRC mirrors the approval on China's side, clearing each purchase individually.


The 10,000-unit shipments are ~13% of each firm's licensed ceiling — the rest stays warehoused outside the mainland, mostly in Hong Kong. Two governments retain veto power over the same transaction, and the volume that moved arrived well below what both sides nominally cleared.

Key signal: Watch whether the second delivery tranche is materially larger — if shipments stall near 13% of quota, domestic accelerators remain the marginal compute source regardless of the license headline.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH / EMBODIED INTELLIGENCE
No Demonstration Data Required: A Beijing Startup's Bet Against the VLA Consensus

Acorn Robot closed an angel round led by CM Venture Capital and Nio Capital, four months after a ~$15M seed round, and unveiled Natus AGE-0 — a manipulation model built on tactile sensing and contact mechanics rather than vision-language-action. Founder Jiang Yao calls the approach "instinct-driven": no large demonstration dataset required to begin operating.


The go-to-market is narrower than most embodied-AI raises — standardized dual-arm cells for flexible manufacturing (cosmetics, food, consumer goods) sold as Manufacturing-as-a-Service, with one paid cosmetics-ODM deployment as proof point. Total disclosed funding is a fraction of Unitree's or AgiBot's.

Key signal: The no-pre-training-data claim is the real technical wager — but Acorn's own disclosures say generalization still depends on unverified "production environment" performance.

STORY 3 · MACRO & MARKETS / PROPERTY
15% Down, Not 20%: Shanghai Becomes the First Top-Tier City to Blink

Shanghai cut the minimum commercial-mortgage down payment for second homes outside the Outer Ring to 15% from 20%, expanded housing-provident-fund withdrawal to completed (not just pre-sale) homes, and added resale subsidies up to ¥80,000 — the first tier-1 city to ease since Q2 GDP growth slowed to 4.3%.


The move targets a specific pathology: court-auction home sales rose 42.7% y/y through July, with steep discounts reinforcing buyer expectations that prices haven't bottomed. Lowering the entry barrier helps marginal buyers but doesn't absorb the auction-driven inventory glut.

Key signal: Shanghai typically leads; other tier-1 cities likely reach for the same toolkit next if August transaction volume doesn't turn.
