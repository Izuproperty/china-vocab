STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Infinigence Closes a ¥2.3 Billion Series C, the Largest Round Yet for a Chinese AI Infrastructure Startup, Ahead of a Planned Hong Kong IPO

Infinigence raised ¥2.3 billion, more than its three earlier disclosed rounds combined, from a register led by state funds plus Tencent, Baidu and Lenovo. Bloomberg reports a confidential Hong Kong IPO filing for as early as the first half of 2027.


Its software trains and serves models across mixed Chinese and US chips. The company reports peak utilization of 97.6% and a 37.5% cut in per-token cost, but no revenue or margins.

Key signal: State and platform investors are paying to stitch China's fragmented domestic chips into usable capacity, and the IPO will be priced on whether token volume turns into gross margin.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH
Shanghai Targets 8,000 Humanoid Robots in Its Factories and 600 Industrial Robots per 10,000 Workers

Shanghai's new smart-manufacturing action sets 2028 goals of full smart-factory coverage for key manufacturers, 500 advanced factories, 10 industry models and 300 agents, plus 8,000 humanoids on factory floors.


Robot density already passed 500 per 10,000 workers in 2025, up from 260 in 2023. The humanoid goal counts wheeled and half-body machines and opens trial sites; no budget was published.

Key signal: Shanghai is handing its robot makers a pipeline of factory trial sites, which can produce the named customers that funding and listing reviews require, but reliability and payback still decide whether pilots become orders.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
Beijing Cuts the Export VAT Rebate on Electric Cars to 9% From 13% Starting April 2027, After NEV Exports Rose 130% This Year

From April 1, 2027, NEV exporters get back 9% of VAT instead of 13%, so about 4% of export value becomes a cost. It follows the end of solar rebates and the phase-out of battery rebates.


NEV exports rose 160% in September while domestic NEV sales fell 9.4%. Deutsche Bank puts the cost at about ¥5,000 per exported BYD car from Q2 2027.

Key signal: Beijing is withdrawing tax support from an export boom that now carries carmakers' growth, which squeezes overseas margins from Q2 2027 and gives exporters a reason to ship early in Q1.
