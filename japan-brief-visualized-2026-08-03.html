STORY 1 · AI & TECH BigGo Finance
Sakura Internet lifts its profit forecast 6.9-fold, then bets ¥26 billion more on NVIDIA's Rubin GPUs

Sakura Internet raised its FY2027 net profit forecast to ¥1.5bn — 6.9x the prior year — after Q1 (Apr–Jun) revenue jumped 49% YoY to ¥11.1bn and net profit swung to an ¥855m surplus from a ¥324m loss a year earlier. Alongside the guidance raise, the company unveiled a ¥26bn debt-financed investment in servers built around NVIDIA's next-generation Rubin GPU, delivery scheduled January 2027.

The ¥26bn outlay is not yet reflected in current guidance — the market is being asked to underwrite further capex ahead of confirmed revenue, financed entirely through bank borrowing rather than equity or cash on hand.

Investor read-through: A Government Cloud contract win gives Sakura Internet a recession-resistant revenue floor that most GPU-cloud peers lack, but the Rubin bet raises leverage into a demand cycle whose durability is still unproven.

STORY 2 · SEMI & INDUSTRIAL BigGo Finance / earnings call
Tokyo Electron posts a record quarter, raises first-half guidance, and splits its stock 5-for-1

Q1 FY2027 revenue reached ¥732.3bn (+2.9% QoQ, +33.3% YoY) with record gross profit of ¥342.7bn, driven by AI-data-center-linked WFE demand — non-memory (logic) 57% of sales, DRAM 32% on HBM. First-half guidance was sharply raised to revenue of ¥1.62tn and operating profit of ¥458bn, both would-be half-year records.

Management reiterated FY2027 mid-term targets (revenue >¥3tn, operating margin ≥35%) and added a fresh gross-margin-above-50%-within-two-years goal, alongside a 5-for-1 stock split (record date Sep 30) explicitly framed as broadening the individual-investor base.

Investor read-through: With TEL carrying ~10% price-weighted Nikkei 225 weight, this print argues the late-July AI-capex correction across Japanese chip-equipment names was a valuation reset, not an order-book concern.

STORY 3 · MACRO & FINANCE Bloomberg / Reuters
Tokyo and Washington intervene jointly to defend the yen — the first coordinated action since 2011

Finance Minister Katayama announced Tokyo and Washington undertook joint action to arrest the yen's slide toward 40-year lows; BOJ data suggest Japan sold as much as $58.97bn for yen, in an operation Katayama said began in New York trading and remains ongoing. This is the first coordinated Japan-US intervention since 2011.

The move follows the BOJ's July 31 hold at 1.00%, at a meeting where Ueda's press conference was read as leaving room for a hike sooner than consensus assumed — pairing announced, coordinated FX defense with hawkish rate-path signaling for the first time this cycle.

Investor read-through: A materially higher-commitment signal than MOF's earlier unconfirmed "ambush" operations — durability depends on whether the US-Japan rate gap narrows enough to support the level being defended.
