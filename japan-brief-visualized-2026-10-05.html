STORY 1 · AI & TECH
KDDI's ELYZA Gives Away Japanese Reasoning Models Built on the Public LLM-jp Base, and Says Agents Did Most of the Work

ELYZA released ELYZA-Thinking-1.0 in 33B dense and 32B-A3B mixture-of-experts versions on October 2, post-trained on NII's LLM-jp-4 and free for commercial use.


The dense model beats NII's own LLM-jp-4.1 and Qwen3-32B and trails Qwen3.5-27B and Gemma 4 31B. ELYZA says agents handled most of the work, with one to two person-days of human input per model.

Investor takeaway: ELYZA sells provenance to public-sector and regulated buyers who need models trained and hosted in Japan. Cheap, agent-run post-training lets KDDI (TSE: 9433) refresh its models each time NII publishes a new base, while NII's public budget covers pre-training. Watch for government or bank deployments that name ELYZA-Thinking.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Air Liquide Commits €170 Million to Gas Plants for a Kyushu Logic Fab Expansion

Air Liquide will build, own and operate nitrogen, oxygen, argon and hydrogen units with backup storage for an unnamed chipmaker's advanced logic expansion in southern Japan, under a long-term contract.


The profile matches JASM's second Kumamoto fab, upgraded to 3nm this year with volume output targeted for 2028. Air Liquide also won a €200 million Hiroshima award in April.

Investor takeaway: Utility plants are contracted ahead of tool move-in, so this order is evidence the 3nm schedule is being funded now. It supports the Kumamoto supplier cluster, Tokyo Electron's Kyushu operations among them. The domestic leader Nippon Sanso (TSE: 4091) lost two large, decade-plus contracts in one year.

STORY 3 · MACRO & FINANCE
Food Makers Raise Prices on 3,153 Items in October as the Diet Opens to Debate a Food Tax Cut

Teikoku Databank counted 3,153 food and drink price increases for October, averaging about 10%, and 20,187 for 2026. September was the peak at 4,965 items.


The Diet convenes today on Takaichi's bill to cut the food consumption tax from 8% to 1% for two years from April 2027, costing about ¥5 trillion a year with no named funding.

Investor takeaway: Food inflation stays high through year-end, which keeps a hike from 1.25% on the table, but from April 2027 the tax cut will pull measured CPI down by mechanical means for a year. Real wages (Wednesday) and household spending (Friday) decide how quickly the bill moves, and its funding gap keeps pressure on long JGBs.
