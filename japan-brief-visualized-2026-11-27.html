STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Preferred Networks Offers Manufacturers Its Robots, Software and Chips as One Package and Will Share Its Know-How With Noetra

プリファードネットワークス (Purifādo Nettowākusu, Preferred Networks, PFN), one of Japan's most valuable AI startups, is widening what it sells to manufacturers so that one contract can cover robots, software and its own chips, Nikkei reported on November 25. The company groups the offer under フィジカルAI (fijikaru AI, physical AI), meaning models that see and act on factory floors and in warehouses. It will also share its know-how with the 企業連合 (kigyō rengō, corporate consortium) behind Noetra, the state-backed foundation model company. PFN's transport 自律移動ロボット (jiritsu idō robotto, autonomous mobile robots) are reported to hold a leading share of Japan's market for such machines, but the reports give no share figure, customer names or contract values.


PFN already builds every layer it is now bundling. It designs the MN-Core accelerators, and on November 17 it picked SEMIFIVE and Samsung Foundry to build the MN-Core L2000 inference chip with DRAM stacked on the logic die, aiming for tape-out in early 2027 and a product in 2028 (Issue #172). It trains the PLaMo Japanese language models from scratch, and the Digital Agency has been testing PLaMo 2.0 Prime against Claude and Amazon Nova inside its GENAI platform for government staff. Mitsubishi Heavy Industries put ¥10 billion into PFN in September. A bundle lets a factory buy chip, model and robot from one vendor, and it gives PFN hardware and integration revenue that does not depend on beating US labs on general-purpose models.


The timing matches the policy money. The economic security outline the government showed the LDP on November 25 names physical AI as a target for concentrated investment (Issue #180), and Noetra's budget is already seen near ¥1.8 trillion (Issue #176). The arrangement carries a conflict: if the consortium uses PFN's methods to build its own physical AI stack, PFN ends up training a state-funded competitor. PFN is private and has pointed to a listing in 2028-2030, so listed exposure runs through partners such as MHI, Toyota and Fanuc. PFN has to name manufacturing customers and order sizes before anyone can check the market-share claim or value the business.

Investor takeaway: PFN is packaging its chips, models and robots for factories, which gives it hardware and integration revenue that does not rest on beating US labs on general models. The reports name no customers or contract sizes, so the leading-share claim in transport robots cannot be checked yet. Listed exposure runs through MHI, Toyota and Fanuc until a PFN IPO, which the company has targeted for 2028-2030.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Tokyo Plans to Make Chip Buyers, Not Just Chip Makers, File Stable Procurement Plans Under a 2027 Change to the Economic Security Law

The government is considering a rule that would require companies using semiconductors and advanced electronic components to draw up 安定調達 (antei chōtatsu, stable procurement) plans, Nikkei reported on November 26. The goal is to cut reliance on particular countries and on low-cost suppliers. The rule would come through an amendment to the 経済安全保障推進法 (Keizai Anzen Hoshō Suishin Hō, Economic Security Promotion Act) submitted to the 通常国会 (tsūjō kokkai, ordinary Diet session) in 2027, and the obligations would be phased in after it passes. 経済産業省 (Keizai Sangyō-shō, METI) and other ministries would decide which companies are covered after consulting industry. Kei Takagi, METI's parliamentary vice-minister, set out the direction at an expert meeting.


The 2022 law works on the supply side. It designates 特定重要物資 (tokutei jūyō busshi, specified critical materials), including semiconductors, critical minerals, natural gas and fertilizer, and supports them mainly through subsidies to producers. The new rule puts a duty on buyers such as automakers and electronics makers. To offset the cost, the state would subsidize the performance tests and trial production a buyer needs before switching to a new supplier, and where only one supplier exists it could fund facilities through an independent administrative agency. Which companies are covered, what a plan must contain and whether penalties apply have not been decided.


China's export curbs on permanent magnets and critical minerals are behind the plan. METI said in October that global yttrium supply had stalled (Issue #148). A mandate makes buyers pay for a second source, which raises input costs for automakers and electronics firms and gives domestic makers of mature-node chips, components and materials a customer base backed by law. The reports name no beneficiaries. The bill reaches the Diet in 2027, so any shift in orders lands in FY2027-28 at the earliest, and the size of the switching subsidies will set how quickly buyers move.

Investor takeaway: The rule moves the cost of supply security onto chip buyers: automakers and electronics firms pay for second sources, and domestic component and materials suppliers gain demand backed by law. Coverage, plan content and penalties are undecided, and the bill reaches the Diet in 2027, so orders shift in FY2027-28 at the earliest. Watch the size of the switching subsidies.

STORY 3 · MACRO
Tokyo CPI Is Due at 8:30 With Core Seen at 3.0%, a Day After BOJ Board Member Tamura Said Rates Should Reach Neutral 'As Quickly As Practicable'

The November 東京都区部消費者物価指数 (Tōkyō-to kubu shōhisha bukka shisū, Tokyo-area consumer price index) is released at 8:30 a.m. today. Forecasters expect core inflation, which excludes fresh food, to rise to 3.0% from 2.6% in October, and the measure that also strips out energy to rise to 3.0% from 2.4%. Headline Tokyo CPI was 2.8% in October. Scotiabank strategists said strong Tokyo prices, together with producer prices, wages and the end of government エネルギー補助金 (enerugī hojokin, energy subsidies), point to higher national inflation. Nationwide core CPI was 2.2% in October (Issue #175).


On Thursday, November 26, BOJ board member 田村直樹 (Tamura Naoki, Naoki Tamura) said in a speech in Kofu that rates should move toward the 中立金利 (chūritsu kinri, neutral rate) "as quickly as practicable." He said the 1.25% policy rate is below neutral, described 0.25-point hikes every few months as his baseline, and warned that a faster pace or a higher end point may be needed. He cited upside risks from the Middle East war, the weak yen and AI-related demand. A Reuters poll found 86% of economists expect a 利上げ (riage, rate hike) to 1.50% at the December 17-18 meeting, and markets price roughly 75-80%. The yen traded around 159.1-159.3 per dollar and the 10-year JGB yielded about 2.635%.


A 3.0% Tokyo core reading would follow the 4.2% services PPI reported on November 25 (Issue #180) and give the board a second sign that price gains are spreading. Part of the jump is mechanical because utility subsidies are lapsing, and the BOJ discounts energy swings, so the figure excluding energy will carry more weight in the vote. The nationwide November CPI comes out on December 17, the first day of the meeting. A print at or above 3.0% supports a December hike and strengthens Tamura's case for a faster path in 2027. A print near October's 2.6% would probably leave the December move intact but weaken the argument for speed. USD/JPY is near 160, where the Finance Ministry has stepped in before, so a firmer BOJ also eases pressure for intervention.

Investor takeaway: A Tokyo core print at or above 3.0% adds to the 4.2% services PPI and supports a December hike to 1.50%, with Tamura already arguing for a faster path after that. Expiring utility subsidies inflate the headline, so the reading excluding energy carries more weight with the board. Front-end JGB yields and the yen near 159 are the most direct exposures.
