STORY 1 · AI & TECH StartupHub.ai
Sakana AI opens Namazu, a commercial Japanese LLM API built on Moonshot's Kimi K2.6

Sakana AI's Namazu is a fine-tuned version of Moonshot AI's open-weight Kimi K2.6, adapted for Japanese business context and agentic tool use. The company reports the tuned model holds Kimi K2.6's general reasoning and coding scores (AIME26, MMLU-Pro, LiveCodeBench v6) while gaining sharply on Japan-specific tests — most notably a jump from 34.10% to 56.30% on FairPoliticsQA, an internal neutrality benchmark, alongside improvement on JFBench, a Japanese instruction-following test.

The commercial logic is narrower than a frontier-model play: Namazu targets the gap between expensive high-tier US APIs and untuned open-source models, in a market with few credible Japanese-language options at the professional tier. It is Sakana's second distinct monetization path alongside Fugu's open-model orchestration layer — both built on other labs' open weights rather than ground-up frontier training.

Investor read-through: Watch enterprise contract disclosures, not benchmark scores — that is what will validate the "narrower but defensible" niche strategy over the next 1-2 quarters.

STORY 2 · SEMI & INDUSTRIAL Reuters
Renesas sues Navitas over stolen GaN trade secrets, sharpening the fight over AI data-center power chips

Renesas alleges that Navitas CEO Christopher Allexandre and executive Felicia Cheng, both former Renesas employees, took confidential presentations covering Renesas's gallium-nitride (GaN) chip roadmap before joining Navitas — and that Navitas's subsequent "2.0" strategy mirrors the same target customers and technologies. Renesas seeks damages and an injunction; Navitas has said it will contest the claims.

The dispute sits on top of the fastest-growing corner of power semiconductors: both firms are chasing the shift to 800-volt data-center architectures for AI accelerators, where GaN and SiC displace older silicon designs. Navitas has rebuilt around it — guiding AI-linked revenue above a third of sales by year-end — while Renesas's own data-center exposure is a stated driver behind last quarter's 23% revenue growth.

Investor read-through: Treat this as a signal of how contested AI-power design wins have become, not a near-term financial event — trade-secret suits typically run for years and rarely move share prices on the filing alone.

STORY 3 · MACRO & FINANCE Bloomberg / CNBC
BOJ data confirm an ~$87 billion two-day intervention — and put a September rate hike back in play

BOJ data this week quantified last week's coordinated action: roughly $53 billion spent defending the yen on Thursday and a further $34 billion on Friday — the first joint US-Japan currency operation since 2011, lifting the yen from ¥163 to around ¥157/USD. The move has been unsteady since, swinging 1.4% intraday Monday before settling near ¥156.70, a pattern strategists read as consistent with further unconfirmed intervention.

Governor Ueda has flagged the September meeting as where an additional rate hike could land, warning that delaying policy action risks an inflation overshoot — read as mildly hawkish despite the July 31 hold at 1.00%. Intervention paired with a credible hike signal is a different policy mix than 2026's earlier solo operations, which failed to durably move the yen.

Investor read-through: The US-Japan rate differential, not intervention size, is the variable that decides whether ¥157 holds. Exporters and financials with unhedged dollar exposure are the direct readers of any follow-through.
