STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Qualcomm Signs Its First 5G Cross-License With Huawei and Agrees to Buy Huawei's US Patents, Reversing Who Pays Whom

Huawei and Qualcomm signed a multi-year cross-license on October 5 covering 5G, compute, AI and networking, their first 5G license. Qualcomm will also buy some of Huawei's US patents, pending regulatory approval, which Huawei says lifts its licensing contracts past $6.9 billion.


Huawei paid Qualcomm $1.8 billion in back royalties in 2020. Now the confirmed cash runs from Qualcomm to Huawei, through a channel US export controls do not cover, though both Washington and Beijing's technology-export rules could slow the patent transfer.

Key signal: Huawei has turned its patent portfolio into income that US sanctions do not block, and approval of Qualcomm's purchase in both Washington and Beijing will show whether patent sales become a regular channel for Chinese tech firms to earn from their R&D abroad.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH
Kling Picks Banks for a $1 Billion-Plus Hong Kong IPO, Carrying a ¥122.8 Billion Private Mark Above Kuaishou's Own Market Value

Kling picked CICC and Goldman Sachs among banks for a Hong Kong IPO of at least $1 billion, with a filing targeted for early 2027. Its ¥122.8 billion post-money valuation sits above Kuaishou's own market value of about ¥111 billion.


Revenue grew more than 200% in Q2 to over ¥850 million, slower than Q1's 300%. A state AI fund joined in August. The listing will force a public price on Kling and test whether Kuaishou's core video business is undervalued or Kling's private mark is too high.

Key signal: Kling's IPO will put a public price on Chinese AI video, and a valuation near the private ¥122.8 billion mark would make Kuaishou's short-video business look nearly free, while a lower price would cut into the parent's sum-of-parts case.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
¥19.6 Billion in Three Days: Holiday Trade-Ins Spend 2026's Last Subsidy Batch, and Car Swaps Run 30% Below the Year's Pace

Trade-ins drove ¥19.63 billion of sales from October 1 to 3 across 3.48 million buyers. The last ¥62.5 billion batch of 2026 funding went out before the holiday, so all ¥250 billion is now released, down from ¥300 billion in 2025.


Appliance and digital-product purchases ran about 30% above their 2026 daily average, but car trade-ins ran about 30% below it. Subsidies still lift small-ticket goods while the eligible pool of old cars shrinks.

Key signal: Trade-in money still moves phones and appliances, but car swaps are running well below the year's pace with the 2026 funds fully released, so a 2027 renewal at the December work conference matters more for durables demand than holiday headlines.
