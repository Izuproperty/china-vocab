STORY 1 · CHIPS / CAPITAL MARKETS
$3.0 Billion and "No Chance" of Cuts: SMIC Says AI Demand Is Spilling Into Everyday Chips

SMIC reported record Q2 2026 revenue of $3.0B, wafer shipments +14% q/q to 2.9M 8-inch-equivalent units, and ASP +5.7% q/q. Management said there's "no chance" of price cuts in H2 despite soft phone/auto demand, citing AI-driven "spillover" tightness reaching peripheral and analog chips. HK shares rose as much as 6.4% on the print.


Distinct from CXMT's memory-specific rally last month: SMIC's claim is broader and less verifiable — a read on mature-node foundry capacity generally, not one company's product-market fit. SMIC remains concentrated in mature nodes; Huawei's Ascend line is still the more direct domestic Nvidia alternative.

Key signal: Watch whether Hua Hong and other mature-node foundries echo the "spillover" language next earnings round before treating this as sector-wide.

STORY 2 · AI / FOUNDATION MODELS
Apple Breaks From Its China Playbook, Trains Its Own Model — With Alibaba's Help

Apple trained its own LLM for the China version of Apple Intelligence with technical help from Alibaba, breaking from its earlier plan to license a domestic model outright. Framed as a "dual-track" strategy: China-specific model with a local partner, Apple's own models elsewhere. Apple is described as the first foreign firm cleared to offer a proprietary AI model in China; rollout expected in coming months.


No commercial terms, rollout date, or benchmarks disclosed. What it confirms: Alibaba's Qwen stack is credible enough for one of the world's most risk-averse hardware companies to build on — at the same moment Alibaba is the name most exposed to a potential MOFCOM model-weight export-control catalogue.

Key signal: A distribution/market-access story before a capability one — the real signal arrives once China rollout details and performance data actually surface.

STORY 3 · MACRO / WEEK AHEAD
Week Ahead: China's July Activity Data Lands Monday — Economists Are Bracing for a Soft Print

NBS releases the full July batch Monday: industrial production (forecast 4.8% y/y, down from 5.3% in June), retail sales (forecast 1.4%, up from 1.0% — the lone bright spot), fixed-asset investment (expected to contract further), property prices, and July credit/TSF data (forecast ~RMB 980B, +7.4% y/y stock growth).


If the prints land near consensus, the more interesting question is whether it shifts the PBOC's calculus after last week's "appropriately loose" pledge with no specific rate move. A soft industrial/investment read alongside resilient retail would support the trade-in-subsidy narrative — pending Monday's actual numbers.

Key signal: This is a forecast preview, not a data release — treat directionally and revisit once Monday's NBS batch is in.
