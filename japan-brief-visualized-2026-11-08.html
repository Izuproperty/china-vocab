STORY 1 · AI & TECH
SoftBank Group's Default Swaps Widen to 466 Basis Points and Its Shares Fall 10.8%, Four Days Before Results

ソフトバンクグループ (Sofutobanku Gurūpu, SoftBank Group)'s five-year クレジット・デフォルト・スワップ (kurejitto deforuto suwappu, credit default swap) spread widened as far as 466 basis points on November 6, the widest since October 2022, and stood at 457.5 at 0653 GMT on Markit data. Reuters calculated that the level implies about a 30.6% probability of default. The spread was near 280 in June and around 430 on October 20. The shares closed down 10.8% the same day and have lost almost half their value since early September.


Reuters tied the selling to the way SoftBank paid for its AI bets. The group has put about $64.6 billion into OpenAI for roughly 13%, funded in part with bonds sold at record yields, including September's $11.1 billion high-yield issue with coupons up to 9.75%. OpenAI has pushed its own listing to 2027. SB Energy, the US data center developer that filed in September for a 新規株式公開 (shinki kabushiki kōkai, initial public offering) at a potential $50 billion valuation, has slipped to December or later. Its filing projected about $439 billion of revenue from customer contracts, but it runs no data centers today and depends heavily on OpenAI. The US midterms added a permitting risk: Futurum Group's Rolf Bulk estimated that about a third of US data center capacity planned for 2027-28 sits in states whose governors may slow development, while near-term demand is unaffected. The same week, China's security review blocked the China unit of ABB's robotics business from SoftBank's $5.375 billion purchase, and Bloomberg reported that the two sides are discussing a carve-out of a unit that could be worth $2 billion to $3 billion.


SoftBank reports results for the six months to September on November 10 at 3:30 p.m. JST. The OpenAI stake is marked to private funding rounds, so the quarter can show investment gains while the credit market charges more for the debt behind them. A spread above 450 raises the coupon on every bond SoftBank rolls, and that pushes the company toward selling or borrowing against Arm shares to meet commitments. Investors will read the 純資産価値 (jun shisan kachi, net asset value) and loan-to-value figures first, then any new date for SB Energy. Amir Anvarzadeh of Asymmetric Advisors said the stock may have found a near-term bottom but expects the lows to be tested again once a data center slowdown shows up next year.

Investor takeaway: SoftBank's stock and its credit now depend on whether OpenAI and SB Energy can raise outside money on schedule. A spread at a four-year high makes each refinancing more expensive and raises the odds of Arm-backed borrowing or asset sales. On November 10, check the loan-to-value ratio and whether management gives SB Energy a listing date.

STORY 2 · ROBOTICS & AUTO
Toyota Raises Its Profit Guide by ¥500 Billion to ¥3.9 Trillion as Tariff Costs Shrink, and Makes Robotics a Company-Wide Unit

トヨタ自動車 (Toyota Jidōsha, Toyota Motor) reported on November 5 that April-September revenue rose 9.5% to ¥26.96 trillion and 営業利益 (eigyō rieki, operating profit) rose 9.2% to ¥2.19 trillion, an 8.1% margin. Net profit rose 26.1% to ¥2.24 trillion. Bloomberg said quarterly profit rose 34% to about ¥1.1 trillion, the first gain in six quarters. North America swung to a first-half operating profit of ¥166.1 billion from a ¥234 billion loss a year earlier, helped by lower tariff costs and price increases. Electrified vehicles made up 55.6% of sales, up from 46.7%, with hybrids at 2.45 million units and battery EVs more than doubling to 231,000.


Toyota raised its full-year operating profit guide by ¥500 billion to ¥3.9 trillion and its net profit guide by ¥320 billion to ¥3.57 trillion. It cut the revenue guide by ¥500 billion to ¥53.5 trillion and the group sales target to 10.99 million vehicles from 11.18 million because of weaker China volumes. Its estimate of US 関税 (kanzei, tariff) costs fell to about ¥1.1 trillion after parts-classification changes let some components qualify for lower rates, which accounts for about ¥210 billion of the increase. The yen averaged ¥159 to the dollar in the first half and added ¥685 billion to profit, and Toyota now assumes ¥157 for the rest of the year. It lifted the buyback cap by ¥300 billion to ¥1.3 trillion and the annual dividend to ¥100 from ¥95. The shares jumped more than 16%, their biggest one-day gain since 1987.


Toyota also gave robotics its own place in the company. Toyota Robotics becomes an organization directly under headquarters on January 1, 2027, led by chief production officer Takefumi Shiga. CFO 宮崎洋一 (Miyazaki Yōichi, Yoichi Miyazaki) called robotics a new pillar with revenue growth from 2030, citing demand for about 400,000 robots across Toyota's plants and suppliers and about ¥1 trillion a year of replacement demand. In September Toyota presented those figures as the cost of modernizing its factories. It now describes the same demand as a market it intends to serve. If Toyota builds much of that 産業用ロボット (sangyōyō robotto, industrial robot) fleet itself, part of the demand bypasses ファナック (Fanakku, Fanuc) and 安川電機 (Yasukawa Denki, Yaskawa Electric). If it buys arms and supplies the software and integration, those makers gain a very large customer.

Investor takeaway: Tariff relief, cost cuts and the weak yen produced most of the ¥500 billion raise, while China volumes fell. The buyback and the North American turnaround drove the 16% move. The robotics unit turns a ¥1 trillion-a-year cost line into a possible revenue line, which makes Toyota either a large customer or a new competitor for Fanuc and Yaskawa. Watch for a product plan or outside customers before the January launch, and how the guide holds if the yen strengthens past ¥150.

STORY 3 · MACRO
Real Wages Rise 2.2% in September for a Ninth Straight Month as Nominal Pay Grows at the Fastest Pace Since 1997

The 厚生労働省 (Kōsei Rōdō-shō, Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare) said on November 6 that 実質賃金 (jisshitsu chingin, real wages) rose 2.2% in September from a year earlier, a ninth straight monthly gain, after a revised 1.6% in August. Nominal total cash earnings rose 4.6% to ¥311,409 per worker, the fastest increase since January 1997. 基本給 (kihonkyū, base pay) rose 3.7% after 3.8% in August, which the ministry credited to this year's 春闘 (shuntō, spring wage talks). Overtime pay rose 3.6%. Special payments, mostly bonuses, jumped 32.6%, and the ministry said a statistical quirk caused most of that. The price index used to deflate wages rose 2.4%, down from 3.4% a year earlier. In September 2025 real wages fell 1.4%, and they declined in every month of 2025, so this year's gains start from a low base.


Base pay is the number the 日本銀行 (Nippon Ginkō, Bank of Japan) can lean on. Base salaries rising 3.7% against 2.4% inflation lift household purchasing power without help from bonuses, and that is the wage-price link the board has said it needs before raising rates further. The bank held at 1.25% on October 30 while saying underlying inflation had generally reached 2%, and Tokyo core inflation ran above 2% in October. Markets have weakened the case for moving soon. The wage data came out on the day the Nikkei closed 21% below its June record, and a board that sees financial conditions tightening on their own can wait. The Summary of Opinions from the October meeting, expected on November 10, will show how many members want to move in December.


The bonus surge will likely drop out of October's figures, so the headline real wage gain should narrow next month even if base pay holds near 3.7%. A December hike would help bank margins and support the yen, which has stayed near ¥158 to the dollar through the equity selloff. A delay would extend the weak yen that is lifting exporter guidance this earnings season, including Toyota's.

Investor takeaway: Base pay is outrunning prices, which supports the BOJ's case for another hike. The 32.6% bonus jump inflates the headline and will likely fade. The equity slide gives the board a reason to wait, so the November 10 Summary of Opinions matters more than usual: a December hike favors banks and the yen over exporters.
