STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Tencent Leases 100,000 Advanced AI Chips From Oracle in Southeast Asia in a Reported $7 Billion Deal

The Financial Times reported on September 30 that Tencent signed a five-year, ~$7 billion lease with Oracle for about 100,000 advanced AI chips in Southeast Asia, roughly 30% upfront. Neither company has confirmed it.


Leasing compute abroad sits outside US export controls. At ~$14,000 per chip per year, Tencent is paying a premium for hardware it cannot buy in China, and its Q2 free cash flow turned negative as capex rose 176%.

Key signal: Tencent is paying about $14,000 per chip per year to rent compute it cannot buy at home, a lawful channel that remote-access legislation would close and that Oracle has not yet confirmed.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH
JPMorgan Downgrades UBTech and Backs Humanoid Parts Makers, Forecasting a 56% Price Slide by 2035

JPMorgan forecasts humanoid and legged robot shipments rising from ~60,000 in 2026 to ~1.75 million in 2030 but expects prices to fall ~56% by 2035. It cut UBTech to Neutral (HK$74 from HK$118).


The bank kept Overweight on Leaderdrive and Orbbec, arguing reducer and vision suppliers are harder to displace than integrators facing constant new entrants.

Key signal: JPMorgan expects humanoid volumes to rise 29-fold by 2030 while prices fall 56% by 2035, so it prefers reducer and vision suppliers to integrators like UBTech, which it cut to Neutral with a HK$74 target.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
Week Ahead: A-Shares Reopen Thursday to a Golden Week Spending Tally, With 3.8% Producer Inflation in the Background

Mainland markets reopen Thursday, October 8, with the Golden Week tourism tally the first data test against last year's 911-yuan per-trip low.


September trade and price data follow mid-month. August exports rose 25% and PPI 3.8%, which limits the case for broad PBOC rate cuts.

Key signal: The Golden Week per-trip spending figure against last year's 911-yuan low is the first test when A-shares reopen on October 8, and 3.8% producer inflation makes broad PBOC easing a harder sell.
