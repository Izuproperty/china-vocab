STORY 1 · AI & TECH · ROBOTICS / CROSS-BORDER M&A
Beijing Blocks SoftBank's $5.4 Billion Purchase of ABB Robotics in China, Reportedly Over Fears That Chinese Robot Data Could Train OpenAI's Models

SoftBank told ABB that China's foreign investment security review office (外商投资安全审查), housed at the NDRC, has prohibited the acquisition of ABB Robotics' China business. The FT reported the decision on November 4 and ABB confirmed it on November 5. The deal, announced in October 2025 at a $5.375 billion enterprise value, was due to close in mid-to-late 2026.


No official reason has been given. The FT's sources cited concern that robotics data from China could help train models at OpenAI, which SoftBank backs heavily. China is ABB Robotics' largest market, roughly 30% of the business, and about 90% of robots sold there are built locally.

Key signal: Beijing has used its security review to stop a deal between a Swiss seller and a Japanese buyer because China-generated robot data could reach a US AI lab, which widens the review's reach to any automation asset with a US-linked owner.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · CHIPS
Nexperia's Dutch Arm Seeks $2 Billion From Wingtech and Its Founder in Amsterdam, as Wingtech Rejects a Plan That Would Leave It a Minority Holder Outside China

Nexperia B.V. (安世半导体) has filed a $2 billion damages claim in Amsterdam against Wingtech (闻泰科技), founder Zhang Xuezheng and two China units, alleging they built a rival China supply chain. Wingtech rejects the claim, says it has not been served, and turned down an administrator's plan that would leave it with the China business and only a minority of the rest.


A Dongguan court has barred any sale or dilution of Nexperia shares, with fines of ¥100,000 ($15,000) a day, on top of a ¥2.14 billion ($320 million) asset freeze. Wingtech's January–September revenue fell 93% to ¥2.05 billion ($310 million) and its shares carry a delisting warning.

Key signal: Both sides' legal moves now assume two separate Nexperias, and a $2 billion claim exceeds Wingtech's cash several times over, though enforcing a Dutch judgment in China would be difficult.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · LOCAL SOE CREDIT
Zhuhai Pledges ¥110 Billion ($16 Billion) of Funding Support for Huafa Group as the State Developer's ¥413 Billion Debt Pile Shakes China's Credit Market

Zhuhai's state-asset regulator (国资委) said on November 5 it has delivered more than ¥40 billion ($6.0 billion) to Huafa Group and will coordinate ¥70 billion ($10 billion) more. Huafa's 2029 onshore bond rebounded 17% to about 85, after missed trust payments and rating watches from Fitch and China Lianhe.


Huafa carries ¥413 billion ($61 billion) of interest-bearing debt (有息负债). At end-September its ¥52.2 billion ($7.8 billion) of cash covered 0.34× its short-term debt, and Zhuhai's land-sale revenue fell 94% between 2020 and 2025.

Key signal: The pledge leans on coordinated bank and state-firm money from a city whose land income has collapsed, so it eases near-term default fears without changing the cash shortfall that drove the sell-off.
