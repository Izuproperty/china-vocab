STORY 1 · AI & TECH
CADDi Becomes a Manufacturing-AI Unicorn With Toyota's Venture Arm at the Table
CADDi raised $114 million (¥17.7 billion) on September 16, lifting its valuation to $1.2 billion. New investors Moore Strategic Ventures, Coreline Ventures, Salesforce Ventures, and Toyota's Woven Capital joined returning backers Atomico, Globis Capital Partners, and Japan Post Asset Management, bringing total funding to $234 million. The platform reads engineering drawings and structures manufacturing data so factories can search designs by shape instead of filename.
Funding Trajectory to Unicorn Status
Investor Takeaway
Toyota's venture arm invests to fix its own problems, not to chase returns. The next proof point is a disclosed Toyota production deployment, not another funding headline.
SIGNAL
Woven Capital's presence suggests CADDi's platform is being tested inside Toyota's own supplier network.
WATCH
Whether CADDi discloses a live production deployment at Toyota or a supplier, not just funding news.
RISK
A domestic product built around Japanese manufacturing conventions may not translate directly to US factories.
STORY 2 · ROBOTICS & INDUSTRIAL
Panasonic Opens a 500-Person Battery Lab Built for Robots as Much as Cars
Panasonic Energy opened Energy Innovation Square in Kadoma, Osaka on September 16, staffed by roughly 500 researchers and paired with the adjacent Suminoe production site for about 800 combined staff. The company's stated goal is to double development efficiency by FY2031 versus FY2024. Automotive and data-center batteries are today's priority; drones, robotaxis, and humanoid robots are named as future demand.
Priority Ranking: Today vs. Future Battery Demand
Investor Takeaway
Panasonic prioritized automotive and data-center batteries today and named robotics as future demand. A real capacity commitment for robotics-grade cells -- not just this R&D lab -- would mark the shift from watching that market to building for it.
SIGNAL
Consolidating materials, cell design, and prototyping under one roof is a speed-to-market bet, not a capacity expansion.
WATCH
A follow-on capacity announcement specifically earmarked for robotics-grade cells.
RISK
EV demand growth has slowed relative to earlier forecasts, the core market this lab is built to defend.
STORY 3 · MACRO & FINANCE
Japan's Markets Reopen Into a Weak Yen and a Live Intervention Threat
Tokyo's cash equity market reopened September 24 after a five-day Silver Week closure, the longest in 11 years. Nikkei futures traded through the holiday priced about 2.5% above the September 18 close of 65,018.95. The yen weakened to ¥157-158/$ during the closure despite the BOJ's September 18 rate hike to 1.25%, as a 7-2 dissenting vote left traders skeptical further hikes will come easily.
Yen vs. Dollar: Rate Hike, Then Reopen
Investor Takeaway
A 25bp hike that doesn't stabilize the yen shifts the burden onto intervention, which cost ¥15.4 trillion last time for a temporary effect. Position for continued yen weakness until the currency itself turns, not just the policy rate.
SIGNAL
Rate checks reported by traders typically precede intervention -- authorities are already testing the water.
WATCH
Whether the Trump-Xi summit this week shifts chip-equipment demand expectations for Tokyo Electron and Advantest.
RISK
The 7-2 BOJ dissent caps how much further tightening markets expect, limiting rate policy's ability to support the yen.
