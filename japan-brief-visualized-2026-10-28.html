STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Son Picks Kuching and Peninsular Malaysia for a $50 Billion AI and Data Center Plan, Anwar Says, With Most of the Money From Others

ソフトバンクグループ (Sofutobanku Gurūpu, SoftBank Group) founder 孫正義 (Son Masayoshi) has chosen Kuching, the capital of Sarawak, and a site in Peninsular Malaysia for about $50 billion of investment in AI, new technology and データセンター (dēta sentā, data centers), Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said in a speech on October 24 after a video call with Son. SoftBank has not commented. Nikkei Asia reported that SoftBank would put in only part of the total, with project finance and partners covering the rest. The plan follows a 枠組み合意 (wakugumi gōi, framework agreement) that SoftBank signed on October 8 with Grab Holdings and Petroleum Sarawak, the state energy company, to study an AI and digital infrastructure platform in Sarawak.


Sarawak can offer what AI campuses now compete for: land and power. The state runs large 水力発電 (suiryoku hatsuden, hydropower) dams, and in May its premier set aside a 405-hectare site at Tanjung Embang, near Kuching, for a "Kuching AI Data Campus" with its own power and water supply, to be built as a public-private partnership starting with 120 hectares. Malaysia drew RM385.7 billion ($94 billion) of data-center-related investment from 2021 through the first half of 2026, according to the Malaysian Investment Development Authority, most of it from Amazon, Microsoft and Google. A SoftBank-led campus would add a Japanese sponsor to a market the US cloud companies have so far dominated.


The figure lands on a balance sheet that markets already doubt. SoftBank has committed about $64.6 billion to OpenAI, a $500 billion campus in Piketon, Ohio whose cost it expects to share with tenants, and €45 billion for data centers in northern France. Its shares had halved from the June peak by October 22 and its five-year credit default swaps reached a four-year high. The Malaysia structure, with SoftBank arranging the site and tenants while lenders and partners supply most of the capital, lets it add another project of this size without taking on more debt at the parent. Sarawak's legislature has been dissolved and state elections must be held by December 26, which gives Anwar and the state government a reason to announce large numbers early.

Investor takeaway: SoftBank Group (TSE: 9984) rose about 5% on October 27, but that move came from Anthropic's IPO filing lifting AI shares, and the Malaysia plan commits no SoftBank cash yet. Watch for three disclosures: how much equity the parent puts in, whether an anchor tenant such as OpenAI is named, and what management says at fiscal second-quarter results in November. A large cash share from the parent would push the credit spreads wider again.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Advantest Jumps 9% on Anthropic's IPO Filing and Reports Today, With Tokyo Electron and Kioxia on Friday

アドバンテスト (Adobantesuto, Advantest) rose about 9% to roughly ¥34,000 on October 27, the biggest gain in the Nikkei 225, after Anthropic filed its 新規株式公開 (shinki kabushiki kōkai, IPO) prospectus with the US Securities and Exchange Commission on October 26. The S-1 showed first-half revenue of $16.3 billion against $1.2 billion a year earlier. イビデン (Ibiden, Ibiden) rose 8.5%, SUMCO 5.5%, SoftBank Group 5.0% and Lasertec 4.9%, and the Nikkei closed up 1.73% at 63,268.37, ending a three-day losing streak. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index had gained 2.8% overnight.


The filing changed the funding picture that had driven the selloff. Investors had been selling AI hardware since mid-October on fears that the biggest buyers of compute could not keep raising money, and Advantest and Tokyo Electron alone took more than 420 points off the Nikkei on October 26. Anthropic's S-1 lists compute commitments with Google, Amazon, Microsoft and Broadcom that The Next Web tallied at more than $430 billion, and a public listing gives Anthropic a new way to pay for them. Those commitments turn into accelerators and memory, and a large share of AI accelerators pass through Advantest's 試験装置 (shiken sōchi, test equipment) before shipment, while Ibiden makes the パッケージ基板 (pakkēji kiban, package substrates) the largest chips sit on. Daiwa Securities strategist Daisuke Hashizume said the market was relieved by the news. The rally was narrow: about half of Prime Market stocks fell, real estate dropped 3.4% and Daiichi Sankyo lost 8.2%.


Advantest publishes fiscal second-quarter results today, October 28. In the first quarter revenue rose 39.3% and operating profit 53.3%, and the company has guided to ¥846 billion of operating profit for the year. Tokyo Electron and Kioxia report on October 30. Disco guided to a pause in HBM-related shipments for October through December when it reported on October 22, so memory test demand is the number most likely to disappoint.

Investor takeaway: After a 9% jump Advantest needs more than a beat in today's 決算発表 (kessan happyō, earnings release). A higher full-year guide or a firm view on 2027 tester demand would support the rebound in Ibiden, SUMCO and Lasertec as well. A cautious HBM test outlook that echoes Disco would put the October lows back in play before Tokyo Electron reports on Friday.

STORY 3 · MACRO
Services Prices Speed Up to 3.7% and Katayama Repeats His Yen Warning as the Dollar Nears ¥158

The Bank of Japan's 企業向けサービス価格指数 (kigyō-muke sābisu kakaku shisū, services producer price index) rose 3.7% from a year earlier in September, matching forecasts. August was revised down to 3.5% from 3.7%, so the rate picked up and now stands at its highest in more than two years. Goods producer prices went the other way, slowing to 6.8% from 7.6% as government fuel subsidies cut raw-material costs.


The services index measures what companies charge each other for transport, advertising, IT, leasing and similar work, and its biggest input is 人件費 (jinkenhi, labor costs). When it rises while goods inflation fades, it shows wage increases moving into prices, which is the kind of inflation the BOJ says it wants before raising rates further. The board meets on October 29-30 and is expected to hold the policy rate at 1.25%, set in September, while its Outlook Report decides whether to say underlying inflation has reached 2%. Most economists expect the next hike in December, and Tuesday's data support that call.


The yen is the BOJ's other pressure point. On the same day 財務大臣 (zaimu daijin, finance minister) 片山さつき (Katayama Satsuki) said the government "stands ready to take decisive action" in currency markets and is watching moves with a high sense of urgency. USD/JPY traded around 157.7 to 157.9, up from 156.37 the previous week, with the 200-day moving average near 158.5. The Ministry of Finance carried out 為替介入 (kawase kainyū, currency intervention) around 160 in 2024. For now Tokyo is relying on 口先介入 (kuchisaki kainyū, verbal intervention), and a weak yen raises import costs just as subsidies are holding goods prices down.

Investor takeaway: A firmer services trend makes it harder for the BOJ to sound patient on Friday. A clear December signal would support the yen and short-dated JGB yields and weigh on exporters that have benefited from ¥157. If the statement is cautious and USD/JPY breaks through 158.5, intervention risk rises quickly, and the 160 area becomes the line to watch.
