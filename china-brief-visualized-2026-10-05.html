STORY 1 · AI & TECH
A Washington Think Tank Counts 343 Immersion Scanners in Chinese Fabs and Asks for a Total DUV Export Ban

The Center for Technology & Statecraft estimates Chinese-owned fabs held ~343 immersion DUV scanners by early 2026, ~270 of them ASML NXT:1980i units, after buying about 90 in 2024 and 89 in 2025. One NXT:1980i can support 100,000+ Ascend 910C-equivalents a year, the authors estimate.


China's share of ASML system sales has fallen from 41% to 14% as the buying wave ended. The tools are installed, so a new ban would work through servicing and parts rather than new shipments.

Key signal: China front-loaded about 180 NXT:1980i purchases in two years, so a scanner ban now would mainly cut ASML's servicing and parts business in China, and its effect on output depends on whether Washington reaches maintenance.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH
Astribot Puts Its T1 Humanoid on Sale in the US at $18,000, Roughly 40% Above Its China Price

Shenzhen's Astribot used IROS 2026 in Pittsburgh to open US orders for the T1 at $18,000 with immediate delivery. The 66 kg, 23-degree-of-freedom robot runs the Lumo-2 foundation model on a cable-driven body.


Astribot retrains Lumo on curated customer data and pushes updates over the air, so a US research base would feed it Western task data. That same data loop is the main procurement risk.

Key signal: Astribot is charging US labs about 40% more than Chinese buyers and asking them to share task data for retraining, which tests whether US research demand holds once data-residency questions come up.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
Golden Week Box Office Reaches 753 Million Yuan in Four Days, About 30% Behind 2025, After the Weakest Opening Day in Years

Maoyan Pro data put National Day takings at 753 million yuan by 8 p.m. on October 4, against about 1.106 billion at the same point in 2025. Opening day fell 45% to about 176 million yuan.


The average ticket dropped to 36.3 yuan without lifting attendance, and holiday trips fell only 1.9% on day one, so travel diversion explains little of the slide.

Key signal: Cinema spending fell 45% on opening day while trips fell 1.9%, so households are cutting cheap discretionary services, which strengthens the case for fourth-quarter consumer transfers if the tourism tally also misses last year's 911 yuan per trip.
