STORY 1 · AI & TECH Sakana AI
Sakana AI Decouples Fugu's Orchestrator From Its Original Base Model
Sakana retrained Fugu's small "conductor" — the model that routes each request across a swappable pool of larger models — on Google's Gemma 4 instead of its original Qwen base, and found comparable accuracy and cost-efficiency versus random routing on an internal eval set. The pool was already modular (it added NVIDIA's Nemotron in Issue #62); this shows the conductor layer is equally swappable. Sakana says it next plans to train conductors on its own in-house models, so customers with data-sovereignty requirements could pick a domestically pretrained conductor while still routing into foreign frontier models inside the pool.
Investor read-through: A technical, not a policy, data point for Japan's sovereign-AI narrative — credible because it ships as an architectural option rather than a slogan, though no timeline or named customer for the in-house conductor has been disclosed yet.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS Sony · TSMC
Sony and TSMC to Build ¥1 Trillion Image-Sensor Joint Venture in Kumamoto
A new JV — Sony 60% / TSMC 40% — will build a next-gen CMOS image-sensor plant at Sony's existing Koshi, Kumamoto site, targeting ¥1 trillion (~$6.4B) combined investment and production as early as 2029. Apple's iPhone camera line is the anchor customer; both firms also point to higher-performance sensors for robots and autonomous vehicles under Japan's physical-AI push. This sits alongside, but is structurally distinct from, the TSMC-majority JASM logic fab already in the Kumamoto cluster.
Investor read-through: First output is three years out, so the near-term earnings impact is limited — the signal is capital commitment. Layered on JASM's $17B logic buildout, it reinforces Kyushu as Japan's leading-edge semiconductor hub, with execution risk stretched across a multi-year timeline.

STORY 3 · MACRO MoF · BOJ
Yen's Intervention Bounce Erodes as Current Account Swings to Surprise Deficit
The yen weakened as much as 1% to ¥159.35/USD on Aug 10 — the worst G-10 performer that day — erasing roughly half the gain from the Jul 31 US-Japan joint intervention. The same day, MoF data showed a surprise ¥92.3bn June current-account deficit, adding a fundamentals-based headwind. Katayama has pledged further intervention and flagged the Fed's FIMA Repo Facility as a backstop, but the real test is whether the BOJ backs the currency case with a September or October hike.
Investor read-through: Intervention is a signal, not a fix. The Aug 10 Summary of Opinions showed a hawkish tilt (one dissent for an immediate hike to 1.25%) alongside six straight months of real-wage gains — reinforcing, but not yet confirming, a September move.
