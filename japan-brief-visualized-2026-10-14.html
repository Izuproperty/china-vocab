STORY 1 · AI & TECH
NTT Data Nears a Deal to Build Anthropic a 500-Megawatt Data Center in Osaka

NTTデータ (NTT Dēta, NTT Data) is in the final stage of talks to build a data center in Osaka Prefecture for Anthropic, Nikkei Asia reported on October 13, citing several people familiar with the plan. The site would draw up to 500 megawatts, which would make it one of the largest data centers in Japan and Anthropic's first facility in the country built only for its use. Construction could cost up to ¥1 trillion (about $6.3 billion), and the GPU servers inside would lift the total to several trillion yen. The two sides aim to sign by the end of March 2027, the close of Japan's fiscal year, and part of the site could open in 2028. Neither company commented.


Power sets the schedule. A 500-megawatt connection to the grid would take years to secure, so the plan starts with 自家発電 (jika hatsuden, on-site power generation) and 燃料電池 (nenryō denchi, fuel cells). A Nikkei survey in September had NTT Data adding about 750 megawatts in Japan by 2033 for an estimated ¥1.5 trillion, and NTT Data shares dipped then on worries about the capital bill. This one customer would fill two-thirds of that plan. NTT Data has been a 完全子会社 (kanzen kogaisha, wholly owned subsidiary) of 日本電信電話 (Nippon Denshin Denwa, NTT) since NTT bought out minority shareholders in 2025, so listed investors see the spending and the contract through NTT itself.

Investor takeaway: A signed contract with a named AI lab would turn part of NTT's data center spending into contracted revenue, which answers the capex worry that hit NTT Data in September. Power is the risk: if on-site generation and fuel cells cannot carry the load until a grid link arrives, the 2028 start slips. Watch for a signing before March and for which power supplier joins the project.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Kioxia Falls 13% on a Chinese DRAM Advance in a Market It Does Not Sell Into, the Same Day S&P Raises Its Rating

キオクシア (Kiokushia, Kioxia) closed down 13.28% at ¥15,180 on Tuesday, among the most heavily traded stocks in Tokyo as the market reopened after Sports Day. 東京エレクトロン (Tōkyō Erekutoron, Tokyo Electron (TEL)) fell 7.15%, and the Nikkei 225 lost 1,253 points, or 1.8%, to 67,777.52. The broader TOPIX rose 5.86 points because money moved out of chips into the rest of the market. In Seoul, Samsung Electronics fell about 8.7% and SK hynix about 9.2%. The trigger was a report that China's ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) will release server DDR5 modules built on a 4F² cell layout by the end of 2026. That layout cuts the area of each memory cell by about a third from the mainstream 6F² design, so each wafer yields more bits. Samsung and SK hynix are researching the same structure and have not shipped it.


CXMT makes DRAM. Kioxia makes NAND flash and sells no DRAM, so Tuesday's drop was 連れ安 (tsureyasu, sympathy selling) on the fear that cheaper Chinese supply will end the scarcity pricing behind memory makers' margins. Positioning deepened the fall. Kioxia's 信用買い残 (shin'yō kaizan, margin buying balance) was large, and margin buyers forced to cut positions sold into the decline. CXMT has published no speed, capacity or yield figures, and it does not yet take meaningful share in high-end server memory.

Investor takeaway: The market priced a DRAM threat into a NAND company while a rating agency moved the other way. CXMT's 4F² parts have no published yields, so the test comes when samples reach server makers. Kioxia's October 30 results and the index buying due that evening are the next two price events; if management reports NAND contract prices still rising, the case for Tuesday's selloff weakens.

STORY 3 · MACRO
The BOJ Leans Against an October Hike as Producer Price Growth Slows to 6.8%, Leaving December in Play

Bank of Japan officials see little need to raise rates again at the October 29-30 meeting unless upside inflation risks jump, Bloomberg reported on October 13, citing people familiar with the discussions. They regard a hike as possible as early as December. The BOJ raised its policy rate to 1.25% on September 18, its second increase in three months, and officials see no case for a 連続利上げ (renzoku neage, back-to-back hike) on current data. Overnight swaps moved to about a 10% chance of an October hike and about 88% for a move by December. The yen dipped on the report and then firmed to about ¥157.1 per dollar by Tuesday evening in Tokyo.


Data released that morning supported the wait. The 企業物価指数 (kigyō bukka shisū, corporate goods price index) rose 6.8% from a year earlier in September, against a 7.6% median forecast and August's 7.6%, its first reading under 7% in four months. Prices fell 0.3% from August for a second monthly decline, led by farm products and petroleum. The 輸入物価指数 (yunyū bukka shisū, import price index) was still up 24.5% in yen terms and 17.9% in contract currencies, and nonferrous metals were up 40.2%. Month-on-month declines remove the urgency for October, and the high annual rates keep a later hike on the table.

Investor takeaway: With October priced near 10%, the yen loses its nearest policy support, and the ¥160 intervention line carries more of the defense. A December hike is close to fully priced, so the inflation path in the October 30 outlook report will move JGB yields more than the rate decision itself. Exporters keep their weak-yen tailwind for at least another six weeks.
