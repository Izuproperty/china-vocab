STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Panasonic Raises Its Profit Guide 20% to ¥710 Billion as Energy Profit Jumps to ¥55.6 Billion on Data Center Storage and EV Cells

パナソニック ホールディングス (Panasonikku Hōrudingusu, Panasonic Holdings) raised its forecast for adjusted operating profit in the year to March 2027 to ¥710 billion on October 30, from ¥590 billion. It is the second 上方修正 (jōhō shūsei, upward revision) in a row and adds ¥120 billion to the 業績予想 (gyōseki yosō, earnings forecast). Group CEO 楠見雄規 (Kusumi Yūki) said AI-related demand came in stronger than expected and that the size of the improvement surprised him. Price increases, a weaker yen and the absence of last year's US tariff charge also added to profit.


The battery unit did most of the work. Panasonic Energy earned ¥55.6 billion of operating profit in July-September, against ¥1.2 billion a year earlier, on higher sales of EV cells in North America and 蓄電池 (chikudenchi, storage battery) systems for data centers. Its full-year profit guide rose 16% to ¥198 billion from ¥171 billion. At a September 10 briefing the unit said data center storage revenue could reach as much as ¥1.5 trillion by fiscal 2030, up from ¥322 billion in fiscal 2025, with ¥550 billion targeted this year and ¥1 trillion in fiscal 2028. It is spending ¥350 billion over three years on that business and says it holds the largest global share in battery backup units for server racks.


AI demand also reached the components side. Panasonic Industry sold more capacitors and multilayer circuit board materials for AI servers. 村田製作所 (Murata Seisakusho, Murata Manufacturing) reported the same day and raised its full-year guide to ¥520 billion of operating profit on demand for multilayer ceramic capacitors in AI data centers. Both stocks fell during the October 30 session, Panasonic by more than 4% and Murata by more than 9%, as the Nikkei dropped 2.1% in a global sell-off of AI shares. Panasonic released its numbers after the close, so Tokyo trades them for the first time on Monday.

Investor takeaway: Panasonic's upgrade came from AI data center hardware and North American EV cells, and the market sold the stock on the day it lifted its guide. Monday's trading will show whether investors price it alongside Murata and the AI component suppliers or keep treating it as an EV battery maker. The company did not split Energy profit between data center storage and EV cells, so the next disclosure to watch is the storage revenue run rate against the ¥550 billion target, along with US EV demand from its main automaker customers.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Kioxia Earns ¥1.99 Trillion in a Quarter at an 80% Margin, Then Guides Profit Flat and Falls 11% in Overseas Trading

キオクシア (Kiokushia, Kioxia) reported July-September revenue of ¥2.48 trillion on October 30, up 40.3% from the previous quarter, and operating profit of ¥1.99 trillion, about 22 times the ¥85.9 billion of a year earlier. The 営業利益率 (eigyō rieki-ritsu, operating margin) reached about 80%, level with SK hynix. SSDs and storage brought in ¥1.62 trillion and smartphone and PC memory ¥789 billion. The quarter's gain came from price: the NAND 平均販売価格 (heikin hanbai kakaku, average selling price) rose by roughly a quarter in the three months to September, after a 70% jump in April-June.


The guide for October-December calls for about ¥2.5 trillion of revenue and ¥2 trillion of operating profit, roughly the same as the second quarter and well short of the ¥2.35 trillion analysts expected. A flat profit guide with full factories means Kioxia assumes prices stop rising. Volume is already sold: capacity for 2027 is fully booked, and long-term contracts with hyperscalers cover well over half of the shipments it projects for 2028. Chief Financial Officer Akira Menju said that after such large increases he cannot tell whether prices will keep rising in fiscal 2027, even slowly.


Kioxia also paid shareholders for the first time. The board declared a ¥90 中間配当 (chūkan haitō, interim dividend), payable December 8, after ¥800 billion of buybacks in the first half. The shares fell more than 9% during the Tokyo session on October 30 in the AI sell-off, the same day the first step of its TOPIX weight increase took effect, and they dropped another 11% on Germany's Tradegate after the release. The stock sits about 60% below its June peak. Tokyo trades the results on Monday and then closes Tuesday for 文化の日 (Bunka no Hi, Culture Day).

Investor takeaway: Kioxia has sold out next year's output, so its fiscal 2027 profit will move almost entirely with NAND contract prices, and management has said it cannot forecast them. The dividend shows the company now produces enough cash to pay out while funding its ¥5 trillion Japan capacity plan with Sandisk. Watch Samsung and SK hynix comments on NAND pricing and the contract price talks for the January quarter. A second flat guide in February would tell investors that earnings have peaked for this cycle.

STORY 3 · MACRO
Factory Output Jumps 2.2% and Manufacturers Plan 5.1% More for October, While Retail Sales Fall for a Second Month

経済産業省 (Keizai Sangyō-shō, METI) said 鉱工業生産 (kōkōgyō seisan, industrial production) rose 2.2% in September from August, against a 1.3% forecast, after a 2.4% fall the month before. It was the first increase in three months and the largest since January, taking the index to 104.1 (2020 = 100). Cars, production machinery and general-purpose machinery led eight rising sectors, while iron and steel and nonferrous metals fell. Manufacturers surveyed by METI plan to raise output another 5.1% in October and 0.8% in November. Inventories rose 2.5% in September, faster than shipments at 0.6%, so factories produced more than they sold.


Household demand moved the other way. 小売業販売額 (kourigyō hanbaigaku, retail sales) fell 0.7% from August, the second monthly decline after a revised 0.9% drop. Sales rose 2.3% from a year earlier, against the 3.3% economists expected and the slowest pace in three months; department store and supermarket sales rose only 1.7%. The labour market tightened. Unemployment fell to 2.4% from 2.5% as employment rose by 340,000 to 68.68 million, and the 有効求人倍率 (yūkō kyūjin bairitsu, jobs-to-applicants ratio) rose to 1.19 after three months at 1.18.


Prices explain part of the gap. Tokyo core inflation was 2.7% in September and 2.6% in October, so nominal retail growth of 2.3% means sales volumes fell once prices are stripped out. The yen traded near ¥157.6 per dollar on Friday, which keeps import costs high. The Nikkei closed at 63,290 on October 30, down 2.1% on the day, and Tokyo is shut on Tuesday for a public holiday before the July-September GDP estimate due in mid-November.

Investor takeaway: Production is recovering faster than spending, and inventories are building. METI's planned 5.1% October increase is large, and manufacturers' plans usually run ahead of actual output, so a smaller October print would not by itself signal weakness. The risk for third-quarter GDP sits in consumption: if real retail volumes kept falling through September, the quarter rests on factories and exports. Watch October retail sales, the next production flash, and whether automakers keep output high with inventories already up.
