STORY 1 · AI & TECH / ROBOTICS
The Ban Nobody Priced Into Vacuum Stocks: FCC's Robot Rule Freezes China's Cleaning-Robot Giants Out of New US Launches

The FCC's Covered List addition — read last week as a humanoid-robot measure aimed at Unitree — turns out to cover any foreign-made device over 2kg with locomotion, navigation and network connectivity. That definition sweeps in household robot vacuums, freezing new US model authorizations for the five Chinese brands that made up 68% of the 2025 global market.


Unlike humanoids, this is realized revenue, not a speculative market: Roborock alone posted ¥18.7B in 2025 sales (+56.5% y/y). The freeze hits new launches, not the installed base — existing units are grandfathered through at least 2029 — so the open question is substitution risk, and there are few non-Chinese alternatives at comparable price points.

Key signal: A rule framed as industrial policy is, in practice, a consumer-electronics freeze in a category China already won outright.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH / FOUNDATION MODELS
Same Size, Higher Score: DeepSeek's V4-Flash Beats Its Own Flagship Without Adding a Single Parameter

V4-Flash-0731 keeps the exact 284B/13B MoE architecture of the April preview — DeepSeek only re-ran post-training. The company's own results show it outscoring the larger V4-Pro-Preview flagship on all nine published agent/coding benchmarks, at unchanged pricing of $0.14/$0.28 per million tokens.


The read-through is sector-wide: if training technique substitutes for scale this reliably, the floor on "good enough" model pricing keeps falling — arriving two weeks after Moonshot's K3 pricing reset already pressured Zhipu and MiniMax valuations.

Key signal: Capability gains without compute scale-up are a headwind for anyone underwriting durable model-layer margins.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
The Number Behind the Politburo's Caution: July PMI Slips to 49.2, First Contraction Since February

Official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 in July from 50.3 in June, missing the 50.0 consensus and marking the first contraction reading since February. New orders — the more diagnostic sub-index — dropped to 48.5, the lowest since 2023.


The data landed the same day the Politburo pledged "incremental" H2 support — this reading is the evidence behind that language. A new-orders-led decline points to a demand problem rather than one-off noise, though July's typhoon disruption complicates a clean read this early.

Key signal: Watch August new orders specifically — a headline bounce driven by restocking would be a weaker signal than genuine order recovery.
