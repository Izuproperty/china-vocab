STORY 1 · AI & TECH · CHIPS
Three Quarters Early: Huawei Pulls Forward Its Ascend Roadmap to Answer Nvidia

At Huawei Connect 2026 in Shanghai (Sept 16–17), Huawei accelerated its Ascend chip roadmap: the 960DT moves up three quarters to Q1 2027, the 960PR one quarter to Q3 2027. Both are specified at double the compute, bandwidth, memory capacity, and interconnect ports of the current Ascend 950, built around a new "super node" rack-scale interconnect meant to rival Nvidia's NVLink.


The acceleration follows Huawei's disclosure of plans to enter South Korea's AI chip market with 8,192-accelerator Atlas SuperPod clusters, claimed at triple H20 inference throughput for a quarter of the cost — unverified vendor figures, but a clear signal of execution confidence on packaging and yield.

Key signal: A roadmap pulled forward nine months implies Huawei believes its packaging/yield bottlenecks are loosening faster than planned — watch for confirmed shipment volumes, not just the spec sheet.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · GPU FUNDING
A Third Raise in Eight Months: Biren Weighs Another $1 Billion Share Sale

Biren is sounding out investors for a ~$1B share placement — its third raise since its HK IPO earlier this year, timed to the start-of-October expiry of its 90-day post-placement lock-up. Biren is one of China's "four little dragons" of domestic GPU makers and has been on the US Entity List since October 2023.


The repeated-raise cadence says as much about the capital intensity of China's fabless-GPU-to-volume-production cycle as it does about Biren specifically — dilution costs and scarcity-value upside now sit in direct tension for holders.

Key signal: Three capital raises in eight months is a signal about sector-wide capital intensity, not just company execution — weigh dilution against scarcity value of domestic Nvidia-alternative exposure.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · PROPERTY
38 Straight Months, Mildest Decline Since 2025: China's Home-Price Slump Signals a Bottoming, Not a Recovery

New-home prices across 70 cities fell 0.17% m/m in August (-3.0% y/y, the mildest annual drop since Dec 2025), the 38th straight month of declines. Resale prices fell 0.31% m/m, an acceleration from July — the secondary market remains under more pressure than new-build pricing.


NBS and state media frame this as "bottoming," citing city-level easing and inventory drawdown programs. But a narrower decline isn't stabilization, and policymakers are reportedly weighing more direct intervention — including local-government purchases of unsold inventory — precisely because the current toolkit hasn't flipped the trend.

Key signal: Watch for a shift from city-level "targeted" easing to large-scale, centrally funded inventory absorption — nothing in this release confirms that shift has happened yet.
