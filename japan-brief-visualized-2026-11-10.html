STORY 1 · AI & TECH
SoftBank Corp Raises Its Profit Guide to ¥610 Billion, and Its CEO Says the US Neocloud May Launch in Other Countries First

ソフトバンク (Sofutobanku, SoftBank Corp), the listed telecom arm of SoftBank Group, reported on November 9 that April-September revenue rose 11.7% to ¥3.80 trillion, operating profit rose 2.1% to ¥642.1 billion and net profit rose 4.5% to ¥364.5 billion, all records for a first half. Second-quarter net profit grew 5.4% to ¥214.4 billion, but the quarterly operating margin fell to 17.1% from 19.4%. The company raised its full-year net profit guide to ¥610 billion from ¥560 billion and its revenue guide to ¥7.9 trillion from ¥7.5 trillion, and kept operating profit at ¥1.16 trillion. Part of the net increase comes from a gain on selling preferred equity in SB Energy, so the core earnings guide moved less than the headline.


The enterprise segment is where the AI business sits. Its half-year sales rose 12% to ¥538.9 billion and segment profit rose 20% to ¥124.8 billion, with cloud and AI revenue up about 37%. The company plan takes cloud and AI revenue from ¥255.8 billion to ¥330 billion and then ¥430 billion over successive fiscal years. A security service built on OpenAI technology has more than 7,000 corporate customers. The results also disclosed that a ランサムウェア (ransamuwea, ransomware) attack on data center subsidiary IDC Frontier on October 7 affected 495 corporate and local government customers.


Chief executive 宮川潤一 (Miyakawa Jun'ichi, Junichi Miyakawa) told Bloomberg he is assessing how the Democratic sweep of Congress and local opposition to data centers affect plans for SB Neo, the ネオクラウド (neo kuraudo, neocloud) that SoftBank Corp owns 51% and SoftBank Group 49%, which targets up to 10 gigawatts of US capacity and is due to start in the year to March 2028. He said the venture "may launch in other countries first" and that global demand for AI data centers remains strong. A move outside the US would change where SoftBank spends its capex and which power and permitting regimes it depends on. SoftBank Group reports at 3:30 p.m. today, and investors will look for whether it repeats the subsidiary's caution on US sites.

Investor takeaway: SoftBank Corp's raised guide rests partly on a one-off SB Energy gain, and its quarterly operating margin fell. The bigger change is Miyakawa's openness to starting SB Neo outside the US, which ties the group's largest infrastructure plan to US permitting politics. SoftBank Group's results this afternoon will show whether the parent shares that view.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Furukawa Electric Raises Its Fiber Profit Guide 23% and Says Data Center Orders Have Not Slowed After the US Midterms

古河電気工業 (Furukawa Denki Kōgyō, Furukawa Electric) reported on November 9 that April-September sales rose 20.8% to ¥737.7 billion and operating profit tripled to ¥57.6 billion, both the highest for a first half since at least 2001. Net profit rose 4.6 times to ¥58.8 billion, helped by a ¥21.7 billion gain on selling shares in aluminum maker UACJ in September. The second-quarter operating margin reached 8.6%, up from 3.4% a year earlier. The company raised its full-year guide for the year to March 2027: operating profit to ¥136 billion from ¥123 billion, ordinary profit to ¥155 billion from ¥143 billion, and net profit to ¥118 billion from ¥105 billion. The dividend forecast stays at ¥22 a share after the 10-for-1 split this summer.


Optical Solutions drove the upgrade. 光ファイバ (hikari faiba, optical fiber) and cable sales rose 43.9% in the half to ¥130.9 billion and segment profit reached ¥23.6 billion, almost all of it new compared with a year ago, and the full-year segment profit guide rose to ¥53 billion from ¥43 billion. Hyperscalers are buying high-count ribbon cable to link GPUs inside and between data center buildings. Furukawa announced a ¥100 billion plan in August to roughly double Rollable Ribbon Cable capacity at its Lightera plants in the US, Brazil and Japan and an Indian joint venture, with mass production from the second half of FY2028. It also expects to double supply of 銅箔 (dōhaku, copper foil) for AI server boards by the end of FY2029.


President Hideya Moridaira said he sees no negative signs in customer orders after the US midterms, that enquiries remain strong, and that he will not cut investment. He said the ¥170 billion operating profit target for FY2028 could arrive a year early. The stock needed that message. Furukawa fell about a third between October 23 and November 6 as US election results raised fears of slower data center permitting, and it closed down 8.6% on November 6 alone. The shares rose with the market on November 9 and extended gains after the 2 p.m. release. A similar 上方修正 (jōhō shūsei, upward revision) from peers Fujikura or Sumitomo Electric at their next results would confirm that fiber orders are holding up across the industry.

Investor takeaway: Furukawa's upgrade comes from fiber volumes, and the ¥13 billion operating profit raise excludes the one-off UACJ gain that inflated the net line. Management sees no order slowdown, which answers the specific fear behind the late-October selloff. Moridaira's view that the ¥170 billion target could come a year early is the next claim for investors to test.

STORY 3 · MACRO
The Finance Ministry Discloses a Record ¥7.98 Trillion Intervention on July 30, and the Yen Has Given Back Most of the Gain

The 財務省 (Zaimushō, Ministry of Finance) published daily 為替介入 (kawase kainyū, currency intervention) data for July through September on November 9. It sold dollars for ¥7.98 trillion, about $50 billion, on July 30, the largest single day since records began in 1991, as USD/JPY fell from 163.74 to 157.97. It sold another ¥5.55 trillion on July 31 in a joint operation with the US Treasury, after the dollar bounced to 160.88, and ¥1.87 trillion on August 3. The three days total ¥15.40 trillion. The previous one-day record was ¥6.28 trillion on April 30 of this year.


Japan has now intervened on six days in 2026 for ¥27.13 trillion, the largest annual total in the series. The ministry did not intervene between August 27 and October 28. 外貨準備高 (gaika junbi-daka, foreign reserves) stood at $1.179 trillion at the end of October, about $230 billion below end-February. The ministry funds yen purchases by selling dollar assets, mostly US Treasuries and deposits, so another ¥7.98 trillion day would use about $50 billion of them.


The dollar traded near ¥158 on November 9, roughly where it settled after the July 31 協調介入 (kyōchō kainyū, coordinated intervention). Interest rate differentials have not narrowed enough to hold the yen up: the BOJ kept its rate at 1.25% on October 30, and futures priced about a 65% chance of a December hike on November 6, down from about 80% a day earlier as the Nikkei fell. Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama said on November 2 that the September 2025 currency statement with Washington remains in force. Tokyo has shown it will spend at record scale near ¥160 to ¥164, and the BOJ's Summary of Opinions, released this morning, will show whether board members see enough inflation pressure to support the yen with a December move.

Investor takeaway: The July operation bought about six yen for ¥7.98 trillion, and three months later the dollar is near ¥158 again. The ministry still holds $1.18 trillion of reserves and has shown its threshold sits around ¥160 to ¥164. A lasting yen recovery depends on a BOJ hike, so today's Summary of Opinions is the next release that can move the currency.
