STORY 1 · AI & TECH · CHIPS
IFP Puts China's AI Compute at 4.6 Million H100-Equivalents, Rising to 8.8 Million in 2027, With About 65% on US-Designed Chips and Offshore Rentals Nearly Tripling

The Institute for Progress estimates Chinese entities have about 4.6M H100-equivalents of AI compute this year and will have about 8.8M in 2027, SCMP reported on October 24. US-designed chips make up about 65% of the 2027 figure, and capacity rented from offshore clouds rises from roughly 1M to 2.8M.


Huawei has moved its Ascend 960DT training chip forward to Q1 2027 and deployed more than 1,000 Atlas 900 A3 SuperPoDs, but Epoch AI says HBM limits its 2026 output to under 4% of the compute in Nvidia's global shipments. A separate think-tank paper urges BIS to stop listed Chinese firms from renting US compute legally.

Key signal: China's compute growth to 2027 still depends on US licensing and on cloud rentals that a single BIS rule could curb, which would shift demand onto Ascend and Cambricon parts already short of HBM and SMIC capacity.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · ROBOTICS
AgiBot-Controlled Swancor's Qiyuan Unit Rolls Out Its 5,000th ¥19,999 Personal Robot Five Weeks After Launch, With Daily Output Up From About 10 to More Than 200

Qiyuan Robotics, the robot unit of STAR-listed Swancor (688585), said on October 25 its 5,000th personal robot came off the line. It now builds one every 2.5 minutes, and daily capacity has gone from just over 10 to more than 200 using a semi-automated line set up with suppliers.


The Q1 and T1 launched on September 20 at ¥19,999 and began shipping October 1. An AgiBot-linked vehicle controls Swancor, AgiBot is reportedly raising at about ¥50B, and a Guancha briefing says regulators have raised the IPO bar for robot makers.

Key signal: 5,000 units at list price is at least ¥100M of gross value, but Qiyuan reported production rather than deliveries or orders, so the milestone proves the line works and leaves consumer demand untested.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · WEEK AHEAD
Week Ahead: The Fifth Plenum Opens Today on Party Discipline, as MSCI China Companies Miss Earnings by 9% on Average Before September Profits on Tuesday and PMIs on Saturday

The Central Committee's Fifth Plenum runs October 26 to 29 and reviews a decision on strict Party governance, with no five-year plan to ratify and no stimulus expected. September industrial profits come Tuesday after August growth slowed to 4.2%, and official PMIs come Saturday.


Bloomberg says Chinese stocks have lost $3.5T since May, MSCI China 2026 earnings forecasts are down more than 4% since March, Hang Seng Tech's more than 13%, and Q3 reporters have missed by 9% on average. The NPC Standing Committee takes up a draft Finance Law from November 2.

Key signal: With the plenum focused on discipline, Tuesday's profit data and Saturday's PMI will decide whether earnings downgrades keep running before December's Central Economic Work Conference.
