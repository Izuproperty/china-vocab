STORY 1 · CHIPS / AUTONOMOUS DRIVING
96 TOPS, Half the Cost: Geely's Chip Unit Ships an AI Accelerator That Skips the Redesign

SiEngine, a Geely-affiliated chipmaker, began mass deliveries Aug 12 of the TianGong 100 (NNA100), a 7nm automotive-grade AI accelerator: 96 TOPS INT8, 102GB/s of LPDDR5 bandwidth, enough for 3B–7B parameter on-device models. AEC-Q100 Grade 3 / ISO 26262 ASIL-B certified, priced in the low-thousands-of-yuan range, and pitched as requiring no redesign of a vehicle's E/E architecture.


The competitive axis here is edge-inference economics, not headline compute — a mid-tier category between smartphone SoCs and datacenter accelerators. No independent OEM or Tier-1 order book has been disclosed; the mass-production and cost claims are company-sourced for now.

Key signal: Watch for named automaker design wins before treating the cost-halving claim as validated rather than marketing.

STORY 2 · AUTONOMOUS DRIVING / CAPITAL MARKETS
2,000 Robotaxis, Zero Named Cities: Pony.ai and Uber Push Into Europe and the Middle East

Pony.ai (Nasdaq: PONY) and Uber said Aug 14 they'll expand beyond their existing Zagreb service — Europe's first commercial robotaxi launch — to four more European cities plus a Middle East rollout, targeting 2,000+ robotaxis. No cities or dates disclosed. Structure unchanged: Pony.ai's L4 stack, Uber's demand platform, a local fleet operator.


The asset-light model lets Pony.ai scale without fleet-ownership capex, and a listed Western partner sidesteps some trust/data friction Chinese AV exports face in the US. Without city-level detail, this is a strategic framework, not a committed operating plan.

Key signal: Treat as TAM-expansion optionality, not modeled revenue, until specific cities and launch dates are confirmed.

STORY 3 · MACRO / MARKET ACCESS
First in Europe: Deutsche Bank Becomes Beijing's Frankfurt Clearing Bank as the Yuan's Payment Share Hits 3.1%

PBOC named Deutsche Bank the RMB clearing bank for Frankfurt on Aug 10 — the first such designation for a foreign bank in Europe — alongside its 2026-2030 reform plan. Same window: SWIFT data showed RMB's global payments share at 3.1% (5th-ranked, up from 2.75%) and CIPS daily settlement at ~¥830B, up from ~¥680B in 2025.


Reads as market access more than monetary policy — a continuation of Beijing's pattern of doling out financial access incrementally (see Citigroup's May securities license). Hong Kong still holds ~76% of offshore RMB activity, so this is deepening within hubs, not broad diversification.

Key signal: Track which clearing hub or swap-line counterparty gets named next — that sequencing shows which financial relationships Beijing treats as durable.
