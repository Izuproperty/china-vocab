STORY 1 · AI & TECH Bloomberg / Japan Times
SoftBank's $40B OpenAI bridge loan draws 21 more lenders as the syndicate absorbs another $7B

SoftBank Group widened the syndicate on its $40B bridge loan backing OpenAI, with 21 additional lenders — First Abu Dhabi Bank, GIC, Standard Chartered, plus European, Japanese, and Taiwanese banks — absorbing roughly $7B. Signed in March, in general syndication since May, the loan carries a margin near 250bp over SOFR (~6.14% all-in) and leaves underwriters holding the remaining $33B.

The facility funds Vision Fund 2's $30B follow-on OpenAI commitment — the second $10B tranche closed July 1, a third due in October. The signal is in the willingness of 21 new banks to hold this credit risk at these spreads, not in the headline financing amount itself.

Investor read-through: SoftBank is increasingly funding its AI bet with debt secured against a single counterparty's trajectory (OpenAI). The loan's market-set pricing is a cleaner signal of how that concentration risk is viewed than SoftBank's own disclosures.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS Bloomberg / Nikkei / Reuters
Kioxia, Tokyo Electron and Renesas lead a chip-stock rout as China's CXMT debuts at a $487B valuation

Kioxia fell 18.3% to ¥44,550 (¥24.41T market cap), Tokyo Electron dropped 11.2%, and Renesas fell 10.6% on July 28. The Nikkei 225 closed down 4.16% at 62,230 — its lowest close in over two months. Trigger: CXMT's July 27 STAR Market debut, which raised $8.6B and surged up to 472% intraday to a $487B valuation.

Two compounding worries: memory oversupply as CXMT joins Samsung/SK Hynix/Micron at scale, and renewed concern that China's domestic DUV lithography progress erodes the export-control moat protecting Tokyo Electron. A separate worry over Nvidia's circular AI-financing arrangements added a second leg to the sell-off.

Investor read-through: Advantest reports Jul 29, Tokyo Electron Jul 30, Kioxia Jul 31 — order commentary from these three prints, not the CXMT headline, determines whether this is a one-day repricing or a sustained derating.

STORY 3 · MACRO & BOJ Reuters / Bloomberg
The Fed holds for a fifth straight meeting hours before the BOJ opens its own two-day session

The Fed held its target range at 3.50–3.75% at its July 28–29 FOMC meeting — the fifth consecutive hold — with Chair Kevin Warsh citing persistent above-target inflation. The decision landed just hours before the BOJ opens its own July 30–31 policy meeting. USD/JPY traded near ¥163.90 into the announcement.

With the Fed firmly on hold rather than easing, the BOJ faces no fresh pressure from a widening rate differential. Consensus still expects a BOJ hold at 1.00% alongside an upgraded FY2026 growth forecast in the quarterly Outlook Report.

Investor read-through: A hold with hawkish language from Ueda would clear the way for an autumn hike; a hold with cautious language, against this week's chip-driven equity weakness, would signal the BOJ prioritizing financial stability over its inflation mandate.
