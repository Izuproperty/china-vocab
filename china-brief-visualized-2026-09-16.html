STORY 1 · AI & TECH · POLICY
19 Articles, Effective Immediately: Beijing's New Exit-Entry Decree Puts Chip and AI Engineers on Notice

State Council Decree No. 841 — signed July 31 by Premier Li Qiang, effective Sept 15 — expands the grounds for barring citizens from leaving China and lets authorities withhold notification when national security is invoked, explicitly targeting engineers and researchers holding trade secrets in chips, rare earths, and batteries.


It formalizes a precedent already set in March 2026, when Beijing barred two Manus AI co-founders from leaving during the review of Meta's since-unwound $2B acquisition — but now as standing law rather than an ad hoc case.

Key signal: Cross-border investors and JV partners should treat executive and technical-staff travel risk at Chinese chip/AI portfolio companies as a new, harder-to-predict variable — enforcement scope is untested.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · ROBOTICS
13,361 Orders, a 40% Slide: UBTech Starts Shipping Its Consumer Humanoid Robot

UBTech began shipping UWORLD U1 consumer humanoids on Sept 16, converting a slice of 13,361 refundable-deposit pre-orders into paid deliveries across three tiers priced ¥119,800 to ¥990,000, marketed purely for adult companionship.


The launch lands against a stock down ~40% year-to-date, pressured by capital rotating to newly listed rival Unitree since its Aug 19 debut — even as UBTech lines up a $1B Infini Capital credit facility to fund the scale-up.

Key signal: Pre-order deposits are a weaker demand proof than shipped units — watch whether U1 becomes a real second revenue line or a thin-margin product on a stretched balance sheet.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · ACTIVITY DATA
0.4% and Falling: China's August Retail Sales Mark the Weakest Print of the Slowdown

August retail sales grew just 0.4% y/y, below consensus and the weakest of the current slowdown, while industrial output beat at 5.2% y/y. Fixed asset investment fell 7.2% y/y over the first eight months, a steeper decline than July, led by a 19.9% drop in property investment.


NBS flagged "intensifying" external headwinds and a persistent supply-demand imbalance — the second straight month confirming a widening gap between resilient exports/industrial output and weakening domestic consumption and investment.

Key signal: FAI's decline is accelerating, not stabilizing — raising the bar for the "targeted" stimulus Beijing has signaled but not detailed, and tilting growth further toward export/tariff exposure.
