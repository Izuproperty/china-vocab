STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Tsukuba Residents Push Back on Goodman's ¥3 Trillion AI Data Center Built Next to a Hospital

グッドマン・グループ (Guddoman Gurūpu, Goodman Group) (ASX: GMG), Australia's largest property company by market value, faces organised local opposition to its biggest project in Japan, a データセンター (dēta sentā, data center) campus in つくば市 (Tsukuba-shi, Tsukuba City) that would reach 1 gigawatt and cost about ¥3 trillion ($20 billion) at full build. Reuters reported on October 7 that construction of the first 50-megawatt phase began this year on a 45-hectare site in the Oho district, about 50 km northeast of Tokyo, beside a residential block, a hospital and a nursing home. Residents say municipal documents from 2023 described the project as a logistics warehouse and that the words "data centre" appeared partway through planning. They first grasped its scale when large cranes arrived in February.


No open 住民説明会 (jūmin setsumeikai, residents' briefing) has taken place. Goodman's first sessions came in August, six months into construction, as two invitation-only dinners at a local restaurant attended by a few dozen people. Resident leader Hiroyuki Yanagimachi has collected about 800 signatures for a public town hall and sent Goodman two letters without a reply; his group says it will protest at the site and outside Goodman's Tokyo office if talks do not happen. Goodman says it has exceeded legal requirements and guideline standards and will hold an information meeting once it agrees a format with the city. The company dropped a similar Sydney project after local resistance. It is a member of the 日本データセンター協会 (Nihon Dēta Sentā Kyōkai, Japan Data Center Council), whose May guidelines said consultation should be a two-way forum and should not be a one-sided report.

Investor takeaway: Goodman's 1 GW Tsukuba campus shows that community consent is now a schedule risk for Tokyo-area AI data centers. Developers with sites in industrial zones or in regions favoured by METI's power-data policy face less of it. Watch for an open residents' meeting and any city conditions on phases after the first 50 MW.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Renesas Launches Its First 100V GaN Transistors for the 48V Power Stages Inside AI Server Racks

ルネサスエレクトロニクス (Runesasu Erekutoronikusu, Renesas Electronics) (TSE: 6723) on October 6 released its first 100V enhancement-mode 窒化ガリウム (chikka gariumu, gallium nitride, GaN) power transistors. The four parts have on-resistance between 5 and 1.2 milliohms, no reverse-recovery charge, and package footprints that match silicon MOSFETs, so designers can place them on existing boards. Renesas claims up to 35% lower hard-switching and 63% lower soft-switching figures of merit than comparable GaN devices, 40-70% lower switching losses, and efficiency 1-3 points higher than silicon. The parts ship now with evaluation boards and pair with Renesas's 6 kW 800V-to-48V converter for AI data centers and its DC servo platform for robots.


The product line comes from a February licence of low-voltage eGaN technology from US-based Efficient Power Conversion (EPC). Under that deal Renesas second-sources EPC devices already in production while it builds its own wafer capacity. Renesas's 650V-and-above パワー半導体 (pawā handōtai, power semiconductor) range came with its 2024 purchase of Transphorm, and a new 650V device will appear at the OCP Global Summit from October 12, with 量産 (ryōsan, mass production) planned for mid-2027. Renesas sued Navitas over GaN intellectual property in August.

Investor takeaway: Renesas now sells GaN across both the 650V and 100V classes, which lets it bid for the full 800V-to-48V conversion chain in AI racks. The 100V market already has established GaN suppliers, so design wins with server power-supply makers matter more than the launch. Watch the October 28 results for any GaN revenue disclosure.

STORY 3 · MACRO
Real Wages Rise for an Eighth Straight Month as Base Pay Holds at 3.8%

The 厚生労働省 (Kōsei Rōdōshō, Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare) said on October 7 that 実質賃金 (jisshitsu chingin, real wages) rose 1.5% in August from a year earlier, matching the median forecast. It was the eighth straight monthly gain and the longest run in almost a decade, though slower than July's revised 2.0%. Nominal total cash earnings in the 毎月勤労統計 (Maitsuki Kinrō Tōkei, Monthly Labour Survey) rose 3.8% to ¥311,364, down from 4.3%. 所定内給与 (shoteinai kyūyo, scheduled base pay) rose 3.8% for a second month, overtime pay rose 5.2% after 4.5%, and bonuses were flat after a 5.3% rise in July. The price deflator the ministry uses held at 2.2%, against 3.1% a year earlier.


Two forces produced the gain. Base pay is carrying the spring wage settlements into monthly payslips at a steady pace. The deflator is low because Prime Minister Takaichi's 物価高対策 (bukkadaka taisaku, inflation relief measures), including energy subsidies and tax cuts, have held down the prices households pay. Bloomberg credited those measures for part of the streak. The relief is already fading: the end of water bill subsidies helped push Tokyo core inflation to 2.7% in September, and national CPI on October 23 is likely to show a higher deflator for the September wage data.

Investor takeaway: Base pay at 3.8% supports the BOJ's case for further hikes, but part of the real-wage streak comes from subsidies that are now expiring. Household spending on October 9 and national CPI on October 23 decide whether October or December is the live meeting. A later hike leaves the yen near ¥158 exposed.
