STORY 1 · AI & TECH
NTT and SOMPO Sign a Physical AI Pact for Nursing Care, Starting With Laundry, Meals and Cleaning

日本電信電話 (Nippon Denshin Denwa, NTT) and SOMPOホールディングス (Sonpo Hōrudingusu, SOMPO Holdings) signed a パートナーシップ協定 (pātonāshippu kyōtei, partnership agreement) on November 11 to put フィジカルAI (fijikaru Ē-Ai, physical AI) into 介護 (kaigo, nursing care), and NTT announced it the next day. The two companies define physical AI as cameras, sensors and robots plus the systems that connect them. The first targets are the indirect work around residents: cleaning, laundry, serving and clearing meals, and moving supplies. Direct physical care is not on the initial list. SOMPO supplies its care operations and site know-how. NTT supplies secure networks, a platform that links devices from different makers, and the operating standards it uses for national telecoms infrastructure. The release gives no budget, dates or targets, and the partners still have to agree on use cases, a pilot plan, data and IP handling, and who sells the finished service.


Care homes cannot hire enough staff. The health ministry estimates Japan will need about 2.72 million care workers in FY2040, roughly 570,000 more than were employed in FY2022. Pay is capped in practice by the fee schedule of 介護保険 (kaigo hoken, long-term care insurance), so operators cannot outbid factories and retailers for workers. SOMPO runs one of the country's largest care operators, SOMPO Care, and every hour of laundry or tray service a machine absorbs either lowers its staffing cost or frees a carer for residents. Back-room tasks are repetitive and carry little risk to residents when a robot makes an error, which is why the partners start there.


NTT showed two related pieces of research the same week. On November 11 it said it had built physical AI that uses physics to predict whether a load will collapse or fall before a robot moves, aimed at loading airport baggage and mixed goods. On November 12 it said it can tune its tsuzumi language model for a specific business from small amounts of data. A robot pushing laundry carts down a corridor needs the first, and care records with their specialist vocabulary need the second. The early revenue will be small for a company guiding to ¥994 billion of net profit. NTT gains a reference customer for selling its device platform and network to other operators in a sector that the public insurance system funds at more than ¥10 trillion a year.

Investor takeaway: The pact carries no money, dates or targets yet, so it does not change NTT's numbers. A named pilot site and robot supplier would show SOMPO is ready to spend. The April 2027 care fee revision, now under debate, decides how much operators can recover from technology spending, and that sets the size of this market.

STORY 2 · INDUSTRIAL
Machine Tool Orders Rise 42.5% to ¥204 Billion in October, Above ¥200 Billion for a Second Month, as Foreign Buyers Take 75%

Japanese 工作機械 (kōsaku kikai, machine tool) makers booked ¥204.38 billion of orders in October, up 42.5% from a year earlier, according to preliminary data from the 日本工作機械工業会 (Nihon Kōsaku Kikai Kōgyōkai, Japan Machine Tool Builders' Association, JMTBA) released on November 11. The total fell 8.4% from September's record ¥223.21 billion and stayed above ¥200 billion for a second month. 外需 (gaiju, foreign demand) was ¥152.09 billion, up 41.1% and almost three-quarters of the total, and fell 4.5% on the month. 内需 (naiju, domestic demand) was ¥52.29 billion, up 46.5% on the year and down 18.2% from September. Annual growth slowed from 60.4% in September and came in below the 54.5% Trading Economics had forecast.


Orders have risen year on year every month since July 2025. The total for January to October is ¥1.874 trillion, up 43.2%, already above the ¥1.7 trillion outlook for all of 2026 that Seisanzai Japan reported at the start of the year. Part of the rise comes from the currency. Foreign orders are counted in yen, so the dollar's climb toward ¥160 raises the yen value of an unchanged overseas order, and it also makes Japanese machines cheaper than German, Swiss and Taiwanese ones. The preliminary data do not split orders by region or customer industry. The final release later this month will show how much came from China, North America and Europe, and from the electrical and precision machinery buyers that supply chip and data center equipment.


Listed exposure runs through ファナック (Fanakku, Fanuc), whose CNC controllers sit in most Japanese machines, and through builders such as DMG Mori and Okuma. The 18.2% monthly fall in domestic orders is the weak number. Domestic orders swing from month to month, and September's ¥63.9 billion was unusually high, so October may be a pause. November data are due on December 9. A second weak domestic month would suggest Japanese factories are delaying equipment purchases as the weak yen raises their input costs, which Monday's GDP figure for business investment will also measure.

Investor takeaway: Machine tool demand is strong and mostly foreign, and the weak yen inflates the yen totals. Fanuc and the builders gain on both volume and currency translation. A second month of falling domestic orders on December 9 would be the first sign that Japanese factory capex is cooling.

STORY 3 · MACRO
Week Ahead: Monday's GDP Report Is Expected to Show Growth Slowing to 0.3% Annualized as Household Spending Shrinks

The Cabinet Office publishes its first 速報値 (sokuhōchi, preliminary estimate) of July-September 国内総生産 (kokunai sōseisan, gross domestic product) at 8:50 a.m. on Monday, November 16. A Reuters poll of 16 economists puts growth at 0.1% on the quarter, or 0.3% at a 年率換算 (nenritsu kansan, annualized rate), down from 1.4% in April-June. Ten private think tanks surveyed by Jiji Press average 0.4% annualized, and forecasts collected by Mace News range from -0.3% to +1.5%. A positive number would be the fourth straight quarter of growth.


The mix is weaker than the headline. 個人消費 (kojin shōhi, private consumption), about 55% of GDP, is forecast to fall 0.2% as two one-off boosts end: car purchases after the environmental performance levy was abolished, and air conditioner buying ahead of stricter efficiency standards. Typhoons and heavy rain also cut leisure spending. 設備投資 (setsubi tōshi, business investment) is forecast to rise 1.5% after a 0.9% fall, with data center construction and AI equipment cited. 純輸出 (jun yushutsu, net exports) are expected to subtract 0.4 points after adding 0.5 points in the second quarter.


The Bank of Japan meets on December 17-18 with its policy rate at 1.25%, and many economists in the Reuters poll expect a hike there. Falling consumption gives cautious board members a reason to wait, while 7.1% producer price inflation and a yen near 160 push for a move. Real wages rose for a ninth straight month in September, so a drop in spending alongside rising incomes would support reading it as the end of one-off purchases. The rest of the week brings Nvidia's results on November 17, US time, which will move Advantest, Tokyo Electron and SoftBank Group, and October national inflation data at the end of the week.

Investor takeaway: A reading near 0.3% with consumption down keeps a December hike likely but weakens the case for more than 25 basis points. A negative print would raise the odds the BOJ waits until January, which would push the yen further past 160 and bring another Finance Ministry intervention closer. Business investment above 1.5% would support the AI equipment names whatever the headline shows.
