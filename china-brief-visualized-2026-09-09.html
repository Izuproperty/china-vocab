STORY 1 · ROBOTICS
80% Automated, Zero Orders Booked: Xpeng's Humanoid Walks Off Its Own Production Line

Xpeng activated a new Guangzhou production line on Sept 8 and had its Iron (铁甲) humanoid walk off it autonomously — a first move from prototype demos to production-line manufacturing. Core process automation exceeds 80%, reusing Xpeng's automotive-grade QC systems; the same VLA model drives both Iron and Xpeng's L4 robotaxi stack.


Mass production is still targeted for end-2026, with commercial launch pushed to 2027 — a timeline that has already slipped once. No order books, pricing, or per-unit costs have been disclosed.

Key signal: A manufacturing-readiness milestone, not a demand one — the $900M+ robotics-unit raise now rides on a 2027 commercial date that hasn't been demonstrated at scale.

STORY 2 · AI INFRASTRUCTURE POLICY
6x by 2030, $532 Billion to Get There: MIIT Puts a Number on China's Compute Bet

MIIT's 15th Five-Year Plan (2026-2030) for information/communications targets 9,800 EFLOPS of intelligent computing power by 2030, roughly 6x 2025's 1,590 EFLOPS, plus 1,700 exabytes of advanced storage (up from 540). The plan calls for ¥3.8T (~$532B) in cumulative infrastructure investment and "orderly deployment" of 10,000-GPU and 100,000+-GPU clusters, including inference-specific facilities.


State media frames this as proof China can keep scaling compute despite US chip export controls. The plan doesn't specify which chips fill the clusters, at what utilization, or how much investment is genuinely incremental.

Key signal: A target and investment ceiling, not a funded build-out — watch whether MIIT reports interim EFLOPS progress rather than restating breadth metrics.

STORY 3 · TRADE DATA
25% Export Growth, Imports Outrunning Them: Inside China's August Trade Print

GACC reported August exports up 25% y/y, accelerating from ~24% in July, with the rolling trade surplus nearing $806B. Jan-Aug total trade rose 17.6% y/y to ¥34.78T (~$5.13T): exports +14.6%, imports +22%. State media cites AI-linked electronics demand offsetting weather disruption elsewhere.


Imports growing faster than exports year-to-date narrows the trade surplus's marginal GDP contribution even as its level stays large. The release doesn't break out how much AI-linked demand is chip equipment versus finished electronics.

Key signal: One month's acceleration is a data point, not a trend — September data needs to hold above 20% export growth without a further import/export gap widening to confirm reacceleration.
