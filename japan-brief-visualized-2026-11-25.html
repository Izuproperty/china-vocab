STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Tokyo's New AI Panel Starts Work on Who Pays When an AI Agent Books the Wrong Hotel, With a Report Due by June 2027

A government panel on 規制改革 (kisei kaikaku, regulatory reform) for AI held its first meeting at the デジタル庁 (Dejitaru-chō, Digital Agency) on Tuesday, November 24, to work out who pays when an AIエージェント (AI ējento, AI agent) gets something wrong. The 12 experts sit under the government's council for promoting AI and digital transformation, are chaired by University of Tokyo professor 宍戸常寿 (Shishido Jōji), and aim to compile a report as early as June 2027. Their test cases are ordinary ones. An agent that books travel or does the shopping makes a purchase its user did not intend, or fails to cancel a hotel reservation, and current law does not say whether the user, the service provider or the model developer is liable. State Minister for Digital Transformation 塩崎彰久 (Shiozaki Akihisa) told members the goal is a framework that leaves no 責任の空白 (sekinin no kūhaku, vacuum of responsibility).


The gap exists because Japan's consumer rules assume a person makes the click. The 電子消費者契約法 (Denshi Shōhisha Keiyaku Hō, Electronic Consumer Contract Act) lets a consumer void an online order entered by mistake unless the seller showed a confirmation screen, and an agent that clears confirmation screens by itself fits that rule badly. The panel is the third step in two months. In October the Cabinet Office, METI and the Digital Agency asked companies to name rules that block AI agents and physical AI (Issue #130), and on November 13 officials said they were weighing binding orders and penalties for high-risk AI developers (Issue #167). Those steps put duties on developers. This one is about 損害救済 (songai kyūsai, damage relief) for the person who ends up with the wrong hotel bill.


The split of liability decides which listed companies carry the cost. If it falls on platforms, operators building agent shopping and travel, such as 楽天グループ (Rakuten Gurūpu, Rakuten Group) and LY Corporation, absorb refunds, and card issuers handle more disputed charges. If it falls on developers, the cost lands mostly on foreign model providers serving Japan. Under any split, non-life insurers gain a product to sell, and Tokio Marine already reports cyber claims tripling in October (Issue #175). A June 2027 report means no binding rule before fiscal 2027, so until then each service sets liability in its own terms of use, and those terms are where investors can see how much risk a platform has chosen to keep.

Investor takeaway: Nothing binds before mid-2027, so agent services in Japan launch under their own terms of use. Platforms running agent shopping and travel, and the card issuers behind them, carry the most risk if liability lands on providers; non-life insurers gain a new line of cover under any outcome. Watch whether the panel extends the Electronic Consumer Contract Act's mistake rule to orders placed by agents.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Ibiden Ends Tuesday Untraded at Its Daily Limit and Murata Jumps 14.9% as the Nikkei Regains 3.9% on Huang's Return

Tokyo reopened on Tuesday, November 24, after Monday's Labor Thanksgiving Day holiday and priced in Nvidia's 31% jump on Jensen Huang's reinstatement in one session. The 日経平均株価 (Nikkei Heikin Kabuka, Nikkei 225) rose more than 2,800 points in the morning and closed at 58,644.63, up 2,207.98 or 3.91%. The Topix gained 2.47% to 3,764.88, its first rise in six sessions. Market participants said money that had left AI shares during the previous week's selloff came back after the US rebound, and short covering added to the move.


Component and packaging suppliers led, ahead of the equipment makers. 村田製作所 (Murata Seisakusho, Murata Manufacturing), the largest maker of the multilayer ceramic capacitors packed onto AI server boards, rose 14.89% to its daily limit. アドバンテスト (Adobantesuto, Advantest) gained 12.45%, and Furukawa Electric, which sells optical fiber to data centers, rose 11.54%. イビデン (Ibiden), which gets about 30% of its sales from Nvidia through FC-BGA package substrates, never traded at all. Buy orders stacked up at its ストップ高 (sutoppu daka, limit-up price) with too few sellers to match them, so the stock showed only a 買い気配 (kai kehai, bid quote) until the close, and those orders carry into this morning's open.


Ibiden is the cleanest test of the rebound because its own numbers were never the problem. On October 30 it reported record first-half operating profit of ¥60.1 billion, up 85%, and raised its full-year guide to ¥133 billion, then fell 15% on the day and hit its lower limit on November 5 as investors questioned AI capex and its pricing assumptions (Issue #163). CEO 河島浩二 (Kawashima Kōji) has said substrate supply is tight enough to become a bottleneck for chip production. Tuesday's move changed sentiment and left the estimates where they were. Strategists cited in market reports said Huang's return does nothing about restrictions on data-center construction or the profitability of AI spending, and one said uncertainty over tighter China rules could last six months to a year. If Ibiden opens higher today and holds, buyers are paying for the substrate shortage. If it opens and fades, Tuesday was mostly short covering.

Investor takeaway: Tuesday paid the suppliers with the most direct Nvidia exposure, Ibiden and Murata, more than the equipment makers. Ibiden's first trade this morning shows whether buyers will pay up for a substrate maker that fell 15% on record results. The rally came from positioning, and the questions over data-center restrictions and China are still open.

STORY 3 · MACRO
Factory PMI Rises to 53.7 on the Fastest Export Orders Since 2018, but Services Slow to 50.5 and Pull the Composite to a Six-Month Low

The au Jibun Bank flash 製造業 (seizōgyō, manufacturing) PMI rose to 53.7 in November from 53.3, above the 53.5 forecast. Output returned to growth and new orders rose at a solid pace, led by a sharp gain in 新規輸出受注 (shinki yushutsu juchū, new export orders), mostly from other Asian economies. Across the private sector, new export business grew at its fastest rate since January 2018. The survey matches October's trade data, when exports hit a record ¥11.77 trillion on a 49.7% jump in chips (Issue #173).


Services pulled the other way. The services PMI fell to 50.5 from 51.7, and the composite dropped to 51.5 from 52.1, a six-month low. Annabel Fiddes of S&P Global Market Intelligence said the private sector lost momentum as service activity slowed. Confidence fell in both sectors, and firms blamed rising costs, 人手不足 (hitode busoku, labor shortages) and a possible slowdown in consumer spending. Price pressure eased. Input cost inflation fell to an eight-month low and output price inflation to its weakest since March, though both stay high by historical standards.


The split matters for the 日本銀行 (Nihon Ginkō, BOJ) meeting on December 17-18, where 86% of economists in a Reuters poll expect a hike to 1.50% from 1.25%. Strong factories support the view that July-September's 0.1% annualized contraction was temporary. Weaker services and slower output-price inflation argue for waiting, because the BOJ wants wage gains to show up in service prices, and core-core CPI already slowed to 2.2% in October (Issue #175). The bond market gets its next test at 10:30 a.m. today, when the Ministry of Finance (MoF) auctions about ¥300 billion of 40-year JGBs maturing in March 2066; the September sale cleared at 4.125%. Tokyo CPI follows on Friday.

Investor takeaway: The manufacturing beat supports the BOJ's case that the third-quarter contraction was temporary, but softer services and slower output-price inflation give the board a reason to wait past December. A weak result in today's 40-year auction would push super-long yields higher, and Friday's Tokyo CPI services component is the next number to check.
