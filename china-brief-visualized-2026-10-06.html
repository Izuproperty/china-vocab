STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Reflection Launches Beam and Measures It Against Zhipu's GLM-5.2, Making a Chinese Model the Yardstick for America's Open-Weight Pitch

Nvidia-backed Reflection AI unveiled Beam on October 5: a 501B-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 23B active parameters, 23.8T training tokens and a 1M-token context. It claims parity with Zhipu's GLM-5.2 on reasoning at three to four times less inference compute.


Beam targets regulated US buyers who cannot run Chinese weights. Those buyers were never Zhipu customers, so the pressure lands on cost-driven US adopters of Chinese open models, where a cheaper-to-serve rival narrows China's price edge.

Key signal: A $25 billion US lab chose a Chinese model as its benchmark, and if Beam's unpublished numbers hold, Zhipu's overseas API business faces a substitute that serves similar answers with 40% fewer active parameters.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH
Golden Week Travel Agents Take Off, Fliggy AI Use Up 2.8x and DeepTrip Traffic Triples, as Scholars Flag Unlabeled Paid Placements

Fliggy's AI trip-planner use rose 2.8 times over National Day versus the prior month, and Tongcheng's DeepTrip agent saw peak-hour visits more than triple on October 1. Fliggy Bangbang now plans, books and handles changes in one flow.


An October 4 report found platforms rank paid partners in AI answers without ad labels, and one AI-picked flight cost about 10 yuan more than a rival's fare. China's 2022 algorithm rules do not explicitly cover paid placement inside agent answers.

Key signal: Agents give travel platforms control of both the recommendation and the sale, so a labeling rule for paid placements inside AI answers would directly limit how Trip.com, Tongcheng and Alibaba monetize ranking.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
China's Fund Closures Head for an Eight-Year High: 256 Liquidated in 2026 and 46 More on Notice

Bloomberg data show 256 public funds liquidated so far in 2026, 46 more on notice and 300+ on pace for the year, the most since 2018. Nearly 100 closures came from sponsor-backed funds under a 2023 three-year rule.


Active stock funds returned -0.8% a year over five years. This year's gains sit in chip stocks (STAR 50 +14%, CSI 300 about -6%), and small funds that missed them fall below the 50-million-yuan floor while launches keep rising.

Key signal: Product count is up 10% while closures hit an eight-year high, so managers are churning shelves under fee and performance pressure, which squeezes sub-scale active-equity houses hardest.
