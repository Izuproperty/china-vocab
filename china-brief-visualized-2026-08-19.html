STORY 1 · ROBOTICS / IPO
8,289x to 4x: Unitree's Shanghai Debut Lands Beside a 300-Company Reality Check

Unitree Robotics listed on Shanghai's STAR Market Wednesday — the first pure-play humanoid maker on a mainland exchange — after an IPO priced at ¥150.80/share (~$9B valuation) that was oversubscribed 8,289x by retail investors. Gray-market bids ran ~170% above the offer price pre-open, and Hyperliquid perpetuals implied up to 4x upside; no exchange-confirmed close was available as of this issue.


The debut coincides with the 2026 World Robot Conference (Aug 19–23), which 300+ companies are using to show commercial-readiness — not just choreography. Unitree's own prospectus concedes current humanoids can't yet reliably do complex, unstructured work, and estimates suggest up to half of this year's production may end up as data-collection units rather than revenue-generating deployments.

Key signal: A valuation priced well above sector P/E norms now needs commercial evidence from WRC deployments to justify itself — the gap between speculative pricing and disclosed product limitations is what to track next.

STORY 2 · EARNINGS / FOUNDATION MODELS
Up 3%, Not Enough: Baidu's AI Cloud Can't Yet Outrun Its Ad Slide

Baidu's Q2 2026 revenue missed estimates as core search-advertising weakness outweighed AI cloud and product gains. AI-application revenue reached ¥2.5B for the quarter — up only 3% y/y, a soft number given the scale of capital directed at ERNIE Bot, AI cloud, and Apollo Go.


The pattern is familiar across China's legacy internet platforms: generative AI opens new revenue lines while accelerating decline in the ad business that funded expansion. The question for Baidu is timing — AI cloud and Apollo Go are growing, but not yet fast enough to offset the core-business slide.

Key signal: Investors are likely to keep discounting Baidu's AI narrative until cloud/AV revenue growth clearly outpaces the advertising decline it's meant to offset.

STORY 3 · MONETARY POLICY / PAYMENTS
10 to 30: PBOC Triples Digital Yuan Operators in Four Months

The PBOC authorized eight more banks as e-CNY operators on Aug 17, lifting the total to 30 — up from 10 before an April 2026 batch added 12. The new cohort (Ping An, Hengfeng, China Bohai, plus five city commercial banks) is connected to the central-side system but customer rollout depends on each bank's own readiness.


The move is explicit follow-through on the PBOC's 2026–2030 reform plan (Aug 10), which also covers CIPS expansion and RMB internationalization — read alongside record panda-bond issuance and Deutsche Bank's new Frankfurt RMB clearing role, this looks like sustained infrastructure-building rather than a discrete policy signal.

Key signal: Operator count is a capacity metric — the actual catalyst to watch is transaction volume and merchant acceptance once these banks go live, data not yet available.
