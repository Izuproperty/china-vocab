STORY 1 · AI & TECH
SoftBank Group Has Lost Half Its Market Value Since June and Now Trails Hitachi and Recruit

ソフトバンクグループ (Sofutobanku Gurūpu, SoftBank Group) fell 6.9% in Tokyo on October 22. The stock is now 50% below its June 2 record and down more than 30% in October, the worst performer in the Nikkei 225 this month. Its 時価総額 (jika sōgaku, market capitalization) of about ¥24.7 trillion ($157 billion) is smaller than Hitachi's or Recruit Holdings', four months after it briefly passed Toyota as Japan's most valuable listed company. Chip stocks fell with it: 東京エレクトロン (Tōkyō Erekutoron, Tokyo Electron) dropped 4.1% after Lam Research slipped in after-hours trading on worries that Chinese customers had bought equipment early, and the Nikkei was down 0.8% in morning trade.


SoftBank pays for its AI positions with borrowed money, and the price of that money has risen. It has committed about $64.6 billion to OpenAI for a stake of roughly 13%, agreed to pay $5.4 billion for ABB's robotics business and $3.1 billion for DigitalBridge, and in September sold $11.1 billion of bonds, the largest junk bond sale on record, with dollar coupons as high as 9.75%. Fitch rates the company BB+, one notch below 投資適格 (tōshi tekikaku, investment grade), and S&P has its rating on negative outlook. Five-year 信用デフォルト・スワップ (shin'yō deforuto suwappu, credit default swap) spreads reached their highest level in almost four years on October 20, around 400 basis points against about 280 in June. When CoreWeave's bonds fell to distressed prices last week, investors sold AI companies that rely on debt, and SoftBank is the largest of them listed in Tokyo.


The pressure feeds on itself. SoftBank's 純資産価値 (jun shisan kachi, net asset value) depends mostly on its OpenAI and Arm holdings, and OpenAI now competes with cheaper Chinese models that perform about as well. A lower OpenAI valuation raises SoftBank's loan-to-value ratio, and a higher ratio makes its next bond more expensive. Kazuyuki Muramatsu of Nagomi Capital said a turn in sentiment on AI growth could produce valuation losses at the Vision Fund and raise SoftBank's funding costs. The reported talks to buy Allied Universal for up to $18 billion excluding debt would add another large cash need on top of these.

Investor takeaway: SoftBank Group (TSE: 9984) now trades as a leveraged claim on OpenAI's private valuation, and its CDS spread measures that stress more directly than the share price. Watch the November results for the loan-to-value ratio and any OpenAI markdown, whether the Allied Universal talks go ahead and how they would be financed, and any action by S&P. A rating cut would raise the cost of every refinancing SoftBank has ahead of it.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Disco Posts a 52% Profit Jump and a Record Margin, Then Guides to an HBM Pause for October-December

ディスコ (Disuko, Disco), the Tokyo company that makes most of the world's saws and grinders for cutting and thinning chips, reported on October 22 that July-September net sales rose 36% to ¥142.75 billion and operating profit rose 52% to ¥67.61 billion. Both beat consensus of ¥139.07 billion and ¥63.08 billion. Net income of ¥46.15 billion came in just under the ¥47.06 billion estimate. Quarterly 出荷額 (shukka-gaku, shipment value) reached ¥148.6 billion against a plan of ¥141.0 billion, and gross margin hit a quarterly record of 71.7% because higher volumes spread the fixed cost of making blades and grinding wheels. For April-September, sales rose 32% to ¥257.1 billion and the interim dividend rises to ¥186 from ¥129.


The outlook is flatter. Disco forecast April-December sales of ¥389.5 billion and operating profit of ¥174.7 billion at ¥151 per dollar, which implies about ¥132 billion of sales and ¥58 billion of profit in October-December. Shipments are guided only slightly higher at that rate and above ¥150 billion at today's rate near ¥158. Memory falls from just under 40% of shipments to about 30% because HBM orders pause for a quarter. Each HBM stack needs 研削 (kensaku, grinding) to thin each of its eight to 16 DRAM dies, so HBM spending shows up quickly in Disco's book. Management called the pause a quarterly fluctuation, said underlying demand has not changed, and said it cannot yet explain the January-March quarter. Gross margin is guided down by just under 2 points.


Other lines rise. Logic and CPU customers increase orders in October-December, and photonics work for co-packaged optics grew in July-September and is expected to grow again. Disco's China shipments fell after a strong April-June but are forecast to rise, and management said demand from Chinese OSATs shows no sudden change. That matters on a day when Tokyo Electron fell on fears of early Chinese buying. Panel-level packaging and glass substrates, two 先端パッケージ (sentan pakkēji, advanced packaging) formats that would need new Disco tools, are still in customer evaluation.

Investor takeaway: Disco (TSE: 6146) shows AI packaging demand still growing in logic while HBM orders take a one-quarter break, and the yen near ¥158, against a ¥151 assumption, should lift shipments above ¥150 billion. The guide puts weight on memory makers' January-March orders. Watch SK Hynix and Micron capex comments on HBM4 in the coming weeks: if HBM grinder orders do not return in early 2027, memory stays near 30% of shipments and margins stay below the July-September record.

STORY 3 · MACRO
83% of Economists Now Expect a December BOJ Hike as September Core CPI Is Seen Back Above 2%

A Bloomberg survey published on October 22 found that 83% of economists who follow the 日本銀行 (Nippon Ginkō, Bank of Japan) expect its next rate hike at the December 17-18 meeting. A month earlier 35% picked December and January was the most common answer. None of the respondents expect a move at the October 29-30 meeting. The share expecting a hike every three months rose to 67% from 46%, while the median forecast for the peak rate this cycle stayed at 1.75%. About two-thirds said the government has softened its resistance to higher rates. MUFG strategists said the prime minister's office probably does not welcome further hikes but will tacitly accept them for now.


The September national CPI is due at 8:30 a.m. Tokyo time today. A Reuters poll of 15 economists expects 生鮮食品を除く総合 (seisen shokuhin o nozoku sōgō, core CPI excluding fresh food) at 2.1%, up from 1.7% in August. That would be the first reading above the 2% target since December, after seven months below it. The index that also excludes energy is expected near 2.3% from 1.9%. Tokyo's September data, published three weeks earlier, already showed core at 2.7% and the ex-food-and-energy measure at 3.0%. Expiring electricity subsidies and energy costs tied to the Middle East conflict account for much of the rise. Reuters sources said the BOJ will likely raise its inflation forecast for next fiscal year at the October meeting.


The October decision will turn on wording. The BOJ has never stated that 基調的な物価上昇率 (kichōteki na bukka jōshōritsu, underlying inflation) has reached 2% since it adopted the target in 2013. Governor Ueda has said it is very close, and Deputy Governor Himino warned on October 21 about acting too late. The two September dissenters, Takaichi appointees Toichiro Asada and Ayano Sato, argued that inflation was still below target, and a 2.1% core print would weaken that argument. Standard Chartered still has January as its base case because consumption remains weak. The 1.25% policy rate already sits inside the BOJ's estimated 中立金利 (chūritsu kinri, neutral rate) range of 1.1% to 2.5%.

Investor takeaway: If core CPI prints at 2.1% or higher and the October 30 statement says underlying inflation has reached 2%, a December hike becomes the base case for nearly everyone, which supports two-year JGB yields and gives the yen some help near ¥158. A miss driven by subsidies would leave the dissenters room to argue for January. Watch the outlook report's fiscal 2027 core forecast and whether Asada and Sato dissent again.
