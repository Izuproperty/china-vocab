STORY 1 · AI & TECH · ROBOTICS
1,079 Units, ¥1.4 Billion Backlog: China's Humanoid Robots Start Answering the Revenue Question

UBTech's cumulative 2025 humanoid shipments reached 1,079 units against an order backlog above ¥1.4B from Foxconn, BYD, Geely, and Airbus, alongside 2026-27 capacity targets of 5,000 and 10,000 units and a 40-43% gross-margin goal. Unitree and AgiBot together are separately targeting 75,000+ units of combined annual capacity — more than the rest of the world's announced supply combined.


The investor-relevant shift is from shipment-growth headlines toward whether backlogs convert into recognized revenue at the margins promised, and whether industrial customers are placing repeat orders rather than one-off pilot purchases.

Key signal: Order-book disclosure, not shipment counts, is now the metric that separates a commercializing sector from a demo-stage one.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · CHIPS
Twelve by Year-End: A Huawei-Linked Startup's Domestic DUV Scanner Enters Testing at SMIC

Yuliangsheng, a Shanghai state-backed startup affiliated with Shenzhen's SiCarrier, is testing a domestic immersion DUV lithography scanner at SMIC and on Huawei's own production lines, with a revised end-2026 target of ~12 systems, up from 5. The tool targets 28nm-class output, reaching toward 7nm via multi-patterning.


This addresses supply security at mature nodes, not leading-edge competitiveness — key components including Zeiss projection lenses reportedly remain sourced indirectly, and prior domestic DUV timelines have slipped before.

Key signal: Yield and throughput data from SMIC's testing phase will matter more than the announcement or the revised unit count.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
3.8% and Climbing: China's Factory-Gate Prices Post Their Strongest Gain of the Cycle

August PPI rose 3.8% year-on-year, beating the 3.6% consensus and accelerating from July's 3.5% — the strongest print of the current cycle, following a factory-gate deflationary stretch that persisted for much of the past three years. CPI also accelerated to 0.8% from 0.5%, with core CPI up 1.0%.


A sustained rebound would ease industrial-margin pressure but narrow the PBOC's room to ease further. One month of data doesn't establish a trend, and the sharp jump in transport costs suggests part of the move reflects energy pass-through rather than broad demand strength.

Key signal: September and October prints, plus the PBOC's next policy communication, will show whether this is durable reflation or another false start.
