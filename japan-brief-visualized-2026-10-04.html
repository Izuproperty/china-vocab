STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Hitachi and Fanuc Pair Up to Sell Factory Robots That Learn on the Line

Hitachi and Fanuc signed a strategic partnership on September 30 to build and sell physical AI for factories. Hitachi supplies HMAX Industry software and its own edge AI chips; Fanuc supplies robots and controllers.


Trials at Hitachi's Ibaraki plants cover picking irregular parts and line changeovers. Global sales start from fiscal 2027, and no financial terms were disclosed.

Investor takeaway: Hitachi earns recurring software fees only where its AI runs on robots that are already installed, and Fanuc has the largest installed base among industrial robot makers. Fanuc gets a software and edge-chip partner without building those itself. Revenue starts in fiscal 2027 at the earliest, so the checks before then are cycle-time results from the Ibaraki trials and the first outside customer the pair names.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Japan's Chip Equipment Sales Hit a Record ¥598 Billion in August as Memory Makers Spend on HBM

SEAJ data show Japan-made chip equipment sales at ¥597.9 billion in August (three-month average), up 47.4% from a year earlier and the highest since records began in 1986.


HBM lines for AI accelerators need more tools per wafer than standard DRAM. August's pace annualizes to about ¥7.2 trillion, above SEAJ's ¥6.55 trillion forecast for fiscal 2026.

Investor takeaway: Japanese tool orders are running ahead of the industry's own forecast, and HBM spending accounts for most of the gap. That points to upside for fiscal 2026 guidance at Tokyo Electron, Screen and Disco. Because the growth is concentrated in memory, capex updates from SK Hynix, Samsung and Micron will move these stocks as much as Japanese data does.

STORY 3 · MACRO & BOJ · WEEK AHEAD
Week Ahead: Takaichi Opens the Diet on Monday, Then Ueda Speaks With October Hike Odds Cut in Half

Takaichi gives her Diet policy speech on Monday; a Nikkei-reported draft caps new JGB issuance at ¥40 trillion next fiscal year. Ueda speaks on Tuesday at 15:35 JST.


Swaps-implied odds of an October hike fell by about half after the October 1 Summary of Opinions. Wages (Wed), the Sakura report (Thu) and household spending (Fri) follow.

Investor takeaway: Monday's speech sets the fiscal backdrop for long-dated JGBs, and Tuesday's speech sets October odds at the short end. Banks benefit if Ueda keeps October open. Exporters benefit if he leans toward December and the yen weakens past ¥159, though that raises the risk of intervention. Wednesday's wage print will decide which reading holds into the weekend.
