STORY 1 · AI & TECH
SoftBank Pays Up to 9.75% to Fund Its Last OpenAI Check, and the Money Moves This Week

SoftBank priced $10bn of dollar notes and €1bn of euro notes on September 24, the largest high-yield corporate deal on record. The bonds settle September 29 and fund the final OpenAI tranche due October 1, taking the total commitment to $64.6bn for about 13%.


The company has issued $14.6bn of high-yield debt this year, 63% of the Asia-Pacific and Japan total. Its CDS rose above 400bp from about 280bp in June, and the shares fell 3.2% on September 25 as the Nikkei rose.

Investor takeaway: The OpenAI stake now has to compound faster than 9 to 10% a year to add to net asset value; loan-to-value at the next results is the number to watch.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Mitsubishi Chemical Moves to 6-Inch GaN Substrates as AI Data Centers Hunt for Power Efficiency

Mitsubishi Chemical will sample 6-inch gallium nitride substrates for power chips by March 2027. It mass-produces 4-inch substrates at Tsukuba, runs a crystal pilot line with Japan Steel Works in Muroran, and is developing 8-inch.


Native GaN substrates let power chips handle higher voltages in a smaller area than GaN-on-silicon, which suits AI server power supplies and EV drivetrains. A 6-inch size fits many existing power-chip lines.

Investor takeaway: A small line inside a large chemicals group; a capex decision and a named device customer are the milestones that would matter for the stock.

STORY 3 · MACRO & BOJ
Trump and Bessent Say the Yen Is Too Weak, With the Tankan Due Thursday

Katayama said Trump raised the yen's weakness at the September 22 Takaichi summit, and Bessent said the two finance chiefs discussed "the desirability of a strong yen." USD/JPY eased from ¥158.60 to about ¥158, and the 10-year JGB touched 3.115%.


The October 1 Tankan is expected to show large manufacturers at +26 (up 4) and large non-manufacturers at +36 (down 1). August output and retail sales arrive Wednesday.

Investor takeaway: US backing makes intervention near ¥160 easier and gives the BOJ cover to hike again in late October; a firm Tankan adds to that case and weighs on yen-driven exporter upgrades.
