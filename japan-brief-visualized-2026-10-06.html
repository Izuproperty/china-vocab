STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Noetra's Tamba Opens Nikkei's GenAI Summit by Staking Japan's AI Case on Factory Know-How

ノエトラ (Noetora, Noetra), the government-funded foundation-model company formerly called 日本AI基盤モデル開発 (Nihon AI Kiban Moderu Kaihatsu, Japan AI Foundation Model Development), opened Nikkei's three-day 生成AIサミット (Seisei AI Samitto, GenAI/SUM 2026) in Tokyo on October 5 with a keynote from president 丹波廣寅 (Tanba Hironobu, Hironobu Tamba). His argument was that the 業務ノウハウ (gyōmu nouhau, operational know-how) inside Japanese companies is the country's 勝ち筋 (kachisuji, winning path). In a September 29 interview with ITmedia he put the case for building at home bluntly: a foreign model "could be cut off at any time," and second- or third-tier models produce second- or third-tier results.


Noetra is building one multimodal 母艦モデル (bokan moderu, mothership model) that covers language, images, audio, video, sensor data and a world model of physical space. Member companies will fine-tune it or cut smaller models from it through 知識蒸留 (chishiki jōryū, knowledge distillation) for robots, vehicles and plant control, and Noetra plans to release open weights. The company has 44 shareholders, led by SoftBank, NEC (TSE: 6701), Sony Group (TSE: 6758) and Honda (TSE: 7267) and including the three megabanks and Nippon Steel; about 28 of them are manufacturers. METI has allocated ¥387.3 billion for fiscal 2026 and up to roughly ¥1 trillion through fiscal 2030, released year by year after a stage-gate review. The large model is due by 2028 and commercial deployment around 2030.

Investor takeaway: Noetra's advantage rests on proprietary manufacturing data from its 44 shareholders, not on model size. Watch the fiscal 2027 allocation in December's budget and any member that commits process data by name. Listed exposure runs through SoftBank, NEC, Sony Group and Honda.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Rapidus Signs 17 Design Houses to Bring Customers to Its 2nm Fab

ラピダス (Rapidasu, Rapidus) on October 5 launched Rapidus CORE (Collaborative Open Rapid Ecosystem), a partner program for its 2nm foundry in Chitose, Hokkaido. The first phase names 17 Design Solution Associates that will provide 設計支援 (sekkei shien, design support) to companies that want Rapidus to make their chips. The list includes the two dominant chip-design software vendors, Synopsys and Cadence; Japanese firms 大日本印刷 (Dai Nippon Insatsu, Dai Nippon Printing, TSE: 7912), TOPPAN and Toshiba Information Systems; Indian engineering-services groups Infosys, Wipro and HCL; and Malaysian design houses SkyeChip and Oppstar. Rapidus says later phases will add EDA tools and licensed IP.


Rapidus still targets volume 2nm 量産 (ryōsan, mass production) in the second half of fiscal 2027, operating profit around fiscal 2030 and a listing around fiscal 2031. It has not named a volume customer. Design cost is the obstacle: a 2nm chip costs about $725 million to design against roughly $48 million at 28nm, and few Japanese chip buyers have ever taped out at the leading edge or employ the engineers to do it. METI has filed another ¥150 billion capital request for Rapidus in the fiscal 2027 budget, on top of state support reported near ¥3 trillion.

Investor takeaway: Rapidus is outsourcing customer acquisition to firms that earn fees when clients tape out. The signal to watch is a named 2nm tape-out from a CORE partner before the fiscal 2027 budget closes in December. Listed read-through: DNP (TSE: 7912) and TOPPAN Holdings (TSE: 7911).

STORY 3 · MACRO
BOJ's Uchida Calls AI a Big Demand Shock and Says It Could Raise the Neutral Rate

Deputy Governor 内田眞一 (Uchida Shin'ichi, Shinichi Uchida) opened the ECONDAT 2026 Fall Meeting on October 5 with remarks titled "AI, Big Data and Monetary Policy." He called AI a large positive 需要ショック (juyō shokku, demand shock) that is lifting both activity and prices. Rising AI stocks have made financial conditions easier, he said, while heavy bond issuance by AI companies is pushing long-term yields up. Over time AI could raise productivity and capital investment and so move the 自然利子率 (shizen rishiritsu, natural rate of interest), which together with inflation sets the 中立金利 (chūritsu kinri, neutral interest rate). He added that the BOJ has no clear answer yet on how large the effect will be or when it arrives, and warned that AI could quickly make some white-collar 人的資本 (jinteki shihon, human capital) obsolete.


The policy rate is 1.25% after the 7-2 vote to hike on September 18. USD/JPY slipped to about ¥157.5 after the speech before returning toward ¥158. The Nikkei 225 rose 2.5% to briefly top 70,000, its highest since early July, led by Tokyo Electron (+5.7%), Advantest (+4.5%) and SoftBank Group (+3.7%) after softer US jobs data. Markets price roughly a one-in-four chance of another hike at the October 29-30 meeting. Governor Ueda addresses the National Securities Convention this afternoon.

Investor takeaway: A BOJ deputy governor now treats AI as a reason the neutral rate may be higher, which gives the board room to keep hiking past 1.25%. The same logic makes BOJ policy more sensitive to AI stock swings. Watch Ueda's speech today and the October 30 Outlook Report.
