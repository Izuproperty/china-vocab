STORY 1 · AI & TECH
DeepSeek Open-Sources a CUDA Substitute Tuned on a 128-Chip Ascend 950 Supernode

DeepSeek released TileLang, a kernel programming language, plus Ascend ports of DeepGEMM, FlashMLA, DeepEP, TileKernels and DeepSelect on September 30. Huawei supported the work, which was tuned on a 128-chip Ascend 950 supernode.


Rewriting CUDA kernels is the main cost of leaving Nvidia. Free, working Ascend kernels cut that cost for every Chinese buyer, and DeepSeek needs them itself for an Inner Mongolia site designed for at least 160,000 Ascend chips.

Key signal: The software reason to stay on Nvidia is getting weaker in China. Chip supply and per-chip performance now set how fast Ascend can replace it.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH
China Passes 700 Million Generative AI Users, but New Sign-Ups Have Slowed by About Two-Thirds

CNNIC counted more than 700 million generative AI users at end-June 2026, above 50% penetration. A year earlier it counted 515 million and 36.5%, after the user base doubled in six months.


Roughly 266 million users joined in the first half of 2025 and about 185 million in the following twelve months. Assistant usage frequency more than doubled and compute capacity rose 177%, so growth has moved from new users to heavier use.

Key signal: User counts are nearing saturation. Revenue growth now depends on pricing per user and enterprise API sales, which favors usage-billed firms over free consumer chatbots.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
Factory PMI Climbs Back to 50.1 on Output, While Services and Construction Beat Forecasts

The official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.1 from 49.8, ending two months of contraction. Production rose 1.3 points to 51.7 while new orders eased to 50.5, and small firms stayed below 50 at 48.9.


Non-manufacturing rose to 50.2 against a 49.3 forecast, with construction at 50.3. The PBOC's PSL cut and mortgage subsidy landed a day before the release, so easing arrived as activity stabilized.

Key signal: Supply recovered and demand did not. Broad rate cuts look less likely this quarter unless October weakens, and Golden Week spending is the next read.
