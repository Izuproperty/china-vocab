STORY 1 · AI & TECH · HUMANOID ROBOTICS
Meta Built a Humanoid Prototype at Fourier's Shanghai Factory in About Three Months, and Work on a Second Version Began in September

The Information reported on October 31 that Meta built a humanoid prototype called Max with Shanghai's Fourier in roughly three months. Max is about 1.5 meters and 48 kg, runs on Nvidia's Jetson Thor and has five-fingered hands; a Meta robotics vice president spent almost two months at Fourier's factory.


Max 2 work began in September with a new torso, better hands and stair sensors. Meta says the units are for internal research and will not be sold, and no contract value or unit count was reported.

Key signal: Fourier now has a US platform company as a disclosed build partner, which supports a contract-engineering revenue line for Chinese humanoid makers, but US security scrutiny of Chinese robots in homes could push any commercial version to another manufacturer.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · VIDEO MODELS
Kling AI Closes Its ¥19.8 Billion Round After a Middle East Investor Fails to Pay and Kingdee Takes Part of Its Share

Kuaishou said on October 30 that Kling's capital increase closed at ¥19.80 billion ($2.98 billion), against a ¥20.45 billion ($3.07 billion) cap. Two subscribers, including Middle East-linked BFI MIDCO with $80 million unpaid, dropped out, and Kingdee, MetaX-named entities and domestic funds took their places.


Lucky Labs holds 66.68% of the equity and 52.45% of the votes; CEO Gai Kun holds 3.02% of the equity and 23.72% of the votes. Q2 revenue topped ¥850 million ($128 million), and Bloomberg put the post-money valuation at ¥122.8 billion ($18.5 billion).

Key signal: Kling has close to $3 billion to spend on compute before a planned 2027 Hong Kong filing, and the investor base is now more onshore, but about three quarters of the votes sit with Lucky Labs and Gai, so the IPO test is margin after compute and whether public buyers accept the ¥122.8 billion ($18.5 billion) mark.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · ACTIVITY DATA
October's Factory PMI Falls to 49.3 as Small Firms, Consumer Goods and Construction Contract While High-Tech Output Keeps Expanding

The official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.3 from 50.1, below the 49.9 consensus, with new orders at 49.4 and production at 49.8. Non-manufacturing fell to 49.7, and construction dropped to 48.0 with its new orders at 40.0.


Input prices fell 4.0 points to 56.8 and ex-factory prices 2.3 points to 51.7. Small firms fell to 47.5 while large firms held at 50.2, and high-tech manufacturing stayed at 51.3. NBS blamed holiday demand pulled forward.

Key signal: Weakness is concentrated in small firms, consumer goods and construction while the AI-hardware and equipment chain keeps expanding; with the PBOC moving to an overnight policy rate on November 2, any easing may arrive through that rate, and the private PMI and October trade data will show whether orders keep falling.
