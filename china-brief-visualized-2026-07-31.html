STORY 1 · AI & TECH / FOUNDATION MODELS
The License Is the Story: Moonshot Opens Kimi K3's 2.8 Trillion Parameters — With Strings Attached

Moonshot published Kimi K3's full weights on July 27, eleven days after its API debut — 2.8T parameters, 1M-token context, scoring 57 on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index (roughly on par with Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5). The license is near-MIT until revenue crosses $20M thresholds, at which point resellers need a separate commercial deal and large-scale products must display "Kimi K3" — a structure built to capture leverage as adoption scales, unlike DeepSeek's unrestricted release.


Washington's response has hardened in parallel: distillation and export-circumvention allegations from OSTP and Treasury reportedly triggered a BIS investigation, with no technical evidence published by either side. The public weights make independent verification possible for the first time — cutting both ways for Moonshot's pending Hong Kong IPO.

Key signal: Open weights don't mean open-ended use — Moonshot's revenue-tiered license is a template worth watching as more Chinese labs open-source frontier models.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH / AUTONOMOUS DRIVING REGULATION
Four Years in a Grey Zone: MIIT Enforces the Ban on Blue ADAS Indicator Lights

MIIT is strictly enforcing the existing GB4785-2019 lighting standard — white, red, yellow, amber only — effectively banning the blue "Little Blue Light" that signals active driver assistance. New filings featuring the lamp are barred starting next month. Li Auto popularized the design on the 2022 L9; Aito, Xpeng, Nio and BYD followed, all technically out of compliance since the standard was finalized.


The more interesting fact is the four-year gap between rule and enforcement, not any single OEM's exposure. Remediation for cars already on the road — possibly an OTA fix — remains under study, an unbudgeted compliance cost as Beijing works toward broader L3/L4 safety rules due by July 2027.

Key signal: Fast-moving EV design conventions can outrun China's formal standards process for years — and enforcement, when it lands, arrives abruptly rather than in phases.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO / FISCAL POLICY
Incremental, Not Big-Bang: Politburo Pledges Faster Fiscal Spending, Sets October Plenum

The July 30 Politburo readout pledged to accelerate fiscal spending and bond-proceeds use under a "moderately accommodative" monetary stance, following Q2 GDP of 4.3% y/y — the slowest since late 2022, though H1's 4.7% held inside the 4.5–5.0% target band. October 2026 was set for the Fifth Plenum, notable a year ahead of the next Party Congress.


No explicit rate cut was announced, and specifics were thin — consistent with prior mid-year Politburo meetings that have historically disappointed big-stimulus bets. Markets barely reacted, overshadowed by a same-day tech selloff (STAR 50 -5.4%) driven by valuation concerns rather than the policy statement.

Key signal: The more consequential policy signals — on growth-driver transition and actual fiscal acceleration — are more likely to show up in Q4 data than in this week's announcement.
