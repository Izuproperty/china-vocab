STORY 1 · AI & TECH / FOUNDATION MODELS
$7.4 Billion, Zero US Names: DeepSeek's Pre-IPO Round Closes Out the Foreign-Capital Question

DeepSeek (深度求索) is nearing a ~¥50B ($7.4B) round at a $74B pre-money valuation per WSJ/SCMP reporting Aug 28, closing by month-end ahead of a targeted 2027 STAR Market listing. Backers span existing holders Monolith, Shixiang Capital, and CATL alongside new entrants CPE, Legend Capital, Stony Creek Capital, and funds tied to GigaDevice and Hefei state investment vehicles — every disclosed name is mainland-based.


The round marks a shift from founder Liang Wenfeng's hedge-fund-financed model (via High-Flyer) toward outside capital, as DeepSeek adds ~1GW of compute and resets API pricing to a peak/off-peak structure priced above the old flat rate.

Key signal: The all-domestic cap table says less about DeepSeek's capital-raising ability than about how fully its balance sheet, compute, and now funding run through state-aligned channels.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH / SEMICONDUCTORS
600,000 Targeted, 300,000 Deliverable: Huawei's Ascend 910C Hits a Memory Wall

Huawei's Ascend 910C is sold out domestically (DIGITIMES, Aug 28) even as it trails Nvidia's H200 on compute and bandwidth — Chinese buyers are scaling cluster size rather than closing the per-chip gap. Huawei targets ~600,000 units in 2026 via SMIC's 7nm process, part of a 1.6M-die plan, roughly double 2025 volume.


CXMT, Huawei's main domestic HBM supplier, is projected to produce enough memory for only 250K–300K units next year at typical configurations — wafer capacity isn't the constraint; memory is.

Key signal: Unless CXMT's HBM output scales faster than guided, the 600,000-unit target is a supply-chain aspiration, not a locked-in number.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO / WEEK-AHEAD PREVIEW
49.2 in July, All Eyes Monday: China's August Factory PMI Tests Whether the Contraction Was a Blip

China's official manufacturing PMI is due Monday, Aug 31 — the week's first major release. July fell to 49.2 from 50.3, missing consensus (50.0) and marking the first sub-50 print since February; NBS cited softer domestic orders and typhoon disruption. Caixin's manufacturing PMI follows Sept 1, services Sept 3.


A second straight sub-50 official print would be harder to write off as seasonal, especially against July's industrial-profit deceleration and continued property weakness.

Key signal: PMI is a cleaner real-time read on momentum than last week's lagging profit data — watch for whether weakness broadens beyond electronics.
