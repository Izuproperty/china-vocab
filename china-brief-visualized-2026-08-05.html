STORY 1 · CHIPS & SEMICONDUCTORS
Cities Bid for the Next Fab: CXMT Weighs a Second Beijing Plant Weeks After Its Record IPO

Reuters reported CXMT is in early talks to build a second Beijing DRAM plant in the Yizhuang tech zone, seeking at least ¥60M in initial development-zone backing — a fraction of the $10B+ a leading-edge fab typically costs. The talks predate CXMT's $8.6B July 27 Shanghai IPO, the largest mainland semiconductor listing on record.


Beijing, Shanghai and Hefei are now competing to co-fund CXMT capacity — replaying the "Hefei model" of state-directed municipal capital nurturing national champions. CXMT's near-term lever may be domestic pricing power over capacity growth: it remains far behind Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron's combined ~90% global DRAM share.

Key signal: Watch which cities actually commit capital, not the fab announcement itself — that's the tell for how centrally Beijing now prioritizes DRAM self-sufficiency.

STORY 2 · AUTONOMOUS DRIVING & REGULATION
The Override Becomes Compulsory: China Sets Its First National Safety Standard for Self-Driving Cars

MIIT's new mandatory standard for L3/L4 autonomous driving systems takes effect July 1, 2027, requiring lifecycle safety assurance, black-box-style data recorders, and continuous driver-readiness monitoring for L3 systems. It replaces a city-by-city pilot patchwork with one national compliance bar.


The timing lands as Goldman Sachs raises its 2026 China robotaxi fleet estimate to ~14,000 vehicles and 2030 forecast past 700,000. A unified standard favors scaled operators (Baidu, SAIC-Momenta) who can absorb fixed compliance costs over smaller rivals.

Key signal: The 11-month runway to July 2027 is the window to watch for provincial enforcement clarity and re-validation of existing city pilot permits.

STORY 3 · CAPITAL MARKETS & RMB INTERNATIONALIZATION
Closing a Nine-Year Gap: Hong Kong Lists the World's First Offshore Chinese Government Bond Future

HKEX listed a cash-settled, 5-year offshore CGB futures contract Monday — face value ¥500,000, position cap 22,000 contracts. CSRC chairman Wu Qing called it a "natural development" reinforcing Hong Kong's "bridgehead" role for offshore RMB.


Foreign holdings of Chinese interbank bonds have quadrupled since 2017 to ¥3.2 trillion, with no prior onshore-linked hedging tool. An earlier 2017 CGB futures attempt was shelved over regulatory immaturity — this closes a nine-year gap, though early volumes will determine actual uptake.

Key signal: Track open interest and volume in the coming weeks — the cash-settlement design and position cap suggest a hedging tool, not a directional-trading vehicle.
