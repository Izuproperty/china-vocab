The Revised AI Basic Plan Heads for Cabinet

Why revise so fast
The stated trigger is frontier-model capability growth — specifically weaponisation risk from models that can find and exploit unknown vulnerabilities — plus AI-generated disinformation and Global South cooperation.
What to watch
Whether the final cabinet text stays promotion-first with duties of effort, or adds anything binding on frontier models. Expect signal, not enforcement — the arbitrage against the EU regime is deliberate.
The buildout leg
The same policy window funds Noetra (SoftBank, Sony, NEC, Honda with AIST) at ¥387.3B this fiscal year, first model due before March. Risk monitoring and model funding are one strategy, not a contradiction.

Why HBM matters
High-bandwidth memory is the stacked DRAM co-packaged with AI accelerators — currently the highest-margin, most supply-constrained memory product in the world, and a binding constraint on AI compute.
A different policy bet
Versus Rapidus at 2nm, subsidizing a proven US memory maker is lower technology risk with faster payback in equipment orders — Tokyo Electron, Shin-Etsu, and SUMCO are mechanical beneficiaries of the build.
The timing tension
The ceremony came two days after Kioxia-led memory volatility on supply-demand concerns. Committing capital through 2028 says Micron sees demand through the cycle — or supply arriving as it turns.

The durable signal
Equities whipsawed, but 2.81% on the 10-year is the mechanism to watch: a fiscal premium layered on a BOJ at just 1%. Rising long yields plus a weak yen constrains policy from both sides.
Cleanest expression
Megabanks rose about 4% on margin expectations and some regional banks hit new highs — the rotation out of semiconductor leaders into banks, exporters, and value laggards had real breadth.
The week's number
If long yields grind higher into the BOJ's July 31 meeting, the fiscal-premium story starts to dominate both the equity rotation and the 160-162 yen range. The counter-read requires autumn budget restraint.
