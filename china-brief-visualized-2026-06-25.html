China Mineral Export Controls on Japan — Timeline 2025–2026
Core Claim
China's export restriction on
稀土xītǔ · rare earths
and
钨wū · tungsten
to Japan is functioning as a precision geopolitical instrument: near-zero shipments of militarily critical materials to Japan since February, while total Chinese RE exports hit 4-month highs in May — demonstrating that supply constraints are not the reason.
Unverified
Whether Japan's supply diversification (Australia deal, G7 trilateral buyers club) can realistically replace Chinese supply within a strategically relevant timeframe is unclear. Non-Chinese rare earth processing capacity remains limited, and the
双用途shuāng yòngtú · dual-use
classification could be extended to additional materials without warning.
Investable Signal
Beijing has demonstrated willingness to use
出口管制chūkǒu guǎnzhì · export controls
with surgical precision. The template — dual-use classification, ministry enforcement, MFA reaffirmation under diplomatic pressure — is replicable to other materials (antimony, gallium, germanium) or other target countries. Non-Chinese rare earth mining and processing projects in Australia, Canada, and Scandinavia carry a structural demand tailwind from diversification spending that is now policy-driven rather than cyclical.
