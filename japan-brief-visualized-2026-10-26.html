STORY 1 · AI & TECH
GMO Internet Asks Tokyo to Use Its New Cyber Neutralization Powers After Hackers Raid FX Trading Servers

GMOインターネット (Jī Emu Ō Intānetto, GMO Internet) asked the government on October 24 to carry out 能動的サイバー防御 (nōdōteki saibā bōgyo, active cyber defense) against the group that broke into Onamae.com Desktop Cloud, a Windows virtual server service that retail traders rent to run automated FX programs. The servers stored customers' brokerage logins, and the attackers used them to place trades the customers never made. GMO found malicious programs on some servers on October 20, shut down every plan at 4 a.m. on October 21, and says up to 26,739 servers could be affected by the 不正アクセス (fusei akusesu, unauthorized access). One customer told TBS that an account went to zero within seconds on October 21, with losses above ¥4 million.


The attacker then demanded money and threatened to keep placing orders at more than 70 brokers and FX firms, publish customer data and deploy ransomware. GMO refused to pay and gave evidence to police. It will cover realized losses from the unauthorized trades after any broker refunds, pay every current user ¥5,000 and give six months of free use on a replacement service hosted on a separate cloud. The company said a private firm cannot stop a cross-border criminal group on its own. The neutralization part of Japan's 2025 cyber response law took effect on October 1, so police can now take over or disable attack servers, including ones abroad, after the 警察庁 (Keisatsuchō, National Police Agency) consults the Foreign Minister and an independent oversight commission approves. No government response had been published by the evening of October 24.


This is the first public request from a private company to use the new powers, and the answer sets the scope of the law. The government's December 2025 guidelines describe repeated campaigns against the state or critical infrastructure as the typical case, and a retail FX hosting service is neither. The police statute only requires an urgent risk of serious harm to life, body or property, which a live campaign of unauthorized trades meets on its face. A refusal would leave companies that hold financial credentials to absorb cross-border attacks alone, two weeks after a wave of breaches exposed more than 20 million customer records in Japan.

Investor takeaway: GMO Internet Group (TSE: 9449) has capped part of the cost with a flat ¥5,000 payment, but its total bill depends on how many of the 26,739 servers were used to trade, and it has not disclosed that number. Online brokers and FX firms face reimbursement claims and likely FSA pressure over customers storing logins on third-party servers. Watch for a National Police Agency decision, which will tell every listed company holding financial logins whether the state will act on its behalf.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
A TSE Data Fix Shows Kioxia's Margin Longs Barely Fell, a Week Before Results and Its First TOPIX Step

The 東京証券取引所 (Tōkyō Shōken Torihikijo, Tokyo Stock Exchange) corrected its weekly margin data for キオクシア (Kiokushia, Kioxia) on October 23. The first release showed 信用買い残 (shin'yō kaizan, margin-financed long positions) falling to 31.17 million shares, which traders took as a sign that leveraged holders had sold out during the chip selloff. The corrected figure is 46.94 million shares, close to the 48.8 million shares, worth about ¥920.9 billion, recorded on October 15. That balance is the largest of any stock on the exchange, and the ratio of margin longs to margin shorts rose to 38 times from about 18 times a month earlier.


Kioxia's market value peaked at about ¥44.3 trillion on June 12, then the largest in Japan, and the stock closed 63% below that high at ¥12,880 on October 15, adjusted for the 3-for-1 split on October 1. It fell about 10% on October 22 after the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 6.7% on doubts about AI spending. Most of the margin longs were opened near the top, and standard Japanese margin positions must be closed within six months, so repayment deadlines bunch up toward year-end. A further drop would trigger 追証 (oishō, margin calls) and force sales into a falling market. Matsui Securities strategist Tomoichiro Kubota said the problem is that margin buying has swelled to an extreme.


Two events land on October 30. Kioxia reports July-September results, and the first step of its TOPIX 浮動株比率 (fudōkabu hiritsu, free-float ratio) increase from 15% to 50% takes effect at the close, with ¥2-3 trillion of passive buying estimated across the October and November steps. TrendForce expects NAND contract prices to rise 15-20% in October-December. Index funds will therefore be buying in the same weeks that leveraged retail holders face pressure to sell, and the size of the October 30 guidance decides which side is larger.

Investor takeaway: The correction removes the hope that the margin overhang cleared in the October selloff. About ¥900 billion of leveraged longs sits on Kioxia (TSE: 285A) going into results and the first TOPIX step on October 30. Passive demand and a firm NAND price outlook can absorb part of the forced selling, but another chip-sector drop would hit Kioxia harder than peers because margin calls add to the selling. Watch the weekly margin balance and October-December guidance.

STORY 3 · MACRO
The 10-Year JGB Yield Falls Five Days Running to 2.795% and the Yen Touches 156.37 as Investors Leave AI Stocks

The 10-year 日本国債 (Nihon kokusai, Japanese government bond) yield fell 8.5 basis points to 2.795% on October 23, its fifth straight decline and its lowest close since early August. The 5-year fell 6.5 basis points to 2.225% and the 20-year 5.5 basis points to 3.625%, while the 2-year held at 1.895%. The 10-year is now about 29 basis points below its level a month ago. The yen rose as far as 156.37 per dollar, its strongest in five weeks, before trading near 156.9, and the euro fell to 175.08 yen, its lowest since October 2025.


The buying came from a global sell-off in AI and chip shares. Meta's plan to raise capital spending revived concern that US tech companies will borrow more to fund data centers, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 6.7% on October 22. The Nikkei closed the week about 4% lower at 62,693, with Hitachi, Murata, TDK and Disco among the hardest hit, and investors moved the money into 安全資産 (anzen shisan, safe-haven assets). Takayuki Miyajima, senior economist at Sony Financial Group, expects JGB yields to stay under downward pressure because investors buy JGBs when risk aversion rises. MUFG's Lee Hardman said risk-off trading kept the yen stronger into the weekend.


Domestic data point the other way. September core CPI rose 2.1%, the first reading above the BOJ's 2% target in nine months, and LSEG data put the odds of a December hike from the current 1.25% at 61%. Economy Minister Minoru Kiuchi said Japan is not yet in a position to declare デフレ脱却 (defure dakkyaku, an exit from deflation). The 2-year yield, which tracks the policy rate, did not move, so the rally came from longer maturities and from haven demand. The BOJ's Outlook Report on October 30 will show whether the board shares the market's caution or reasserts the hike path.

Investor takeaway: Haven demand has pulled the 10-year JGB yield down about 29 basis points in a month while inflation crossed 2%. Institutional investors long duration gain while the AI selloff lasts, and they give it back if the BOJ says on October 30 that underlying inflation has reached 2%. A yen near 156 lowers import costs and reduces the chance of another intervention. Watch the 2-year yield: it still prices a December hike, and a move lower would mean the market is giving up on it.
