STORY 1 · AI & TECH FinSMEs
JIJ raises ¥840M as Japan's corporate venture arms back optimization software, not another foundation model

JIJ Inc., a Tokyo-based quantum- and AI-enabled optimization platform, raised ¥840 million ($5.2M) led by Global Brain and a Fujitsu Ventures fund, with KDDI, Mitsubishi Electric, ANA, and Yamato Holdings CVCs participating. Founded 2018, JIJ has 60+ documented optimization use cases across manufacturing, transport, telecom, energy, and logistics.

Five of six backers are corporate venture arms of firms already inside JIJ's customer base — a different capital pattern than the sovereign foundation-model consortiums dominating recent issues. This is corporate Japan capitalizing the application layer, not the model layer.

Investor read-through: A clean, small-scale read on where AI capex actually lands inside corporate Japan — infrastructure-adjacent and efficiency-focused rather than frontier-model prestige spending.

STORY 2 · SEMI & INDUSTRIAL TrendForce
A week after the Kumamoto quake, TSMC's JASM Fab 1 is the last major site still down

A magnitude-7.1 quake hit Kumamoto on July 28. Tokyo Electron's Kyushu sites resumed Aug 3, Sony Semiconductor Solutions began a phased restart Aug 4, and Renesas's Kawajiri plant restarts Aug 5. TSMC's JASM Fab 1 remains under equipment inspection with no confirmed restart date.

Kumamoto is under 3% of TSMC's global capacity, so the read-through is regional fragility, not TSMC's P&L. TEL's near-monopoly in categories like EUV coaters means even undamaged facilities can bottleneck the whole cluster.

Investor read-through: If JASM Fab 1's inspection runs materially longer than peers', that's a live data point on how concentrated equipment dependencies amplify a single-site disruption across an entire regional cluster.

STORY 3 · MACRO & BOJ Bank of Japan
BOJ's full Outlook Report text sharpens the upside-inflation case the July decision only hinted at

The BOJ released the full-text Outlook Report on Aug 3, two days after holding its rate at 1.00% and lifting the FY2026 GDP forecast to 0.8% (from 0.5%). The text adds that core CPI should accelerate to "clearly above 2%" from H2 FY2026, with risks explicitly skewed to the upside.

The Board names three drivers: yen weakness, AI-linked chip/equipment demand feeding into consumer prices, and Middle East logistics costs — none reversible quickly. July CPI (Aug 21) and August CPI (Sep 18) are the next checkpoints for September/October policy.

Investor read-through: The upside-risk framing reads as the Board building a case for a faster hiking path than the "no clear timetable" language from the decision itself implied.
