STORY 1 · AI & TECH · AI GOVERNANCE
NDRC Says It Will Speed Up a National AI Law Covering Data, Compute and Algorithms, With Mandatory Incident Disclosure

NDRC official Wang Ruomeng said in Beijing on November 3 that China will accelerate a comprehensive AI law (人工智能法) covering data, computing power and algorithms, with life-cycle safety standards and duties to disclose safety risks, incidents and vulnerabilities. Sector sandboxes and transition periods would phase it in. DeepSeek, Moonshot AI and Alibaba fall within scope. No draft date was given.


China has regulated AI through targeted rules so far: 2023 generative AI measures, 2025 labeling rules and the October 16 draft export catalogue that adds 23 AI technologies. The same day, NFRA vice head Xiao Yuanqi warned in Hong Kong that capital flooding into AI is raising leverage and crowding out traditional industry.

Key signal: Statutory incident and vulnerability disclosure would add cost and legal exposure for fast-shipping model labs, with open-weight releases the hardest case; the draft text and the agency it names as enforcer will decide how much.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · HUMANOID ROBOTICS
GGII Cuts Its 2026 China Humanoid Shipment Forecast to Just Over 50,000 Units From 60,000-Plus, Half of MIIT's Output Figure

GGII told its annual humanoid conference on November 3 that Chinese humanoid shipments will exceed 50,000 units in 2026, down from an earlier forecast above 60,000. It sized the China market above ¥10 billion ($1.50 billion), put global shipments near 60,000 units and about $2 billion, and expects more than 15% of makers to exit within three years.


MIIT said on October 20 that output passed 40,000 in the first half and will top 100,000 for the year, double GGII's shipment figure. In Counterpoint's first-half data, AgiBot and Unitree took about 74% of global units, and shows and education accounted for over 60% of use.

Key signal: A late-year forecast cut and a wide output-versus-shipment gap point to slower orders and a stock build; valuations priced for steep 2027 growth need factory and logistics deployment to outgrow demo sales.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · CURRENCY POLICY
PBOC Raises the FX Deposit Reserve Ratio to 6% From 4% to Slow the Yuan After It Hit a Four-Year High

The PBOC will lift the FX deposit reserve ratio (外汇存款准备金率) to 6% from 4% on November 15, removing about $24 billion of dollar liquidity on nearly $1.2 trillion of FX deposits. It is the first hike since late 2021 and reverses the September 2023 cut.


The yuan closed at 6.6458 on November 2, its strongest in over four years. The November 3 fixing came in at 6.7150, 692 pips weaker than that close, and the onshore rate eased to about 6.67. MUFG's Marco Sun said the PBOC wants to stop one-way appreciation bets.

Key signal: The liquidity drain is small, about 2% of FX deposits, so the effect works through signalling: weak fixings plus a reserve hike tell the market the PBOC will resist a fast run toward 6.5.
