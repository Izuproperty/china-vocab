STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Takaichi Orders a Year-End AI Action Plan as Her Growth Council Puts ¥101.6 Trillion Behind AI and Chips

Takaichi told the Japan Growth Strategy Council on October 1 that a plan to spread high-performance AI through the economy, including shifts in industrial structure, will be compiled by year-end. The council set FY2027 to FY2031 as the concentrated investment period for its 17 strategic sectors.


AI and semiconductors account for ¥101.6 trillion of the ¥370 trillion-plus roadmap to FY2040. Council member Matsuo Yutaka proposed a fully domestic supply chain from chips to robots, packaged with education and governance for export.

Investor takeaway: The first year of concentrated spending is funded in the budget drafted in December, alongside the AI plan. Deployment-focused integrators and domestic compute providers benefit most, and the split between public money and assumed private spending in that budget will show how much of the ¥101.6 trillion is real.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Kioxia Joins Applied Materials' $5 Billion Lab to Push 3D NAND Past Its Layer Limit

Kioxia joined Applied Materials' EPIC Center on September 29, after Samsung, SK hynix and Micron. Work covers stacked-die memory architectures, multi-die interconnect and materials for volume production.


Leading 3D NAND has about 332 layers with roughly 40nm between word lines, where cell interference and deep-hole etching limit further stacking. Reaching 500+ layers needs new films, metal fills and etch chemistry from tool and materials suppliers.

Investor takeaway: Co-development inside Applied's lab gives Kioxia early access to next-generation process tools its rivals already had. Tokyo Electron competes with Applied in NAND etch and deposition, so the tie-up could shift tool orders at the margin as Kioxia equips its next Iwate fab.

STORY 3 · MACRO & BOJ
Big Manufacturers' Mood Rises for a Sixth Quarter While Service Firms Slip, Giving the BOJ Room to Wait

The September Tankan put large manufacturers at +24, up two points but below forecasts of +25 to +26. Large non-manufacturers fell two points to +35, their first drop in five quarters. Both groups expect worse conditions by December.


Large firms plan 11% higher capex and assume ¥154.23 per dollar, against a market rate near ¥157. One-year inflation expectations eased to 2.6% from 2.7%. The BOJ raised its rate to 1.25% on September 18.

Investor takeaway: Soft services sentiment and flat inflation expectations take pressure off an October hike. Exporters gain because a ¥157 yen is weaker than the ¥154 in their plans, which half-year results from late October will show; banks lose some rate support.
