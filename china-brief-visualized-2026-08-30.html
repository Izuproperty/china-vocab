STORY 1 · AI & TECH / SEMICONDUCTORS
¥6 Billion, 83.8% From One Client: Enflame's IPO Subscription Opens With a Concentration Problem

Enflame Technology (燧原科技), the Tencent-backed chipmaker among China's "four little GPU dragons," set September 2 as its STAR Market subscription date per TechNode/Reuters reporting the week of Aug 26 — 43.04M new shares, ¥6B ($892M) targeted, the last of the four dragons to clear listing approval.


Tencent, Enflame's largest shareholder at ~21%, supplied 83.8% of 2025 revenue. H1 2026 revenue is guided to more than triple y/y, but the company remains unprofitable with ¥4.29B in cumulative 3-year losses.

Key signal: The question isn't whether Enflame can grow, but whether a listing priced on triple-digit growth adequately weighs a customer base concentrated in one relationship.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH / FOUNDATION MODELS
$1.2 Billion, Up 220%: MiniMax's Alibaba Cloud Ceiling Shows Compute Spending Outrunning the Plan

MiniMax's Aug 26 HKEX filings show its 3-year Alibaba Cloud purchase ceiling raised 220% to $1.2B through 2028. 2026's cap nearly tripled to $300M after MiniMax burned two-thirds of its original $115M budget by June; 2027/2028 caps jump to $400M/$500M.


A 220% revision less than eight months into the agreement suggests the original plan underestimated compute needs — a pattern also raising margin questions after Kimi K3's July pricing reset hit MiniMax shares.

Key signal: Whether this reflects durable demand growth or one-time catch-up spending isn't disclosed — but it adds a new variable to an already-pressured margin picture.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO / PROPERTY POLICY
40 Years, Not Lower Rates: Beijing Pairs Longer Mortgages With a Developer Financing Reopening

PBOC said Friday Aug 28 it will extend maximum mortgage terms to 40 years from 30, paired with a CSRC statement supporting developer fundraising via equity and bond issuance. The sector's downturn is in its sixth year: investment -19.2% y/y, starts -24%, prices still under pressure (Bloomberg, Aug 28).


The mechanism lowers monthly payments without touching the LPR — consistent with PBOC's "fiscal over monetary" posture signaled Aug 2, where it declined further RRR/rate cuts.

Key signal: A longer mortgage term only moves transaction volume if buyers believe prices have stopped falling — a belief this package doesn't directly address.
