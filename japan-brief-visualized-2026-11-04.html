STORY 1 · AI & TECH
SBI and Korea Investment Plan a ¥1.15 Trillion Fund for 200MW of AI Data Centers in Japan, With Kyushu on the Shortlist

SBIホールディングス (SBI Hōrudingusu, SBI Holdings) and Korea Investment Holdings are preparing a fund of about ¥1.15 trillion (roughly ₩9.75 trillion) to build AI データセンター (dēta sentā, data centers) in Japan, the Korean outlets FN News and KED Global reported on November 1. The initial target is 200MW of capacity. The two groups would lead the fundraising and are courting Korean and Japanese corporate investors, none of whom has been named. Kyushu is among the candidate sites, and 九州電力 (Kyūshū Denryoku, Kyushu Electric Power) has reportedly been approached. The partners aim to settle a blueprint and an investment structure by the end of 2026. Neither company has confirmed the plan.


The project has political sponsors in both capitals. 北尾吉孝 (Kitao Yoshitaka, Yoshitaka Kitao), SBI's chairman, visited Korea in late September. Before him, 木原誠二 (Kihara Seiji, Seiji Kihara) of the LDP's Japan Growth Strategy headquarters met Korea Investment chairman Kim Nam-koo and Samsung's Lee Jae-yong on a trip that ran from late August into September. Korean press has labelled the plan a "Korea-Japan Stargate," and it follows the May summit in Andong where President Lee Jae Myung and Prime Minister Takaichi agreed to widen AI cooperation. The Korean side also wants supply contracts for chips, cooling, power equipment and construction, which would give Korean industrial firms a way into Japanese data center spending.


Electricity decides where these projects can go, and that explains the Kyushu shortlist. OCCTO, the national grid coordinator, projects 電力需要 (denryoku juyō, power demand) from data centers and chip plants rising from 3.6 billion kWh in FY2025 to 51.4 billion kWh in FY2034. Kyushu runs restarted nuclear plants, regularly curtails surplus solar output, and already hosts TSMC's Kumamoto fabs. The reported budget works out to about ¥5.75 billion per megawatt, several times the usual cost of the buildings and power hookups, so the fund probably intends to own servers or prepay long-term power as well.

Investor takeaway: The report puts a Korean financial sponsor into a Japanese data center market that SoftBank Group, the domestic telecom carriers and US cloud providers have led so far. Kyushu Electric has the most to gain among listed names, because a 200MW contract would be one of the larger single loads on its grid and would lock in demand for its nuclear output. Confirmation from SBI or Korea Investment, a named site and the end-2026 structure decision are the checkpoints. Until one of them arrives, treat this as a press report from the talks stage.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Kyocera's Half-Year Profit Rises 56% on AI Data Center Parts, but a ¥29.5 Billion GaN Write-Down Leaves Its Second Quarter Far Below Forecasts

京セラ (Kyōsera, Kyocera) reported on November 2 that operating profit for April-September rose 56.4% to ¥65.6 billion on sales of ¥1.078 trillion, up 8.8%. The July-September quarter alone produced only ¥16.5 billion against a consensus of about ¥40.5 billion, because the company booked about ¥37.1 billion of one-time costs. Most of that was a ¥29.5 billion 減損損失 (genson sonshitsu, impairment loss) on goodwill and intangible assets in its 窒化ガリウム (chikka gariumu, gallium nitride) device business, with ¥7.6 billion of provisions for energy-related products on top. The shares fell about 4% on the day.


The operating businesses improved. Core Components, which makes ceramic semiconductor packages for AI servers and fine ceramic parts for chip equipment, lifted business profit 68% to ¥50.5 billion, and its margin rose to 13.7% from 9.8%. Electronic Components earned ¥19.6 billion after breaking even a year earlier, helped by demand for small, high-capacity 積層セラミックコンデンサ (sekisō seramikku kondensa, MLCCs) and tantalum capacitors for data centers, although the first quarter also included gains on property sales. Citi said results beat consensus once the one-off charges are removed. Management called data center and network demand robust and expects it to stay firm from the third quarter.


Kyocera raised its full-year sales forecast to ¥2.12 trillion from ¥2.08 trillion and lifted profit targets for all three operating segments, but kept operating profit at ¥160 billion, 35% above last year, because the GaN losses offset the segment upgrades. A gain of about ¥15 billion from selling its chemicals business to Sumitomo Bakelite, which closed on October 30, supports a net profit forecast of ¥160 billion. Capex is planned at ¥225 billion, up 51%, and the interim dividend rises to ¥28 from ¥25.

Investor takeaway: Kyocera's AI exposure now shows up in segment profit. Packages and capacitors for data centers produced the growth, and the GaN write-down clears a loss-making unit out of future quarters. The unchanged ¥160 billion guide needs about ¥94 billion of operating profit in October-March, against ¥65.6 billion in the first half, so the second half carries the raised segment targets with the impairment already booked. A guidance raise at third-quarter results in February would confirm that the GaN charge was a one-time hit.

STORY 3 · MACRO
Japan Sells ¥250 Billion of Five-Year Climate Transition Bonds at 2.2%, With Bids at 3.8 Times the Offer

財務省 (Zaimushō, Ministry of Finance) sold ¥249.8 billion of five-year GX経済移行債 (GX keizai ikō sai, climate transition bonds) on November 2, out of about ¥250 billion offered. Competitive bids totaled ¥959.9 billion, an 応札倍率 (ōsatsu bairitsu, bid-to-cover ratio) of about 3.8 times. The highest accepted yield was 2.200%, equal to the 2.2% coupon, so the bonds priced at par. The notes mature in September 2031 and settle today, November 4. Bloomberg reported the same day that Tokyo is extending its transition bond program even though the economy still runs mostly on fossil fuels.


Transition bonds pay for the government's GX (green transformation) program, which plans about ¥20 trillion of these bonds over ten years to draw ¥150 trillion of public and private decarbonization investment. Proceeds go to areas such as next-generation nuclear, hydrogen, energy-efficient chips and changes to heavy-industry processes, and the ministry posted its list of possible uses on October 26. Future carbon levies are meant to repay them, so they sit outside ordinary deficit financing. The market still has to absorb them alongside regular JGBs while the BOJ cuts its bond buying.


Buyers took the paper readily at this yield. A 2.2% five-year coupon sits almost a full point above the BOJ's 1.25% policy rate, which pays holders for one or two more hikes. The 10-year 長期金利 (chōki kinri, long-term interest rate) ended at about 2.77% before the Culture Day holiday, roughly 33 basis points lower than a month earlier, and MUFG's November 2 weekly expects a 2.65% to 3.00% range. The FY2027 budget and the 国債発行計画 (kokusai hakkō keikaku, JGB issuance plan) arrive in December, and that plan will show how much the ministry shifts issuance toward shorter maturities such as these five-year bonds.

Investor takeaway: A 3.8 times cover at par shows domestic buyers will hold five-year government paper at 2.2%, which eases one concern about JGB supply as the BOJ steps back. The green label has saved Tokyo little at past transition bond auctions, so the bonds trade mainly as ordinary five-year JGBs. For banks and life insurers the December issuance plan matters more than any single auction, because cutting long-dated supply would take pressure off the 20- to 40-year sector, where yields have moved most this year.
