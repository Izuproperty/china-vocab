STORY 1 · AI & TECH · INFERENCE
Approaching.AI Raises More Than ¥1.3 Billion in a Series B Co-Led by Local State Funds and ICBC Investment, Taking 2026 Funding to ¥2.4 Billion for Inference "Token Factories" on Domestic Chips

Beijing-based Approaching.AI closed a Series B of more than ¥1.3 billion on October 26, co-led by six investors including ICBC Investment, Xiaoshan Capital and Hangzhou's Qianjiang Century City, with SenseTime and Wangsu following. It sells inference output priced in tokens and does not train its own models; its team started KTransformers and helped build Mooncake.


The company says core metrics grew more than tenfold in six months and daily token output more than 100 times, and that it is at breakeven. It plans more than ten token factories, including a 10,000-accelerator site in the Hangzhou district whose fund co-led the round, and runs on Moore Threads, MetaX and Iluvatar CoreX chips.

Key signal: Local state funds are paying to turn domestic accelerators into billable inference capacity in their own districts, but with no named customers, no audited figures and token prices falling, the round does not yet show that the model earns a margin.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · ROBOTICS
AgiBot Launches the G2 Max Heavy-Duty Humanoid With a 40kg Dual-Arm Rating and Puts It on JD Logistics' Inbound Docks at About 80 Cases an Hour

AgiBot released the G2 Max on October 26 for depalletizing, palletizing and line feeding: 20kg per arm, 40kg rated across both arms, 50kg peak and 0.5mm repeatability. It says the robot already works on several lines, including its own robot assembly.


JD Logistics uses it at inbound receiving in its Zhilang goods-to-person warehouse, where AgiBot reports about 80 cases an hour unstacked, 75 stacked and above 99% success. Unit numbers and price were not disclosed. Swancor closed limit-up and Unitree rose more than 18% intraday the same day.

Key signal: A fourfold payload increase brings AgiBot's humanoids into ordinary carton handling, but 80 cases an hour is slow next to fixed palletizing cells, so the sale rests on flexibility, and Thursday's Innovation Day needs to show prices and order volumes.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · FX
The Onshore Yuan Reaches 6.6682 per Dollar, Its Strongest Since June 2022, as the PBOC Sets the Fixing 471 Pips Weaker Than Expected to Slow the Climb

Onshore spot touched 6.6682 on Monday, heading for an eighth straight gain. The PBOC fixed at 6.7162, its strongest since February 2023 but 471 pips weaker than the Reuters estimate of 6.6691, the widest weak-side gap since September 23.


Barclays says the PBOC is resisting faster appreciation through weak fixings; Goldman Sachs keeps a 12-month target of 6.40. The Fed decides Wednesday, the ECB and BoJ later in the week, and the Fifth Plenum closes Thursday.

Key signal: The PBOC is allowing the yuan to rise and setting the pace with its fixing; the gap between the fixing and model estimates is the measure to track, and a narrowing gap would mean Beijing is accepting faster gains despite weak exporter earnings.
