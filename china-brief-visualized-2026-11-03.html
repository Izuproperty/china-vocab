STORY 1 · AI & TECH · CHIP DESIGN SOFTWARE
Empyrean Pays Up to ¥220 Million for Control of Qijie, a Loss-Making Maker of Digital Logic-Optimization Tools

Empyrean (华大九天) said on November 1 it will buy 50.3762% of Shenzhen-based Qijie Technology from founder Wei Xing for about ¥219 million ($32.9 million), taking its stake to 73.8651%. Qijie sells logic-optimization tools for digital chips to nearly 100 design houses and lost ¥5.91 million ($887,000) on ¥27.42 million ($4.12 million) of revenue in the first nine months.


Empyrean's base is analog and display design tools; digital implementation is where Synopsys and Cadence still dominate in China. The deal values Qijie at about ¥435 million ($65.3 million), a small sum against Empyrean's ¥47.21 billion ($7.09 billion) market value.

Key signal: Domestic EDA vendors are assembling digital flows by buying point-tool startups, which is faster than building them; the risk sits in integration, goodwill and staff retention, so watch for the signed purchase agreement and Qijie's optimizer inside Empyrean's 2027 releases.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · INFERENCE DEMAND
CCID Says Chinese AI Now Uses More Data for Inference Than for Training, With Daily Token Calls Above 175 Trillion

CCID, the MIIT-affiliated think tank, says daily token calls passed 175 trillion in June 2026, more than 1,000 times early 2024, and that inference data has overtaken training data. The National Data Administration's survey shows 2025 inference data at 101.34 EB of a 199.48 EB total.


Guancha puts daily calls at 100 billion in early 2024 and 140 trillion in March 2026, so June implies about 25% growth in one quarter. Prices fell from $30 to $0.07 per million input tokens, and Chinese models took about 65% of OpenRouter calls in September but only about 30% of spending.

Key signal: Inference favors cheap, efficient domestic accelerators and usage-based cloud revenue, but falling prices mean token growth overstates revenue growth, and CCID has not published its method; Alibaba's and Baidu's November results are the next check.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · MARKET SUPPORT
PBOC Cuts the Buyback Relending Rate to 1% and Makes the Tool Permanent as 31 Firms Bid Over ¥100 Billion for Swap Funds

The PBOC, NFRA and CSRC met brokers, funds, insurers and major banks on November 2. Buyback relending drops from 1.25% to 1% and becomes a permanent arrangement; signed loans total about ¥440 billion ($66.1 billion), with more than ¥280 billion ($42.0 billion) paid out.


The swap facility's third tender runs November 3, with 31 firms applying for more than ¥100 billion ($15.0 billion) against a current balance of ¥105 billion ($15.8 billion). No new quota was announced, and the reported ¥100 billion ($15.0 billion) lines for state capital operators did not appear in the readouts.

Key signal: Regulators cut the price and removed the expiry because companies lacked appetite, not quota; the tools do not change earnings or the AI hardware valuation reset, so watch today's tender result, buyback announcements citing relending, and the STAR 50's 1,160.84 October 30 close.
