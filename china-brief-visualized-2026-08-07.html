STORY 1 · AI & TECH · FOUNDATION MODELS
Not the Model, the Math: Goldman Lifts China's AI Revenue Forecast 30% on a MiniMax-DeepSeek Price War

Goldman Sachs raised its year-end ARR projection for mainland Chinese AI models 30%, to $13B, after MiniMax's Aug 1 open-weight H3 launch (priced 30-50% below incumbents) and DeepSeek's same-day V4 Flash API rollout. Goldman lifted Zhipu AI's ARR estimate to $2.5B and MiniMax's to $1B.


The forecast is built on intensifying price-performance competition, not pricing power — DeepSeek's V4 Flash ranks 8th globally in front-end coding on Arena AI, just behind Zhipu's GLM-5.2 at 7th. The open question for positioning: does revenue scale hold as token prices keep compressing, or is this extrapolating a moment where adoption is outrunning monetization?

Key signal: Three developers hit commercial milestones in a single 10-day window — a margin risk as much as a growth signal.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · CHIPS & SEMICONDUCTORS
Small Amounts, Large Signal: HP, Asus and Acer Quietly Adopt Chinese Memory Chips as the Shortage Bites

HP, Asus and Acer have begun using small volumes of CXMT DRAM chips in select notebooks sold outside the US, per Nikkei Asia, amid an AI-driven global memory shortage. CXMT prioritizes domestic customers (Huawei chief among them), capping export volume; IDC projects an 11%+ PC market contraction in 2026 with prices up 10-20%.


The caution matters as much as the volume: PC makers are sourcing quietly to avoid unsettling incumbent suppliers Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron. CXMT remains on the US Entity List — a supplier still formally sanctioned by Washington is now inside Western laptops, a dynamic likely to draw scrutiny if volumes scale.

Key signal: A shortage severe enough to override supply-chain politics isn't resolving on its own timeline.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · ECONOMIC DATA
Confidence Before Contraction: China's Services PMI Sinks to a 22-Month Low

The RatingDog China general services PMI fell to 50.4 in July from 54.1 in June, missing forecasts of 53.7 and marking the weakest reading since September 2024. Business activity and new orders moderated for a second straight month on softer domestic demand, while export business stayed resilient.


Business confidence fell to its lowest level since February 2020, even as the headline index stayed in expansion territory and hiring extended a third month. The gap between still-positive activity and deteriorating sentiment lands days after the PBOC's pledge to stay "appropriately loose" without a specific rate-cut commitment.

Key signal: Sentiment is deteriorating faster than activity — a leading indicator policymakers haven't yet answered with a specific tool.
