STORY 1 · AI & TECH · MARKETS
Zhipu Jumps as Much as 12% in Hong Kong After Anthropic's IPO Filing While Mainland AI Hardware Falls, and Its $3 Billion ARR Target Now Faces a Revenue Test

Zhipu rose as much as 12% on October 27, the morning after Anthropic filed for a Nasdaq listing, and MiniMax gained more than 5% at one point. Cambricon fell 5.7%, Unitree 4.8% and the CSI 300 1.3%. Southbound investors bought a net HK$2.17 billion of Zhipu over two sessions.


Zhipu reports $1.8 billion of ARR and targets $3 billion by year-end; Goldman Sachs upgraded it to Buy with a $3.2 billion forecast. First-half revenue was ¥953.9 million (about $143 million), with a ¥2.0 billion (about $300 million) net loss. Amazon Bedrock revenue sharing starts showing up from October.

Key signal: Zhipu trades as Hong Kong's closest listed proxy for a US frontier lab, but its ARR implies monthly revenue about six times the first-half average, so second-half results need to confirm the run rate before the valuation can rely on it.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · OPEN SOURCE
Tencent Open-Sources Hy-SimulMT, a 30B Chinese-English Simultaneous Interpretation Model It Says Runs About Three Seconds Behind the Speaker

Tencent Hunyuan released Hy-SimulMT on October 27: a 30B mixture-of-experts model with about 3B active parameters that translates Chinese and English speech as it arrives, with optional voice cloning through an external TTS service. Weights, an inference server and training scripts are public, and the Hy Translate app offers it free.


Tencent reports about 3 seconds of lag on RealSI and says its quality beat all open models tested and four of five unnamed commercial APIs. A Tencent-run study with six CATTI-certified interpreters found comparable quality. It trails Qwen3.8-LiveTranslate on CoVoST2.

Key signal: An open interpretation model that runs on one GPU pushes the price of live translation toward hardware cost, pressuring paid APIs and iFlytek's device line, if Tencent's vendor-run quality claims hold up in independent tests.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · INDUSTRY
September Industrial Profits Rise 14.4%, the Fastest in Three Months, With Electronics Supplying About Six-Tenths of This Year's Growth

Profits at above-scale industrial firms rose 14.4% in September after 4.2% in August; the nine-month figure was 15.5%. Electronics profits doubled and added 9.6 points of that growth, high-tech manufacturing rose 52% to 25.5% of all profits, and mining gained 35.1% through August on commodity prices.


Furniture profits fell by more than half, retail sales grew 1.1% in September and property investment fell 20.6% in nine months. Analysts tie the profit run to AI supply-chain demand and expect some pressure before a fourth-quarter stabilization.

Key signal: September's profit jump comes mostly from electronics and commodities, so industrial earnings now track the AI hardware cycle more than domestic demand, and a reversal in AI capex would hit them directly.
