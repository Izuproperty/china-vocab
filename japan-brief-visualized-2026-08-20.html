STORY ONE · AI & TECH
Japan's IP Strategy Headquarters Drafts a Comply-or-Explain Disclosure Code for Generative AI
On August 18 the Cabinet Office's Intellectual Property Strategy Headquarters unveiled a draft principles code requiring generative-AI developers to publicly disclose models, training data, and collection methods; confirm to rights holders whether specific webpages were used in training; and respond to user copyright-infringement queries. The code is non-binding — comply or publicly explain why not.
The Three Disclosure Principles
Investor Takeaway
Limited near-term compliance cost for Japan's AI developers, but the direction is toward incremental interpretive soft-law rather than an omnibus AI-copyright statute — watch whether pending litigation forces the Diet to harden any of these principles into binding rules.
SIGNAL
Builds on the AI-related technology law enacted May 2025 — Japan's second soft-law AI move in two weeks, after the MOJ's Aug 7 voice-rights guidance.
WATCH
Whether major Japan-facing developers (NEC, Fujitsu, NTT Data, SoftBank/Noetra) adopt the code or choose the "explain" path instead.
RISK
Sensitive training-data information is exempt from disclosure — leaving the code's practical bite largely untested until a real dispute tests it.
STORY TWO · SEMICONDUCTORS
China's Rare-Earth Curbs Cut Japan's Dysprosium Imports 82% — Squeezing Both Chip Tools and EV Motors
Japan imported just 13 tons of dysprosium in 1H26, down 82% year-on-year, with zero imports in four of six months; yttrium imports fell 74% to 204 tons. European dysprosium prices are up roughly sevenfold since China's April 2025 export controls, while China's rare-earth shipments to the Netherlands — home to ASML — surged 121.4% even as shipments to Japan fell 43.8%.
Japan Rare-Earth Imports, 1H26 vs. 1H24 (YoY)
Investor Takeaway
A slow-moving but structural risk, not an acute shock: it raises input costs for Japan's magnet (Shin-Etsu) and chip-equipment-coating supply chains right as AI-driven capex is supposed to be their tailwind — diversification is measured in years, not quarters.
SIGNAL
China's rare-earth shipments to the Netherlands (ASML) rose 121.4% YoY even as shipments to Japan fell 43.8% — selective routing, not a blanket cutback.
WATCH
Japan sourced yttrium from 12 non-China countries in 1H26 — the pace of diversification volumes is the metric to track.
RISK
Dysprosium is essential to EV/hybrid motor magnets and yttrium coats chip-equipment components — a dual hit to two AI-adjacent Shin-Etsu business lines at once.
STORY THREE · MACRO & FISCAL
Japan's Bond Rout Puts Takaichi's ¥370 Trillion Growth Pledge on a Collision Course With a 3% JGB Yield
The 10-year JGB yield pushed toward 2.95-3% this week — last seen in 1996 and the government's own budget assumption threshold — after an early-August auction drew the weakest demand in a year. A sustained move above 3% would push debt-servicing costs past the ¥31tn currently budgeted, squeezing room for Takaichi's own ¥370tn strategic-investment pledge.
10-Year JGB Yield: Path Toward the 3% Budget-Assumption Line
Investor Takeaway
A distinct risk channel from the BOJ's well-telegraphed September hike: even if the Bank moves exactly as expected, a bond market unwilling to fund an expansionary fiscal agenda without a higher term premium can independently tighten conditions and compress AI-linked equity multiples.
SIGNAL
Early-August 10-year JGB auction drew the weakest demand in a year — a direct funding-cost signal, not just a rate-hike-odds story.
WATCH
Whether Takaichi identifies an offsetting revenue source for the food consumption-tax cut before the next major JGB auction.
RISK
Debt already exceeds 200% of GDP; LDP conservatives are pressing for spending curbs even as the honebuto's ¥370tn pledge assumes fiscal room that a 3% yield would erode.
