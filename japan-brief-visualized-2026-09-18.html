STORY 1 · AI & TECH
SoftBank Adds a Fourth Funding Line for Its OpenAI Bet

Apollo Global Management is negotiating to raise the size of a net-asset-value loan backing SoftBank's Vision Fund 2 to $9 billion from $5.4 billion — collateralized by fund assets, not SoftBank's parent balance sheet. It would be at least the fourth distinct instrument SoftBank has stood up in 2026 to fund its $64.6 billion OpenAI commitment.

The debt stack is diversifying across vehicles rather than concentrating in one facility — which spreads refinancing risk but makes the aggregate picture harder to track from outside. Lenders discussing more exposure just days after a two-day AI-safety selloff in SoftBank shares suggests credit is still available at the pace the strategy requires.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Kioxia Weighs a $10 Billion US Listing

Kioxia is in talks with Bank of America, Goldman Sachs, and JPMorgan about listing American depositary shares on a US exchange, targeting at least $10 billion as early as spring 2027 — a second, complementary lever to the 3-for-1 stock split and ¥800 billion buyback already underway at home.

The listing doesn't add capacity; it adds dollar liquidity and a potential path into US semiconductor indices, distinct from the $31 billion Yokkaichi/Kitakami capacity plan already pledged with SanDisk through 2032.

STORY 3 · MACRO & FINANCE
BOJ Reaches Decision Day at Its Fastest Hiking Pace Since the 1990s

A Reuters poll fielded Sept 1-8 found 97% of 68 economists expecting a 25bp hike to 1.25% today — the highest rate since 1993. With the hike itself fully priced, the tradeable variable is Governor Ueda's 3:30pm JST press conference: whether his tone validates the faster pace markets have built in.

62% of respondents now see 1.75% by Q2 2027, three months earlier than August's poll implied. Chip-linked names have already shown sensitivity to the JGB yield moves preceding this meeting; a softer tone could reverse recent yen strength and reopen earnings-risk questions for exporters.
