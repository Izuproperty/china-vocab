STORY 1 · AI & TECH
$10.2 Billion, 8.4% Discount: Alibaba Sells Equity to Keep Funding the AI Build

Alibaba priced a HK$80B ($10.2B) placement on Aug 24 — 710M new shares at HK$112.70, an 8.4% discount — four days after a Q1 FY2026 print that showed capex up 75% y/y to ¥67.68B while net income fell 76% y/y. The deal was nearly 3x oversubscribed and closes Aug 26.


Bears call the raise a "white flag" on cash-flow strain from AI capex and e-commerce price wars; bulls point to a stated 3-year AI-capex breakeven target and a Qwen app past 300M MAU. The more useful signal is whether Alibaba needs to raise equity again before cloud/Qwen revenue growth outpaces capex growth.

Key signal: Watch for repeat capital raises, not this one, as the real read on whether AI capex is self-funding.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH
100,000 Units, No Rendered Pixels: A World-Model Startup Bets Robots Don't Need to Picture the Future to Predict It

QJ Robots (千寻智能), a Tsinghua brain-computing spinout, has drawn Temasek backing for a prediction-only "world model" that forecasts physical-state evolution rather than rendering photorealistic video. The company claims ~100,000 cumulative installations and RMB 100M+ ($15M) in deployment revenue, concentrated in hotel, cleaning, and indoor-service settings.


The architecture bet is distinct from the pixel-generation approach used elsewhere, and cheaper to run at the edge — but the 100,000-unit figure spans mostly lower-complexity form factors, not bipedal humanoids doing manipulation work, which is the harder problem the sector has yet to solve.

Key signal: Temasek's check validates interest in the world-model thesis, not proof this implementation solves the generalization gap.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
Four Days, Five Laws: China's Legislature Puts Bankruptcy and Banking Rules Up for Review

The NPC Standing Committee opened its 24th session (Aug 25-28) reviewing draft revisions to five laws, including the Enterprise Bankruptcy Law (second reading, first overhaul since 2006) and the Banking Supervision and Administration Law, which has been in consultation since December 2025 and includes a clause restricting banks from sharing records with foreign authorities without regulatory consent.


The bankruptcy law revision — adjusting creditor distribution priority and reorganization rules — is the more directly relevant item for institutional investors this week; the banking law's foreign-record-sharing clause remains of interest to foreign financial institutions but its finalization timeline within this session is unconfirmed.

Key signal: Track passage dates, not this week's deliberation — NPC bills routinely carry over multiple readings.
