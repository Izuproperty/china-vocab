STORY 1 · AI & TECH
JERA Opens Its Chiba Power Plant to a $15 Billion AI Data Center Built With Dell and RHAELM

JERA signed an MOU on October 1 with Dell and RHAELM for a 400 MW AI data center beside its Chiba gas plant, costing more than $15 billion across phases. First operations come in 2028 and full capacity in 2029.


Siting the halls inside the power station removes the wait for a grid connection, the slowest step for Tokyo-area builders. The partners want several gigawatts at other JERA plants in the 2030s.

Investor takeaway: Land with ready power has become the scarce input for AI data centers in Japan, and JERA owns a portfolio of it. TEPCO and Chubu Electric shareholders gain a long-term customer for their gas plants, and server, cooling and electrical suppliers gain a template that can repeat across JERA sites. The tenant and the LNG pass-through terms are the two disclosures to watch before treating the multi-gigawatt plan as real demand.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
Shinko Electric Buys JDI's Idle Mobara Plant to Make Substrates for AI Chips

Shinko Electric will buy JDI's Mobara plant, idle since November 2025, keep the eastern part for FC-BGA substrates and pass the western 233,000 m² to supplier FICT. Transfer is end-March 2027; production starts in FY2028.


JDI expects a ¥51.4 billion gain. AI chips need larger, thicker substrates, and the finished cleanroom building saves Shinko years of construction.

Investor takeaway: Substrate capacity limits how many AI accelerators can be packaged, and this deal adds a large new plant without a greenfield build. JDI gets a ¥51.4 billion cushion that reduces near-term funding risk. Output starts in fiscal 2028, after the current tightness, so Ibiden holders should watch whether chip designers sign long-term supply agreements with Shinko for the Mobara lines.

STORY 3 · MACRO & BOJ
Tokyo Core Inflation Jumps to 2.7% in September, Putting an October BOJ Hike Back on the Table

Tokyo's September core CPI rose 2.7%, from 1.8% and above a 2.4% forecast. Core-core hit 3.0% and services 2.3%, with water subsidies ending and wage costs passing through.


August unemployment rose to 2.5%, and USD/JPY stayed near 158. National CPI on October 23 comes a week before the October 29-30 BOJ meeting.

Investor takeaway: The Tokyo print raises the odds of an October hike that the Tankan had lowered, and the national CPI on October 23 is the deciding data point. Banks gain from a faster path, while high-multiple chip stocks and property names carry the most exposure if short-dated JGB yields reprice. Currency markets are still positioned for a hold, so a hike on October 30 would move the yen sharply.
