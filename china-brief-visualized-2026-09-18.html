STORY 1 · AI & TECH
$33 Billion Wiped in a Month: Z.AI and MiniMax Slide as Washington Weighs Cutting Off Chinese Labs' Model Access

Shares of Z.AI and MiniMax — two Hong Kong-listed foundation-model developers that led China's AI rally earlier this year — have each fallen more than 30% in September, erasing a combined $33 billion in market value. The trigger: a push by Anthropic and other US developers urging Washington to restrict Chinese labs' access to frontier models, after both companies were named this month in a joint NSA/CISA/FBI advisory and Anthropic's own threat-intelligence report alleging industrial-scale distillation.


Model access and training-data provenance are now a policy variable, not just a competitive one. If restrictions take effect, labs dependent on cheaper synthetic training data face a real cost-structure shock on top of existing cash-burn concerns; if they stall like earlier export-control pushes, the selloff may be a re-pricing of a narrower risk rather than a re-rating of the sector.

Key signal: Nothing has actually been restricted yet — the entire selloff is priced on the probability of a policy action that remains proposed, not enacted.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH
Four Platforms, Seven Days: ByteDance Brings AI Agents to Feishu as China's Enterprise-Agent Land Grab Accelerates

ByteDance added AI agents to Feishu, linking the feature to Doubao Work — the latest in a compressed run of launches that included Tencent opening WorkBuddy to 100+ partners (Sept 2), Alibaba consolidating three tools into QwenWork, and Baidu's Dazi posting 1,000%+ month-on-month user growth. IDC projects the market at RMB 332B by 2029.


The metric on display is monthly active users, not revenue. None of the four has disclosed agent-specific unit economics, so the launch wave reads as a distribution contest fought inside existing ecosystems rather than evidence of enterprise customers paying at scale.

Key signal: User-growth numbers are being showcased in place of monetization data — the business model for these platforms is still unproven, not just unannounced.

WATCH · MONETIZATION

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
Six Days Out: What's Actually on the Table for the September 24 Trump-Xi Summit

Xi Jinping is confirmed to arrive in Washington September 23 for a White House meeting September 24, skipping the UN General Assembly. Two mechanisms matter more than the atmospherics: China's rare-earth export-control suspension expires November 10, and the two governments are consulting on a tariff-reduction framework covering roughly $30B of goods on each side.


The May summit's record — ag purchases, a Boeing order, selective tariff cuts, but no movement on Taiwan, semiconductors, or export controls — suggests the base case is another partial outcome. The rare-earth deadline is the more consequential thread: a lapse would tighten a supply chain global manufacturers depend on; a renewal extends a status quo markets have already partly priced in.

Key signal: The Nov 10 rare-earth deadline is a harder, more dated trigger than the summit itself — watch that date regardless of what the Sept 24 meeting produces.
Timeline: May Summit → Sept 24 Summit → Nov 10 Deadline
SIGNAL · HARD DEADLINE
稀土暂停xītǔ zàntíng · rare-earth suspension
The Nov 10 expiry is a known, dated trigger — unlike most US-China friction points, this one forces a decision one way or the other by a fixed date.
WATCH · SCOPE
部分协议bùfen xiéyì · partial agreement
The May pattern — ag purchases, one Boeing order, narrow tariff cuts — is the base case again; a comprehensive deal on Taiwan, semiconductors, or export controls remains unlikely on the current record.
RISK · SUPPLY DISRUPTION
稀土断供xītǔ duàngōng · rare-earth supply disruption
A lapse or narrowing of the suspension would tighten a supply chain global manufacturers — not just Chinese exporters — depend on; the tail risk with the most direct industrial and auto read-through.
