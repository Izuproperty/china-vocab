STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Mitsubishi Electric Lets Its CNC Controllers Direct Humanoid Robots, and Shows the System at JIMTOF From Monday

三菱電機 (Mitsubishi Denki, Mitsubishi Electric) said on October 21 it has built a machining system in which a humanoid robot and a CNC machine tool work together under AI control. An operator gives an instruction in plain language, and an AI Machining Task Planner converts it into a work plan drawn from a pre-registered task list, then assigns each step to the machine or the robot. The CNC unit runs what the company calls フィジカルAI (fijikaru AI, physical AI), sold under its Maisart brand, and exchanges status with the robot in natural language while it cuts. The robot takes the jobs operators now do by hand around a machine: setup, cleaning, pulling finished parts, moving them, inspection and clearing faults.


The company says this is the first NC system designed around humanoid coordination, a claim based on its own research. It targets practical use in 2030 and has not named the humanoid it uses, a price or a launch customer. The system goes on show at the 日本国際工作機械見本市 (Nihon Kokusai Kōsaku Kikai Mihonichi, JIMTOF, the Japan International Machine Tool Fair), which opens at Tokyo Big Sight on October 26. Mitsubishi Electric competes with Fanuc in CNC controllers, and Fanuc reported on October 23 that orders in its CNC division ran at 2.3 times the year-earlier level as AI hardware makers bought 工作機械 (kōsaku kikai, machine tools).


The commercial case rests on integration cost. Each robot cell beside a machine tool is engineered to order today, and Mitsubishi Electric says the system cuts that robot-by-robot integration work. A controller that can schedule a general-purpose robot from a spoken instruction gives the controller maker control of the cell, and the robot becomes an interchangeable part. METI's July robotics strategy targets 10 million AI-equipped robots by 2040, and the ministry is collecting complaints about rules that block physical AI until October 30, so policy is pushing in the same direction.

Investor takeaway: Mitsubishi Electric (TSE: 6503) wants its CNC controller to be the place where a machine shop tells a robot what to do, which would defend its controller share as humanoids reach the factory floor. The 2030 target means no earnings effect this fiscal year or next. At JIMTOF, watch which humanoid it runs and how many of the listed tasks it finishes without a human stepping in.

STORY 2 · SEMICONDUCTORS
China's Yttrium Oxide Shipments to Japan Fall 92% and METI Says Supply Has Stalled

A 経済産業省 (Keizai Sangyō-shō, METI) official told AFP on October 21 that global yttrium exports have stalled, describing a supply-demand crunch and soaring prices. The comment followed a Financial Times report that Japan has told Washington it is months from running out. Chinese customs data show 酸化イットリウム (sanka ittoryumu, yttrium oxide) exports to Japan of 4,000 kg in September, against 51,000 kg in the same month a year earlier, a fall of 92%. China refines 92% of the world's rare earths, according to the IEA, and stopped key yttrium shipments to Japan in July under 輸出規制 (yushutsu kisei, export controls) that its foreign ministry says are aimed at Japan's remilitarisation.


Chipmaking is among the most exposed uses. Yttria coatings line the chamber parts of plasma エッチング装置 (etchingu sōchi, etch tools) and deposition tools because they resist erosion and shed fewer particles than alumina under the harsh chemistries used at advanced nodes. A tool maker that switches materials has to requalify the process with its customers, which can take several quarters. That puts the exposure on 東京エレクトロン (Tōkyō Erekutoron, Tokyo Electron), which is adding a ¥104 billion building in Miyagi to make plasma etch tools, and on the ceramics and parts suppliers behind it.


The price gap shows who pays. Inside China, high-purity yttrium oxide traded near $7.6-7.9 per kg in mid-August, while Rotterdam quotes ran $419-470 per kg from May to July and European offers reached about $1,175 in late July. A Chinese parts maker therefore buys the coating at a small fraction of what its Japanese rival pays. Tokyo has asked the US government and US companies to press Beijing, and its domestic response so far has been recycling subsidies, such as the ¥3 billion METI gave Kyocera this month to recover tungsten from used tools.

Investor takeaway: Yttria is a small cost per tool, so the near-term hit lands on margins and lead times at Japanese etch-tool makers and their parts suppliers. Wafer output is safe while inventories last. If shortages force material changes, requalification delays would reach 2027 tool shipments. Watch Tokyo Electron's October 30 results for comments on parts costs, and China's October customs data in late November for any easing.

STORY 3 · MACRO · WEEK AHEAD
Week Ahead: The BOJ Decides on Friday Whether to Say Inflation Has Reached 2%, Two Days After the Fed

The 日本銀行 (Nihon Ginkō, Bank of Japan) board meets on October 29-30 and announces on Friday. Consensus expects the policy rate to stay at 1.25%, where it has been since the September 18 hike. The quarterly 展望レポート (Tenbō Repōto, Outlook Report) carries more weight than the rate this time: Reuters sources expect higher inflation forecasts and possibly a statement that 基調的な物価上昇率 (kichōteki na bukka jōshōritsu, underlying inflation) has reached 2%. Governor Ueda has called underlying inflation quite close to 2%, and money markets price about an 80% chance of a hike in December.


Tokyo's 東京都区部 (Tōkyō-to kubu, 23-ward area) October CPI comes out on the morning of the 30th, hours before the decision, along with the September jobless rate. Tokyo headline inflation rose to 2.7% in September from 1.9%. The Federal Reserve announces on October 28 in Washington; 94 of 100 economists in a Reuters poll expect it to hold at 3.75-4.00% after its September hike, and CME futures put about 10% on another move. USD/JPY touched ¥156.00 on Friday, a one-month low, after the national CPI print, and trades below its 200-day average near ¥158.55. Tokyo Electron reports fiscal second-quarter results the same Friday.


The BOJ has led this slot in three of the last four issues. It returns because the decision falls this week and its wording will set the yen and short-dated JGB yields into December. A hold paired with a statement that underlying inflation has reached 2% would make a December hike close to the base case and support the yen. A hold without that wording would push hike expectations toward January and leave room for USD/JPY to climb back toward ¥158, the zone where intervention speculation has capped the pair.

Investor takeaway: The Outlook Report wording will drive December positioning, and the market already expects Friday's rate decision to be a hold. Institutional investors positioned for a December hike need the 2% statement and a higher fiscal 2027 core CPI forecast, and a Tokyo core print below 2% on Friday morning would weaken that case before the board speaks. Watch for dissents and for Ueda's language on December at his press conference.
