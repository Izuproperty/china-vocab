STORY 1 · CAPITAL MARKETS
$75 Billion and Rising: DeepSeek Taps CITIC Securities to Prepare a STAR Market IPO

DeepSeek appointed CITIC Securities to prepare a STAR Market IPO, aiming to start the process this year. A pre-IPO round is underway at a ¥500B (~$75B) valuation, up from ~$50B post-money in June, when Liang Wenfeng contributed ¥20B personally and Tencent/CATL became top external shareholders (¥10B/¥5B). Proceeds target compute, model development, and talent retention.


Pre-IPO valuation and eventual public pricing are different numbers — untested by public markets yet. Notably, this comes right after DeepSeek was named directly in the US distillation advisory, and the company is pressing ahead with a conventional capital raise regardless.

Key signal: Would become the most closely watched Chinese AI IPO since Unitree — same open question of whether a public valuation holds once quarterly disclosure replaces private-round narrative.

STORY 2 · US-CHINA AI POLICY
"Groundless": Beijing Rejects the US Distillation Advisory and Warns of Countermeasures

China's Commerce Ministry rejected the Sept 8 NSA/CISA/FBI advisory naming six Chinese AI labs, with spokesperson Mao Ning calling the claims "groundless" and citing "technological self-reliance." A separate spokesperson warned of "resolute countermeasures" if Washington acts against Chinese AI firms — while signaling openness to AI-specific talks with the US.


The open-to-talks-but-ready-to-retaliate framing is standard pre-negotiation posture, landing two weeks before the Sept 24 Trump-Xi summit where AI governance is expected on the agenda. Neither side has specified what a countermeasure would target.

Key signal: Watch for concrete US action against the six named labs (API/cloud restrictions) — that would test how fast Beijing's rhetoric converts into an actual response.

STORY 3 · POWER SEMICONDUCTORS
Five Years in the Lab, One Announcement in Shanghai: China Claims a World-First Power-Chip Design

Alkaidsemi (Shanghai) launched what it calls the world's first SiC superjunction MOSFET on Sept 7: 1,200V-class, 1,635V breakdown voltage, 1.27mΩ·cm² on-resistance at room temp (1.5mΩ·cm² at 175°C). Five-year joint effort with CAS academician Hao Yue's team at Xidian University; United Nova Technology (UNT) developed the fab process.


SiC superjunction is a genuinely hard manufacturing target — combining SiC's thermal/voltage advantages with a device architecture that's historically difficult to fabricate reliably at scale. If independently verified, a real technical marker, not just marketing.

Key signal: The gap between a research milestone and a market-moving one is yield and cost at commercial volumes — power-chip pricing is already rising industry-wide.
