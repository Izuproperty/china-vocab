STORY 1 · AI & TECH
SoftBank Drops as Much as 7% After a Report Puts OpenAI's Revenue Run Rate $20 Billion Below Expectations

ソフトバンクグループ (Sofutobanku Gurūpu, SoftBank Group) fell as much as 7.3% to ¥5,600 in Tokyo on Friday, October 9, and closed 3.9% lower at ¥5,803. The Financial Times had reported overnight that OpenAI told investors its 年換算売上高 (nenkansan uriagedaka, annualized revenue) was approaching $50 billion at the end of September. Press reports based on earlier investor documents had put the figure near $70 billion. Bloomberg reported during the Tokyo morning that OpenAI still expects the run rate to reach $70 billion or more by December on enterprise growth, and SoftBank recovered part of its loss after that. The Nasdaq Composite fell 1.3% and the Philadelphia semiconductor index 3.4% on the FT report. Tokyo was closed Monday for スポーツの日 (Supōtsu no Hi, Sports Day), so today is the first session since Friday.


SoftBank completed its final $10 billion payment to OpenAI on October 1, taking its cumulative investment to about $64.6 billion for roughly 13% of the company. It borrowed heavily to get there, through a ¥1 trillion retail bond in September and high-yield notes with coupons up to 9.75%. OpenAI is in talks to raise new money at about a $1.4 trillion pre-money valuation. The 含み益 (fukumi-eki, unrealized gain) on the OpenAI stake now drives SoftBank's net asset value and its loan-to-value ratio, the measure Son uses to show lenders the group can carry its debt. A lower revenue figure makes the $1.4 trillion price harder to defend, and a lower price would shrink that gain.

Investor takeaway: SoftBank trades as a leveraged bet on OpenAI's valuation, and one unconfirmed revenue figure moved it 7% intraday. The terms of OpenAI's current funding round and any December run-rate disclosure decide the next move. A lower round price would cut SoftBank's net asset value and raise its loan-to-value ratio at the same time.

STORY 2 · ROBOTICS & INDUSTRIAL
Yaskawa Books Record Orders as Servo Demand From Chip and Data Center Customers Jumps 76%, Then Cuts Its Profit Forecast

安川電機 (Yasukawa Denki, Yaskawa Electric) reported after Friday's close that its 受注高 (juchūdaka, order intake) in the June to August quarter rose 38% year on year to ¥185.4 billion, the highest in the company's history. Motion control orders, mostly ACサーボ (ē-shī sābo, AC servo motors) and controllers, rose 56%, and AC servo orders alone rose 76%. Robotics orders rose 33%. The Americas led the regions at +60%, followed by other Asia at +53%, Europe at +36% and China at +35%. Yaskawa said data center and semiconductor investment drove the increase, citing wafer-handling robots sold in China and U1000 matrix converters for server cooling at US data centers.


Profit fell anyway. First-half operating profit dropped 5.5% to ¥22.1 billion on revenue up 9.8% to ¥285.6 billion, and the operating margin slipped to 7.7% from 9.0%. Yaskawa blamed its switch to a new 基幹システム (kikan shisutemu, core ERP system), which disrupted production and shipments, along with restructuring costs in Europe. Robotics operating profit fell to ¥6.0 billion from ¥10.5 billion. The company raised its full-year revenue forecast to ¥600 billion from ¥580 billion, mostly because the yen averaged about ¥160.8 per dollar in the quarter, and cut its operating profit forecast to ¥57.5 billion from ¥60 billion. The ¥72 dividend is unchanged.

Investor takeaway: Record orders show AI chip and data center spending reaching Japan's motion-control suppliers. The profit cut traces to Yaskawa's own systems migration while demand keeps rising. If second-half shipments catch up with the new capacity, ¥57.5 billion is beatable; a second cut in January would mean the ERP disruption is worse than disclosed.

STORY 3 · MACRO
Bessent Skips the IMF Meetings in Bangkok, Leaving Katayama Without a Joint Yen Stage as the Dollar Sits Near ¥158.6

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent will not attend the IMF and World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok this week, Reuters reported on October 9, citing unspecified domestic engagements. Deputy Treasury Secretary Francis Brooke and Under Secretary Erin Browne will lead the US delegation. Finance Minister 片山さつき (Katayama Satsuki, Satsuki Katayama) and BOJ Governor 植田和男 (Ueda Kazuo, Kazuo Ueda) are attending. The yen fell for a fourth straight week through Friday and traded between ¥158.2 and ¥158.6 per dollar in thin Monday trade, with Tokyo closed for Sports Day.


Bangkok was the obvious place for Katayama and Bessent to repeat the message behind the July 31 joint 為替介入 (kawase kainyū, FX intervention), when Japan spent an estimated ¥8.45 trillion buying yen alongside the US Treasury with the dollar near ¥164. The yen rallied to about ¥155.20 and had given back half the gain by August 10. The 日米金利差 (Nichi-Bei kinrisa, US-Japan rate gap) explains the slide: the US 10-year yield was near 5.23% on October 8 against 3.09% for the 10-year JGB, and futures priced only about a one-in-four chance that the BOJ lifts its 1.25% policy rate at the October 29-30 meeting. 高市早苗 (Takaichi Sanae, Sanae Takaichi) said on October 9 that a more competitive economy would maintain trust in the yen, which gives traders no new reason to sell dollars.

Investor takeaway: The yen's floor at ¥160 rests on the threat of intervention while the 10-year rate gap stays above 200 basis points. Bessent's absence leaves Katayama to make that threat alone this week. Exporters such as Yaskawa gain from each yen of weakness until Tokyo acts, and importers and households absorb the cost.
