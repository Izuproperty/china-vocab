STORY 1 · AI & TECH
Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Max-0902: Top of CodeArena, No Version Bump

Alibaba refreshed Qwen3.8-Max on Sept 2 without changing the 2.4T-parameter architecture or 1M-token context — a post-trained snapshot (0902) rather than a new release, focused on coding and agentic office-task performance. CodeArena rose 22 points to 1,691 (#1 on the leaderboard); TerminalBench 3.0 nearly tripled from 11.3 to 29.0.


The snapshot-naming pattern — seen also in the Aug 26 Flash-Next update — makes cross-release comparison harder for outside observers, and every benchmark cited is self-reported. The open question is whether these gains show up in enterprise coding-agent adoption, not just leaderboard position, in a market where nearly every Chinese lab is currently publishing benchmark wins on its own tests.

Key signal: Watch for third-party benchmark verification and actual enterprise adoption data before treating self-reported CodeArena gains as a competitive signal.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · CHIPS & MATERIALS
US Locks Up Tungsten & Battery "Black Mass" for a Year

Washington barred exports of tungsten scrap and lithium-ion battery black mass on Aug 27 for one year, requiring US suppliers to offer full monthly output to domestic buyers. Tungsten is a key semiconductor and AI-chip input; the US has had no domestic mine since 2015. The move follows China's own Feb 2025 tungsten licensing regime.


China still controls ~80% of primary tungsten production, so this is a fight over recycled scrap at the margin, not the core resource base. No MOFCOM countermeasure has been announced yet — Beijing's 2026 playbook has favored case-by-case licensing friction over outright bans.

Key signal: Watch whether MOFCOM responds with reciprocal rare-earth/tungsten licensing action, or absorbs the feedstock loss and scales domestic recycling instead.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO
China's Bond Quota Is Running Behind — Catch-Up Season Approaches

Central and local governments have used only ~68% of the 2026 annual bond quota through August, versus 76% a year earlier; sovereign bond issuance sits at 65% of quota, the slowest since 2022. Bloomberg expects issuance to accelerate into year-end, which typically pressures the PBOC to add liquidity so banks can absorb the supply.


The lag itself has several possible readings — more disciplined fiscal deployment, a softer local project pipeline (govt-fund revenue fell 21.6% y/y in H1 per MOF), or front-loaded caution on debt-swap allocations. The PBOC's H2 posture has put fiscal in the lead, with monetary policy in a supporting role.

Key signal: The signal to watch is whether PBOC reverse-repo/MLF operations actually scale with the bond calendar in Q4, not whether issuance itself picks up.
