STORY 1 · AI & TECH / FOUNDATION MODELS
2,436 and Zero: Zhipu's GLM-5.3 Finds Vulnerabilities Nobody Else Caught — and Ships Without a Safety Card

Zhipu released GLM-5.3 Aug 14 via its coding plan, with full weights roughly two weeks out. The company reports 2,436 confirmed vulnerabilities found across 269 open-source projects (1,097 critical/high-severity) and an 84.5% CyberGym score — but only 54.4% on ExploitBench, the benchmark for actually exploiting what's found, trailing Mythos 5 (78%) and GPT-5.6 Sol (76.5%).


No model card, safety framework, or pre-deployment risk assessment accompanied the release. SaferAI's independent testing of the prior GLM-5.2 base found it refused none of the offensive cyber or biology tasks tested — a finding this release does nothing to address.

Key signal: A model marketed on offensive-security capability shipping without published guardrail documentation is the detail to track once open weights let third parties test the claims directly.

Up to 1,100%: DeepSeek's New "Peak Hours" Pricing Is a Rate Hike, Not a Discount

Effective 16:00 UTC Aug 16, DeepSeek's V4-Pro API moved to time-of-day pricing: $3.96/1M output tokens at peak vs. $1.98 off-peak; $1.32/1M input (cache-miss) at peak vs. $0.66 off-peak. DeepSeek frames it as allocating resources "more reasonably" — language that reads as a compute-scarcity admission.


Every tier, including the discounted off-peak rate, prices above the prior flat schedule — up to 1,100% higher in some categories. That's consistent with DeepSeek's broader 2026 pivot toward its own data-center buildout and a reopened funding round.

Key signal: If DeepSeek's cost advantage over US frontier labs was partly below-cost pricing now normalizing, the price gap — distinct from the capability gap — may be narrower going forward than headlines suggested.

STORY 3 · MACRO / PROPERTY
-0.18% and Widening: China's New-Home Prices Show the July Slowdown Reached Housing Too

NBS data released Aug 17 showed 70-city new-home prices fell 0.18% m/m in July, deeper than June's 0.15% decline — reversing a brief stabilizing trend in which 20 cities had posted m/m gains in June, the most since May 2025, and Shanghai new-home prices were still up 3.1% y/y.


The price data compounds already-known weakness in property investment (-12% y/y over 7 months). Foreign capital is nonetheless probing selectively — PAG injected fresh capital into several Wanda Plazas in June-July — a distressed-asset bet distinct from any read on residential demand.

Key signal: Supply-side pullback (investment down 12%) has not yet translated into demand-side price stabilization — the two data series are moving in the same direction, not offsetting.
