STORY ONE · AI & TECH
Japan Locks In a ¥370 Trillion, 17-Sector Investment Blueprint — With Anthropic, TSMC, Microsoft and Oracle Already Making Their Pitch
The Takaichi Cabinet finalized long-term economic and fiscal policy guidelines on August 11 targeting ¥370tn ($2.3tn) in combined public-private investment across 17 sectors through fiscal 2040, with semiconductors and AI as the centerpiece. Ahead of the announcement, executives from Anthropic, TSMC, Microsoft, and Oracle met directly with the government to pitch their own plans.

Investor Takeaway
Foreign frontier-AI labs and foundries lobbying Tokyo for co-investment, rather than the reverse, is the more durable signal than any single figure in the blueprint — watch the FY2027 budget requests due later this month for whether dated line items follow.
SIGNAL

STORY TWO · SEMICONDUCTORS
Reuters Tankan: Japan's Manufacturer Sentiment Hits a Five-Month High, and Semiconductor Demand Is Doing Almost All the Work
The August Reuters Tankan showed the manufacturers' sentiment index climbing to +18 from +13, the highest since March, driven almost entirely by semiconductor-linked sub-indices: chemicals to +33 and metal/machinery to +25. One precision-machinery respondent reported "twice the normal volume of orders."
Reuters Tankan Sub-Indices: July vs. August 2026
Investor Takeaway
This is independent, demand-side confirmation of the AI-capex order flow chip-equipment makers have already been citing in guidance — but manufacturers themselves see the index easing to +16 by November.
SIGNAL
Precision-machinery respondent: "twice the normal volume of orders, an unprecedented situation."
WATCH
Whether Tokyo Electron, Disco and Screen Holdings' next guidance echoes this order-book strength.
RISK
Manufacturers forecast the index easing to +16 by November — and the survey sits upstream of oil/currency volatility already hitting markets.
STORY THREE · MACRO
Tokyo's Rally Meets Its First Real Test as Hormuz Risk Sends Chip Stocks and Bond Yields in Opposite — and Painful — Directions
The Nikkei fell 1.6% to 68,098.54 on August 18 after a lapsed US-Iran ceasefire reopened Strait of Hormuz disruption risk, pushing crude and the 10-year JGB yield to a fresh three-decade high near 2.95%. Chip names led losses: Tokyo Electron -2.9%, Advantest -1.7%, Lasertec -1.6%.
Aug 18 Selloff: Chip-Linked Names vs. the 10-Year JGB Yield
Investor Takeaway
A single Hormuz shock now pulls both of 2026's dominant Japan narratives — BOJ normalization and the AI-chip rally — in the same painful direction at once: higher inflation expectations and a higher discount rate on growth stocks, simultaneously.
SIGNAL
Nomura's Wataru Akiyama: "Rising interest rates tend to highlight the relative overvaluation of share prices."
WATCH
July CPI print due August 21 — the next read on whether the inflation side of this equation is stabilizing or accelerating.
RISK
If Hormuz disruption persists, energy-driven inflation could pull the BOJ hawkish precisely as higher rates squeeze AI-rally valuations further.
