STORY 1 · AI & TECH / ROBOTICS POLICY
Washington Locks the Door: FCC Bans Foreign-Made Humanoid Robot Imports, Aimed Squarely at China

The FCC banned imports of foreign-made humanoid and quadruped robots on July 29, citing supply-chain and cybersecurity risk. Existing US-deployed units are grandfathered; the rule doesn't name China but targets a market it dominates with an estimated 85% share.


The timing lands mid-rollout for Unitree's first three-market commercial launch — Europe went live July 22, with North America originally planned for August 12. Analysts see limited near-term revenue impact given Unitree's existing Europe/Southeast Asia tilt, but flag the rule as a possible template for restricting market access ahead of commercial scale.

Key signal: Chinese hard-tech exporters increasingly need non-US revenue diversification built in from launch, not added as a hedge later.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH / EXPORT CONTROLS
Beijing Widens the Net: 14 EU Entities Added to China's Dual-Use Export Control List

MOFCOM added 14 EU entities to its export control list on July 24, barring Chinese exporters from supplying them dual-use items — including rare earths and permanent magnets — without special authorization. The move follows a June 22 action against two US rare-earth producers and a new July 1 whistleblower mechanism.


The IEA estimates $6.5 trillion in downstream production is at risk annually if the curbs are fully implemented, with the US and Europe accounting for nearly half the exposure. The pattern read is that MOFCOM is running its own entity-list playbook, modeled on Washington's chip-sector approach but extended to Europe.

Key signal: Rising compliance cost for any Western firm with China-linked supply chains, independent of where the underlying trade dispute originated.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO / INDUSTRIAL DATA
The AI Line Item: China's Industrial Profits Rise 18.7% in H1, but the Growth Is Concentrated in Computing Gear

NBS reported H1 2026 profits at above-scale industrial firms up 18.7% y/y to ¥3.95T, though June growth decelerated to 15.1% from May's 21.1%. Electronics profits jumped 96.9%, with computer manufacturing up 689.3% and peripherals up 305.8% — both disproportionate contributors to the headline. Auto manufacturing profits fell 19.5% as sales declined for a ninth straight month.


The pattern is consistent with the July 28 chip-sector selloff (Issue #71) from the other direction: a large share of industrial profit growth looks like AI-infrastructure capex rather than broad domestic demand recovery.

Key signal: Industrial-profit headlines this year are more exposed to AI capex cycles than aggregate GDP or PMI data suggests.
