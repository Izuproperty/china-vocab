STORY 1 · AI & DEFENSE Nikkei Asia
Tokyo weighs Palantir and Anduril combat AI for the JSDF, with Sakana AI as the gatekeeper
Japan's Ministry of Defense is reportedly weighing two US battlefield-AI platforms — Palantir's Maven Smart System and Anduril's Lattice AI — for JSDF sensor fusion, target identification, and drone coordination, aimed at faster decision cycles and closer interoperability with US forces. A third, distinctly Japanese element: a proposed Sakana AI-built command layer that would arbitrate between the two foreign systems and limit how much classified SDF data reaches outside contractors.

This mirrors Japan's civilian AI playbook — import frontier capability, wrap a thin domestic sovereignty layer around it — rather than building battlefield-grade systems from scratch. It's a proposal under consideration, not a signed contract, and the arbitration mechanics remain undefined in reporting so far.
Investor read-through: Watch for a procurement budget line and Diet oversight debate before pricing this into Palantir- or Sakana AI-adjacent exposure — Japanese defense-AI adoption has historically lagged announcement cycles.

STORY 2 · ROBOTICS & INDUSTRIAL Bloomberg, Japan Times
Fanuc's steepest one-day drop in 40 years came from a guidance beat that wasn't big enough
Fanuc shares fell as much as 19% intraday on August 3 — the biggest one-day decline since 1986 — after the company raised full-year operating-profit guidance by only ~3% (to ¥218bn, an ¥8bn miss versus the ¥226bn consensus), despite Q1 orders surging 37% year-on-year and net profit up 35%.

The market sold the gap between demand and guidance, not the demand itself. The signal is about material and component costs (semiconductors, electronics, shipping) eating into margins — even Japan's most dominant robotics name isn't confident it can pass those costs through consistently.
Investor read-through: This looks like a supply-chain-wide input-cost story rather than a Fanuc-specific one — check whether Yaskawa and Kawasaki show the same order-strength/guidance-caution gap in upcoming prints.

STORY 3 · MACRO & BOJ Japan Times, Bloomberg
Six straight months of real wage growth just handed the BOJ its clearest case yet for a September move
June labor-ministry data showed nominal wages up 3.4% YoY — a fifth straight month above 3%, the longest streak in 34 years — while real wages rose 1.7%, a sixth consecutive month of gains, the longest since 2021. Scheduled cash earnings (ex-bonus/overtime) rose 2.9% for full-time workers, a cleaner read on underlying momentum.

Governor Ueda has flagged upside inflation risk as grounds for a hike "as soon as September." Sustained real-wage growth is the demand-side evidence that lets the BOJ hike without obviously choking consumption.
Investor read-through: This reinforces rather than resets the prevailing September/October hike thesis — July CPI (Aug 21) and August CPI (Sep 18) will matter more for timing than another month of wage data.
