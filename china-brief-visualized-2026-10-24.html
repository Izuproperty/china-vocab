STORY 1 · AI & TECH · FUNDING
DeepSeek Nears a ¥100 Billion Round Led by Tencent and CATL After Raising Its Target Twice, as It Prepares a STAR Market Listing

Bloomberg and Reuters reported on October 22 that DeepSeek is finalizing about ¥100B, with CATL and Tencent among the biggest backers. The target opened at ¥50B and was raised to ¥80B. No final valuation is public; CNBC reported about ¥500B sought, and said individual private funds were turned away.


DeepSeek has hired CITIC Securities for a STAR listing, named an ex-Hillhouse CFO, and reports a $1B revenue run rate. The June round raised ¥50B at ¥350B to ¥400B post-money, so most of the extra demand went into size.

Key signal: An all-mainland round at a reported ¥500B target leaves foreign institutions to meet DeepSeek at the STAR listing, which must price above about ¥600B for this round to profit while the STAR 50 sits about 40% below its peak.

STORY 2 · AI & TECH · MODELS
Moonshot Releases Kimi K3.1 Pro, Scoring 65.7% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 Against Claude Opus 5.5's 66.4%, and Says It Stripped Hacking Data Before a November 4 Weight Release

Kimi K3.1 Pro went live on October 23 in Moonshot's apps, Kimi Code and the API, with a faster Highspeed variant. It builds on the roughly 3T-parameter K3, keeps a 1M-token context, and claims a hallucination rate of 8.3% against 23.4% for K3. Weights are due by November 4.


Moonshot says removing cyberattack data cut its exploit score to 81.0% from 90.5% for an unfiltered checkpoint. The release targets finance and coding work as Moonshot's ARR nears $1B ahead of a Hong Kong IPO of up to $5B.

Key signal: K3.1 Pro's self-reported coding score sits within a point of Claude Opus 5.5, but open weights cap Moonshot's API pricing ahead of its IPO, and an 81% exploit score will not end US scrutiny of Chinese open-weight releases.

STORY 3 · FINANCE & MACRO · POLICY
NDRC Replaces Its 2017 Outbound Investment Rules From December 1, Counting Technology and Data as Investment and Bringing Individuals Into Scope

NDRC Order No. 49, published October 22, keeps approval for sensitive countries and sectors and filing for the rest, with deals of $300M or more by local investors filed in Beijing. Portfolio channels such as QDII and Stock Connect are untouched.


The new rules cover individuals, count technology, IP and data as investment, cover round-trip deals, and extend security review to sales of overseas assets. They implement State Council regulations in force since July 1.

Key signal: From December 1, moving AI models, code or data into an offshore entity counts as outbound investment that needs a Chinese filing, which adds seller-side approval risk to any cross-border deal for a China-origin AI team.
