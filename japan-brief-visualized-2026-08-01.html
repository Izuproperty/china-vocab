STORY 1 · AI & TECH The Decoder / Winbuzzer
Sakana AI opens its Fugu orchestrator to NVIDIA's open Nemotron models

Sakana AI confirmed it will route select subtasks — coding, tool-calling, instruction-following — from its Fugu multi-agent orchestrator to NVIDIA's open-weight Nemotron models, accessed alongside Sakana's own and other frontier models through a single OpenAI-compatible endpoint.

The move is grounded in two ICLR 2026 papers on learned orchestration (TRINITY, the Conductor) and is pitched as proof that a coordinated pool of open models can match a single proprietary frontier model — an anti-lock-in argument aimed at enterprise buyers, while giving NVIDIA a reason to stay central to the open-model stack even as it keeps selling the silicon underneath.

Investor read-through: One of the few homegrown Japanese AI products being adopted as working infrastructure rather than announced as strategy — watch whether enterprise customers pay for the routing layer itself, or whether it stays a research showcase.

STORY 2 · SEMI & INDUSTRIAL Bloomberg / Digitimes
Advantest lifts FY2026 profit guidance 35% as AI chip-test demand outruns its own forecast

Advantest raised full-year operating-profit guidance to ¥846.0bn on ¥1.714tn revenue — about 70% YoY growth, up from a 26% prior forecast and above the roughly 42% analyst consensus — and lifted its view of the global tester market to $13.0–14.5bn, a record, citing AI-inference test demand that has outpaced April's assumptions.

Shares jumped as much as 10.9% intraday, reversing the cautious 1.22% dip that followed the July 29 earnings print itself, when investors had focused on margin commentary over the record top line.

Investor read-through: Testers get bought before chips ship, making this one of the more direct reads on whether AI-inference demand is accelerating or just being repriced after July's correction — first of a three-day Advantest → Tokyo Electron → Kioxia earnings sequence.

STORY 3 · MACRO & FX Bloomberg / CNBC
Tokyo suspected of ambush yen intervention hours ahead of the BOJ decision

The dollar posted its steepest single-session drop against the yen since 2022 during July 30 US trading, hitting all major yen pairs simultaneously. FX desks at BBVA, TJM and SBI FX Trade attributed the move to unconfirmed Ministry of Finance intervention — consistent with the ministry's pattern of unannounced "masked" (覆面介入) operations. MOF has not confirmed it, as is standard practice.

The timing, hours before the BOJ held its rate at 1.0% and lifted its GDP forecast, suggests Tokyo judged verbal warnings alone were no longer holding the ¥163-165 line Finance Minister Katayama had flagged on July 24.

Investor read-through: MOF spending reserves to defend a level while the BOJ's own rate path works against it reads as a stopgap, not a trend reversal — testable against the next MOF monthly disclosure and whether USD/JPY revisits 163 within weeks.
